The earliest known attempt to draw the saga lands. It was made by Sigurdur Stefansson, an Icelander, and it shows Gronlandia, Helleland, Markland, Skraelinge Land and Promontorium Winlandiae strung along a continent that wraps the north and west of the Atlantic, with Iotun-heimar and Riseland - the lands of the giants - to the north. What survives, in the Royal Library in Copenhagen, is a copy; the original is lost. The copy carries the date 1570, but Gathorne-Hardy sets that aside as the copyist's mistake for 1590, because a note on the map betrays knowledge of Frobisher's voyage of 1576. A closely related map drawn by Hans Poulson Resen in 1605 covers the same ground; Gathorne-Hardy argues that Resen and Stefansson worked not one from the other but both from a common and older source. It is a map of a text, not of a survey: nobody who drew or copied it had been west of Iceland. That is precisely its value, as a record of what educated Icelanders about 1590 still thought their ancestors had found.
