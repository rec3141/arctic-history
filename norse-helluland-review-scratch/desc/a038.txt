The object that makes the Ellesmere Norse finds intelligible, and it is not Norse. In House 6 on [[place:Skraeling Island]], the section of chain mail lay within a few centimetres of a decorated ivory needle case which Peter Schledermann calls clearly of western derivation. He matched it to a specimen figured by Ford from Nunagiak and, closer still, to one from Tigara Phase Burial 265 published by Larsen and Rainey: the side handles those carry are indicated on the Skraeling Island piece by three bilateral breaking surfaces. Further digging in the same house the next summer produced a second ivory needle case of related design. Schledermann and McCullough are careful about the form: both needle cases do carry the characteristic wings of the eastern style, and it is the incised parallel-line decoration and the western parallels that make the pair look Alaskan; the second one, with two anthropomorphic figures crouching face down at its upper end, has no close parallel in the reviewed Alaskan material either.

So the people whose house held European mail were themselves newcomers, and recent ones, carrying a sewing kit of a kind whose closest matches are on the Bering Sea coast. The pottery, the harpoon heads and the house plan say the same thing. Whatever happened at Smith Sound in the 13th century happened between Norse Greenlanders and Inuit who had only just arrived from Alaska, not between the Norse and a long-settled eastern Arctic population.
