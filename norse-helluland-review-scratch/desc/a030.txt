A small broken stone vessel about 48 mm high from the Nanook site (KdDq-9) at Cape Tanfield on southern Qikiqtaaluk, recovered in the 1960s and re-examined by scanning electron microscopy in 2014. Patricia Sutherland, Peter Thompson and Patricia Hunt report that its interior carries traces of copper-tin alloy - bronze - and glass spherules of the kind produced at high temperature, which they read as showing it was used as a crucible; they suggest it may be the earliest evidence of high-temperature non-ferrous metalworking in the New World north of Mesoamerica. The evidence for what is in the vessel is good. The problem is the date: the radiocarbon bracket runs from 754 BC to AD 1367, a span of more than two thousand years, at a site occupied and abandoned repeatedly. As of 2026 no peer-reviewed reanalysis or rebuttal of Sutherland, Thompson and Hunt has been published - the published criticism is journalism. That is not the same as acceptance; it is a claim nobody has yet tested in print.
