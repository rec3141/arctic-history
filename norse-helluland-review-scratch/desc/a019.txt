The object itself: a small stone bearing a medieval runic inscription, catalogued as GR 1 in Rundata, found before 1824 in one of three cairns set out in an equilateral triangle on the summit of Kingittorsuaq Island in the Upernavik Archipelago at about 73 degrees north. It names Erlingr Sighvatsson, Bjarni Thordarson and Eindridi Oddsson, and says that they erected these cairns and cleared - Nansen's translation dates the act to the Sunday before gagndagr, Rogation Day, 25 April, and gives no year. Six further runes have never been read to anyone's satisfaction; readings of them as a date, as the word for ice, and as an encoded cipher have all been proposed and none accepted. Dated on linguistic grounds to about 1300 or the fourteenth century, not to 1135 as Rasmus Rask's earliest reading proposed. Now in the National Museum of Denmark, Copenhagen.
