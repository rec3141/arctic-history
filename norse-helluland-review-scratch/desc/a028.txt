The finding that keeps the rest honest. Electron microprobe analysis of the iron Peter Schledermann recovered from the Skraeling Island houses showed two distinct populations: terrestrial iron with nickel at 0.06 to 0.07 per cent, and two pieces with nickel at 6.6 and 7.0 per cent - meteoric iron, which Schledermann attributes to the Cape York meteorites of northwest Greenland that Inughuit had been quarrying and cold-working for centuries. Norse iron and meteorite iron sat on the same house floors. The people at Skraeling Island already had a metal industry of their own; the Norse rivets were an addition to it, not an introduction to metal. Any account of this material that treats Norse iron as the arrival of technology has the relationship backwards. Schledermann's own reading of the undiagnostic terrestrial pieces is that they are Norse because they lie in the same houses as the mail, the rivets, the cloth and the barrel sections.
