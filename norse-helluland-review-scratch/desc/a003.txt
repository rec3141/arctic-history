The northernmost Norse inscription known, and the hardest datum in this topic: a small stone found before 1824 in one of three cairns on the summit of Kingittorsuaq Island in the Upernavik Archipelago, at about 73 degrees north. Nansen's 1911 translation renders it: 'Erling Sigvathsson, Bjarne Thordarson, and Endride Oddson on the Sunday before "gagndag" [i.e., April 25] erected these cairns and cleared ...' - gagndagr is Rogation Day. Six secret runes follow which nobody has securely read: Rasmus Rask in 1824 read them as the year 1135; Finnur Jonsson in 1914 called them ornamental; L. F. Laffler explained them as meaning ice, giving 'and cleared away ice'. Judging from the language the inscription is of the fourteenth century, and Magnus Olsen told Nansen he thought it might date from about 1300 or a little later. It proves that Erlingr Sighvatsson, Bjarni Thordarson and Eindridi Oddsson stood at 73 degrees north; it proves nothing about how much further any Norseman went.
