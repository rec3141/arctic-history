Inuit testimony, five centuries after the fact, collected in West Greenland by Hinrich Rink. Rink prefaces the four kavdlunait tales with an honest caution: they are 'known only to the west Greenlanders, especially the southernmost of them, and representing the only trace of intelligence left concerning the ancient Scandinavian settlers which the author has been able to discover by inquiries made in the country.' The story opens with a Norseman gathering shells on the beach at Kakortok who calls out to a passing kayaker from Arpatsivik: 'Let us see whether thou canst hit me with thy lance.' The kayaker refuses, and refuses again; then the chief of the place, Ungortok, says 'Since he seems so very anxious about it, take good aim at him', and the kayaker throws in earnest and kills him. Ungortok does not reproach him. Three winters later the same man kills a second Norseman for no reason at all, and the Norse come at night and cut down a whole household with axes. Two brothers escape onto the ice; the younger is caught, and Ungortok cuts off his left arm and holds it up: 'Kaisape, as long as thou livest thou won't surely forget thy poor brother.' The rest of the tale is Kaisape's long revenge, which ends with Ungortok shot beside a lake at Aluk on the southeast coast. The tale is told from both sides: the first killing is done by a Greenlander but at the victim's own repeated invitation, and the second is unprovoked. There is no archaeological sign at Hvalsey of the burning it describes.
