Peter Schledermann's 1980 Arctic paper carries a three-line addendum, added while the article was in press, listing what the 1980 season in the Bache Peninsula region had just produced: more boat rivets, a small woven woollen bag, an iron spike and a knife blade, many pieces of iron, and a copper end blade. It is worth an entry of its own for two reasons.

First, the woollen bag. A bag is a made thing, not a fragment: somebody cut and sewed European cloth into an object for carrying something. Second, the copper end blade - the working tip of a weapon or a knife, made of copper, in a region where the local metal supply was meteoritic iron from the Cape York fall and native copper from far to the southwest. Whoever made it had metal to spare for a cutting edge.

The addendum also marks the point where Schledermann's published preliminary reporting stops. The seasons after 1980, including the carpenter's plane and the rest of the Skraeling Island assemblage, were published by Karen McCullough in The Ruin Islanders (1989) and in Schledermann's later books.
