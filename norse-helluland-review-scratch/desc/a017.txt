The church at Hvalsey (Qaqortukulooq) in the Eastern Settlement, standing to gable height in dry-laid stone, with Hvalseyfjord beyond. This is what a Norse Greenland building looks like when it survives, and it is the standard against which claimed Norse structures in the Canadian Arctic - the turf-and-stone walls at Tanfield Valley above all - are measured. It is also where the last recorded event in Norse Greenland took place: the wedding of Thorstein Olafsson and Sigrid Bjornsdottir in September 1408, attested in letters from a priest at Gardar and from Icelanders. After that the Norse Greenlanders leave the written record entirely.
