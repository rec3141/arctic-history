Two pieces of plied yarn from House 73 at Nunguvik on Navy Board Inlet, dug up by Guy Mary-Rousseliere in 1984 and recognised as possibly Norse by Patricia Sutherland fifteen years later in the museum collections. They were among the strongest single pieces of evidence for a long Norse presence on Qikiqtaaluk. In 2018 Michele Hayeur Smith, Kevin Smith and Gorill Nilsen AMS-dated them after a protocol that strips marine mammal oil out of the sample: Beta-469301 gave 1290 plus or minus 30 BP, 664 to 770 calAD, and Beta-469307 gave 1390 plus or minus 30 BP, 602 to 674 calAD, both at two sigma. Seventh and eighth century - Middle to Late Dorset, two to four centuries before there were Norse in Greenland. The methodological point matters as much as the dates. Paired dates on these Nunguvik pieces with and without the oil-extraction step came out statistically indistinguishable, so their early dates are real and not an artefact of contamination. (The same was not true everywhere: an untreated Nanook sample dated some 3500 years older than its treated pair, which is why the protocol was needed at all.) The fibres are musk ox and arctic hare, not sheep. Dorset people spun their own thread.
