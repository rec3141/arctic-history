A 27 mm ivory figurine from house ruin 10 on [[place:Haa Island]] in the Flagler Bay area, which Peter Schledermann describes as thought to represent a Norseman and as possibly carved by an Inuk. It is the sort of object that makes headlines. Schledermann's own treatment of it is a model of care: heather from the sleeping platform dated GSC 2981, 270 plus or minus 50 BP, about AD 1680 - nearly three centuries after the Norse settlements in Greenland ended. He notes that the house form, with a nearly linear sleeping platform and a sub-rectangular outline, resembles the communal houses of Disko Bay and Labrador between about 1650 and 1700, and that the figurine could easily have been carried to Haa Island by Inuit from west Greenland. An object that appears to depict a European, in a house of a date when Europeans were whaling and trading in Davis Strait again. Coordinates from the Canadian Geographical Names Database.
