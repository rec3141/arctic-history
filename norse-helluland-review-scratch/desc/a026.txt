Not weapons but containers, which is more interesting. In house ruin 1 at the Eskimobyen site on [[place:Knud Peninsula]], Peter Schledermann's 1978 season found two sections of barrel bottom that had been joined together secondarily and decorated with incised concentric circles - a European cooper's product, taken apart and remade to somebody else's design. From house 3 came a piece of oak with inset wooden dowels, apparently part of a box, dated GX 6073, 560 plus or minus 120 BP, about AD 1390. Oak does not grow in the Arctic. Otto Sverdrup and his men were the first outsiders to observe, name and dig into winter house remains on this coast, in 1899-1900, long before any archaeologist worked here. Position approximate: the northeast coast of Knud Peninsula, facing the polynya that opens between Knud and Bache Peninsulas in late April; the peninsula itself is at 79.10 N, 77.13 W in the Canadian Geographical Names Database.
