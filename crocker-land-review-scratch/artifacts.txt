=== crocker-land-1913-002 | track | The search for Crocker Land: Etah to 82 30 N and back, February to May 1914 | date=1914-02-12 | lat=78.317 lon=-72.633
  creator=Donald B. MacMillan and Fitzhugh Green bibkey=macmillan1918four pages=pp. 46-101 people=["Donald Baxter MacMillan", "Fitzhugh Green", "Piugaattoq", "Ittukusuk"] tags=["crockerland,sledge,mirage,axelheiberg"]
  desc=The whole outward and homeward route of the Crocker Land journey, from MacMillan's own narrative. Nineteen men, fifteen sledges and 165 dogs left Etah in February 1914; influenza and mumps drove them back; the second start on 10 March crossed Smith Sound, climbed the Beitstad Glacier, ran down Bay Fiord and Eureka Sound and reached Cape Thomas Hubbard on 11 April. Four men went out onto the polar pack on 14 April and turned back on 25 April, 150 miles northwest of the cape. The two fixes of 22 and 24 April are Green's observations as printed; the positions on the pack between them are interpolated along the stated course, and the camps on the Axel Heiberg coast (Hvitberget, Blaamanden, the Took-too igloo) are placed from the day's runs and are approximate. Run 2 moved Cape Thomas Hubbard from 89 deg 15 min W to 94 deg 12 min W, its true position: at the old longitude the farthest point would have lain 200 miles from the cape instead of the 150 MacMillan gives, and the waypoints on the pack were re-interpolated to match. This supersedes the summary route at greenland-inughuit-042, which placed the Beitstad Glacier west of Bay Fiord instead of east of it.

  desc=The longest journey of the expedition and the one that mattered most to the map. Ekblaw's own report gives the plan as about 1,200 miles: from Etah to Cape Sabine, across Ellesmere from Beitstad Fiord to Bay Fiord, down Bay Fiord and Eureka Sound to Greely Fiord, up Greely Fiord and its tributaries, across Grant Land by Lake Hazen to Lady Franklin Bay, and home along the Greenland coast. Esayoo talked him out of the Beitstad Fiord crossing in favour of Flagler Fiord and the pass Ekblaw then named for Sverdrup. The work joined Lockwood and Brainard's survey from Lady Franklin Bay to Fosheim's and Raanes's from Jones Sound, and named Borup Fiord, Tanquary Fiord, Louise Homer Land, the Osborn Mountains, Cape James and Mount Bayley. Ekblaw gives dates and place names but few coordinates; the positions here are taken from the modern chart for the places he names, and dates between his fixed points are approximate.

=== crocker-land-1913-004 | track | MacMillan to King Christian Island and North Cornwall, March to May 1916 | date=1916-03-22 | lat=78.317 lon=-72.633
  creator=Donald B. MacMillan bibkey=macmillan1918four pages=pp. 223-260 people=["Donald Baxter MacMillan", "Ak-kom-mo-ding-wa", "Noo-ka-ping-wa", "Arklio", "E-took-a-shoo", "Panikpah"] tags=["queenelizabethislands,sledge,survey,kingchristian,cornwall"]
  desc=The expedition's second long western journey, and the one that reached farthest into the Queen Elizabeth Islands. Sherard Osborn had reported land thirty miles northwest of Bathurst Island in April 1853 and called it Finlay Land; Isachsen and Hassel of the Sverdrup expedition had seen King Christian Island from Ellef Ringnes in April 1901; geographers had made the two one land of some 3,000 square miles. MacMillan's orders were to encircle, map and explore it. He crossed Smith Sound, Ellesmere by Flagler Pass, Eureka Sound and the Sverdrup Islands to King Christian Island, then turned back east by Cornwall Island, where no outsider had landed since Belcher in 1852, naming McLeod Head and charting new islands off its south shore. Twelve hundred miles in eight weeks. The dates and the day's runs are MacMillan's; positions between the named places are approximate, and Bjornesundet, Ulvefjorden and Cape Southwest are placed from Sverdrup's names on the modern chart.
