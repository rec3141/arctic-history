The thing John Davis found that mattered most, and the one he valued least. Off Lumley's Inlet on 30 July 1587 the narrative reads: "great rootes, the water, as it were, whirling and overfalling, as if it were the fall of some great water through a bridge." The next day, by Warwick's Foreland: "This day and night we passed by a very great gulfe, the water whirling and roring, as it were the meetings of tides." That is the eastern mouth of Hudson Strait, the way Henry Hudson, Thomas Button, Jens Munk, Luke Foxe and Thomas James all went in after him. Davis never used the phrase Furious Overfall himself; it is the legend engraved on the Molyneux globe of 1592, and Markham notes the difference.
