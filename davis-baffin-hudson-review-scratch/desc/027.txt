John Davis took a Kalaaleq man hostage at Gilbert Sound for a stolen anchor, got the anchor back, and then sailed with him aboard anyway when the wind came fair. The printed margin of Davis's own narrative reads: "One of the people taken, which after died." Davis records that a companion followed the ship talking to the captive, and that the captive spoke four or five words and clapped his two hands over his face; the other did the same and left. "We judged the covering of his face with his hands, and bowing of his body downe, signified his death." Davis then gave him a new sute of frize after the English fashion and called him a pleasant companion, and adds that the ship's people used him well "with Yliaout, which was the common course of curtesie". He died at sea. The man's name is not recorded, here or anywhere.
