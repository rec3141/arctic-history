# The merchants' names: Hudson, Bylot and Baffin in Hudson Strait
[page · place-names · draft]

Sail west through [[place:Hudson Strait]] and you pass, in order, the boardroom of a London company that went bankrupt looking for China.

The Company of Merchants Discoverers of the North-West Passage was chartered in 1612 after [[person:Henry Hudson]]'s last voyage, and its subscribers put their names on both sides of the strait, some by Hudson in 1610 and the rest by [[person:Robert Bylot]] and [[person:William Baffin]] in 1615 and 1616. We know who most of them were because [[person:Luke Foxe]], sailing the same water in 1631, wrote the derivations down in *[[source:foxe1635northwest|North-West Fox]]*, and because [[source:white1910place|the Geographic Board of Canada]] copied Foxe out in 1910.

## Hudson's names, 1610

**[[place:Cape Wolstenholme]]**, on the Quebec side of the western entrance, is Sir [[person:John Wolstenholme]] (1562-1639), "one of the adventurers for the discovery of the North West passage". The **[[place:Digges Islands]]** off it are [[person:Dudley Digges|Sir Dudley Digges]] (1583-1639), "one of the principal promoters of the early voyages of exploration to the northwest". The Board's entry for Digges carries the sequel in the same sentence: "At this point Green and Jewitt, who abandoned Hudson, were murdered by the Eskimo" ([[artifact:place-names-072]]). The mutineers who set Hudson adrift in June 1611 were killed at Digges Island on their way home.

**[[place:Nottingham Island]]** is [[person:Charles Howard, Earl of Nottingham]], and Foxe gives Hudson's reason in full: "Named by Master Hudson, in due bequest to that most honourable Lord Charles Howard, Earle of Nottingham, then Lord High Admirall of England, a small remembrance for the charge, countenance and instruction given to the Search of the enterprise" ([[artifact:place-names-073]]). **[[place:Salisbury Island]]** is [[person:Robert Cecil, Earl of Salisbury]], "then Lord High Treasurer of England" ([[artifact:place-names-074]]) — the government's man among the backers. Hudson also worked through the royal family: Prince Henry's Cape, King James's Cape, Queen Anne's Cape, and an **Ile of God's Mercie** for a deliverance in shoal water.

## Baffin's names, 1615 and 1616

Bylot and Baffin in the *Discovery* finished the list. The three great sounds of northern Baffin Bay are the three chief adventurers: **Smith Sound** for Sir [[person:Thomas Smith]], governor of the East India Company; **[[place:Jones Sound]]** for the alderman Sir [[person:Francis Jones]]; and **[[place:Lancaster Sound]]** for Sir [[person:James Lancaster]], who had commanded the East India Company's first fleet — "We called it Sir James Lancaster's Sound". Those three lie on the northern side of this topic's boundary and belong with [[topic:davis-baffin-hudson]]. On the southern side Baffin left **Cockin Sound** on the Greenland coast for Alderman William Cockayne, and named the three groups of **Savage Islands** in Hudson Strait, in the Board's flat gloss, "from the number of savages (Eskimo) seen there" ([[artifact:place-names-094]]). The Inuit of [[place:Kimmirut]] call the largest of them Qikiqtarjuaq, and the Names Database has now made that the official name of the island the chart long called Big Island.

## The ones who never came

Two names in the strait belong to men who were not on any of these voyages. **[[place:Mansel Island]]** was given by [[person:Thomas Button]] in 1613 for Vice-Admiral Sir [[person:Robert Mansel]], Treasurer of the Navy — Button had married Mansel's niece. **[[place:Salliq]]**, Southampton Island, was named by Foxe for [[person:Henry Wriothesley, 3rd Earl of Southampton]], Shakespeare's patron, who had joined Essex's rising, been attainted, and been pardoned ([[artifact:place-names-075]]). **Marble Island** Foxe first called Brooke Cobham, "thinking then of the many furtherances this voyage received from that honourable knight, Sir John Brooke"; the descriptive name won. **[[place:Coats Island]]** came a century later, for [[person:William Coats]], "Captain W. Coats of the Hudson's Bay Company's service, who made many voyages to Hudson bay between 1727 and 1751".

Three names further west record failure honestly. **Roes Welcome**, after Sir [[person:Thomas Roe]], was so called because it was the way in. **[[place:Repulse Bay]]** — [[place:Naujaat]] — was named by Middleton in 1741 "because he found land where he expected to find the North West passage". And **Wager Inlet** he named for Sir Charles Wager, the First Lord who had sent him.

The single fact that the strait's names conceal is that none of these men ever saw it. Every one of them is a subscriber. The people who lived on both shores are on the chart once, as "savages".
