After 1900 the naming of this region stopped being done by captains and started being done by governments — first in Ottawa, then, once aircraft could photograph what ships could not reach, by a board sitting in an office. The names get duller and the reasons get better documented.

## Bernier: the Department of Marine and Fisheries afloat

[[person:Joseph-Elzear Bernier]] took the CGS *Arctic* north for the Canadian government four times between 1904 and 1911, landing on islands and reading out proclamations of possession. His names are a directory of the department that paid him: Dobell Point on [[place:Griffith Island]] after [[person:Richard Reid Dobell]], "in recognition of his help towards this expedition"; Gourdeau Point on [[place:Lowther Island]] after [[person:Francois Gourdeau]], the Deputy Minister of Marine and Fisheries; Edwards Point on [[place:Cobourg Island]] after an Ottawa politician; Beloeil Island after the birthplace of the Minister. He read Parry's inscription at [[place:Winter Harbour]] in 1908 and cut the Dominion's claim into the same rock. See [[topic:sverdrup-bernier-peary]].

## Stefansson: a cabinet on the western islands

The last large islands on earth to be found by outsiders were found on foot, on sea ice, between 1915 and 1917, by men of the Canadian Arctic Expedition, and they were named after the government that paid for the sledges. [[person:Storker Storkerson]] and [[person:Ole Andreasen]] found [[place:Brock Island]] and [[place:Borden Island]] in 1915, after the Deputy Minister of Mines and the Prime Minister. [[person:Vilhjalmur Stefansson]] sighted "Second Land" and "Third Land" in June 1916; in [[source:stefansson1921friendly]] he writes of "Second Land (which I have since named [[place:Meighen Island]])" and "Third Land (which I have since named [[place:Lougheed Island]])". See [[artifact:place-names-300]] and [[topic:karluk-cae]].

Stefansson could name descriptively when he wanted — Hight Bears Island, Storkerson Bay "in honor of the man who did more than any other member toward success", Shaler Mountains "in memory of an unexcelled teacher", Knight Harbor, Bernard Island and Bernard Harbor after two different captains named Bernard — but on the Queen Elizabeth Islands he named politicians.

## The police

The RCMP posts of the 1920s were planted to make Canadian sovereignty visible after the Sverdrup claim, and their names come from the same Ottawa world. [[place:Craig Harbour]] (1922) is [[person:John Davidson Craig]], who commanded that year's Eastern Arctic Patrol. [[place:Dundas Harbour]] (1924) took Parry's 1819 name for an Admiralty lord. Bache Peninsula, the northernmost post in the world when it opened in 1926, kept Kane's American name. And the patrols wrote their own men onto Ellesmere: Cape Stallworthy for [[person:Harry Stallworthy]] and, elsewhere in the archipelago, features for [[person:Alfred Herbert Joy]]. See [[artifact:place-names-304]], [[topic:posts-rcmp-missions]] and [[topic:rcmp-high-arctic-patrols]].

## The air survey, 1947-57, and what it changed

Between 1947 and 1957 RCAF and USAF photographic squadrons covered the whole archipelago, and the map changed shape. [[source:dunbar1956arctic]] records the two most striking results. In 1947 a USAF crew on a photographic mission found the strait between Cornwallis and [[place:Little Cornwallis Island|Little Cornwallis]] islands — a coast that ships had sailed past for a century turned out to be two islands. And Borden Island turned out to be two islands; the western one became [[place:Mackenzie King Island]], approved by the Board on 2 June 1950.

Whole clusters of names were then created by committee. Hazen Strait and Desbarats Strait were approved on 13 March 1947, together with the Findlay Group; Wilkins Strait followed on 2 June 1950, and [[place:Maclean Strait]] had been approved as long before as 5 April 1921. The Findlay Group got a run of governors general — [[place:Alexander Island]], [[place:Massey Island]] and [[place:Ile Vanier]], all approved on the same day, 1 February 1962, in the order they held office; [[place:Cameron Island]] beside them is, Dunbar and Greenaway say, "called after the late Chief Cartographer of Canada". See [[artifact:place-names-302]] and [[topic:qei-from-the-air-1947]].

## The weather stations: an inherited address book

The five Joint Arctic Weather Stations built between 1947 and 1950 all took names that were already on the chart, and every one came from a different century and a different nation:

- **Resolute** (Board decision 4 September 1952) — HMS *Resolute*, abandoned by Belcher in 1854.
- **Eureka** (9 June 1948) — [[person:Ivar Fosheim]]'s Norwegian shout of spring 1901.
- **Isachsen** (7 June 1949) — [[person:Gunerius Isachsen]], Sverdrup's cartographer.
- **Mould Bay** (3 April 1949) — [[person:George Frederick Mecham]]'s 1853 name for a friend of Nares nobody can identify.
- **Alert** (6 January 1949) — HMS *Alert*, Nares' ship of 1875-76.

See [[artifact:place-names-303]] and [[topic:science-stations]]. Later scientific names follow the same habit: PEARL at Eureka, the Ayles Ice Shelf named after a petty officer of 1875, [[place:Barbeau Peak]], [[place:Hazen Camp]].

## "Queen Elizabeth Islands", 1953

The whole group north of Parry Channel was the Parry Islands until 1953, when it was renamed for Elizabeth II on her coronation as Queen of Canada. It is the only time a Canadian archipelago has been renamed as a whole for a living person. "Parry Islands" survives in the Canadian Geographical Names Database as an official name for the southern part of the group, so the two names now sit one inside the other. See [[artifact:place-names-301]].

The same year, the government moved Inuit families from Inukjuak and Mittimatalik to [[place:Qausuittuq]] and Craig Harbour — the act that made this region inhabited, and that eventually produced the only substantial body of Inuit names in it. The naming of the islands for the Queen and the relocation of the people who would name them properly happened in the same twelve months, and for related reasons. See [[topic:relocations-qtc]].
