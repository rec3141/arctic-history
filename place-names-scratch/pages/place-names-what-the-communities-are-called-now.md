Charts printed twenty years apart do not agree about what the settlements of Baffin Island are called. What follows is a plain list of the current official names, the ones they replaced, and when the change was made.

Two things in that table are worth reading twice.

**The change is not a translation.** In every case the Inuktitut name is older than the English one and had never stopped being used. What changed in 1987 or 2020 was which one the Government of Canada prints. When Naujaat's name became official on 2 July 2015, Mayor [[person:Solomon Malliki]] said: "We're getting our traditional name back and keeping our language strong." It was the tenth Nunavut community to do so. When [[person:Lorne Kusugak]], the territory's Community and Government Services Minister, signed the last two, he told the Legislative Assembly on 27 February 2020: "Starting now, Hall Beach will be known as Sanirajak, and what was called Cape Dorset, starting this morning will be called Kinngait." Both communities had already voted in plebiscites; the Names Database dates both decisions the following day.

**The change stops at the hamlet boundary.** Iqaluit is Iqaluit; the bay it stands on is still Frobisher Bay. Kimmirut is Kimmirut; the bay is still Lake Harbour. Qikiqtarjuaq is Qikiqtarjuaq; the island is still Broughton Island. Kinngait is Kinngait, but the cape twenty-five kilometres south-east that [[person:Luke Foxe]] named for [[person:Edward Sackville, 4th Earl of Dorset|the Earl of Dorset]] in 1631 is still Cape Dorset. A community can change its own name; the features around it are a separate and much slower process.

That process has not stopped altogether. The Canadian Geographical Names Database records a steady trickle of Inuktitut names replacing English ones on features: **Kangiqtugaapik** has replaced Clyde Inlet, **Ikpiarjuk** has replaced Turton Bay near Iglulik, **Iqaluit Kangiqtunga** has replaced Shaftesbury Inlet, **Iqaluit Nuvua** has replaced Cape Griffith, **Qikiqtarjuaq** has replaced Big Island in Hudson Strait, **Kangiqtualuk Uqquqti** has replaced Sam Ford Fiord, **Tasiujarjuaq** has replaced both Carew Bay and Soper Lake, and **Kinngait Ilua** has replaced Cape Dorset Harbour.

## Why it matters on the bridge

Three practical consequences for anybody navigating or corresponding from a ship.

1. **Charts and pilot books lag.** A chart plate cut before 2020 will say Cape Dorset; the community, the airport code and the radio call will say Kinngait. The Names Database records the old form as "previously official", which is the safest way to check whether a name you are reading is current.
2. **The same word names many places.** Qikiqtarjuaq, "the big island", is a hamlet on the Davis Strait coast, an island in Hudson Strait, and at least three other islands in the Kitikmeot. Iqaluit, "many fish", is a city, a bay, a cape and several lakes. Inuktitut names are descriptions, and the same description fits many places; a name alone is not a position.
3. **Most Inuit names are not on the chart at all.** About ten thousand documented Inuit names in Nunavut are not official. The Government of Nunavut has had no toponymist since 2019, and nothing the [[person:Lynn Peplinski|Inuit Heritage Trust]] has submitted has been approved since 2012 ([[artifact:place-names-095]]). If you want the local name for the bay you are anchored in, the paper maps the Trust has published community by community are where it will be, not on the chart.
