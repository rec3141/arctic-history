The Pond Inlet Inuit thought that the Inukjuak Inuit were speaking English because their dialects were so different. The Inukjuak people never knew where the Pond Inlet people were going or what they were planning to do." The QTC adds that the Mittimatalik families had been told the Inukjuak people were poor and lived on relief, and expected to be paid for teaching them the country. No payment ever came.

=== relocations-qtc-052 quote 1975
TITLE: "We were close to the shore and we are too far from the shore right now"
CREATOR: Saroomie Manik
BIB: qtc2013resolutebay | PAGES: p. 43
CREDIT: Qikiqtani Truth Commission, Community Histories 1950-1975: Resolute Bay (Qikiqtani Inuit Association, 2013) | LIC: copyright Qikiqtani Inuit Association; short quotation for research
DESC: In the summer of 1975 every useful building at Qausuittuq except those at the airport was moved about three kilometres, from the west shore of Resolute Bay to the east. The reasons were drifting snow, the airport approach line, and a wish to put the Inuit settlement, South Camp and the airport together. Saroomie Manik told the Commission what it cost: "I was satisfied with the way it was set up [before 1975]. I'm sorry we moved [to the new settlement]. We were close to the shore and we are too far from the shore right now. When you come here in the late spring, when you don't have transportation, it gets more difficult to bring supplies up. This is the part I don't like about the relocation from the other settlement." The new site is further inland: marine mammals can no longer be watched for from the houses, and gear has to be hauled overland to the boats.
=== relocations-qtc-053 quote 1955
TITLE: "[Tuberculosis] was a scourge in our communities then"
CREATOR: John Amagoalik
BIB: qtc2013resolutebay | PAGES: p. 37

=== relocations-qtc-057 quote 2010-09-08
TITLE: "We were blind hunters": Markoosie Patsauq at the unveiling, 2010
CREATOR: Markoosie Patsauq
BIB: zarate2010high | PAGES: Nunatsiaq News, 9 September 2010
CREDIT: Gabriel Zarate, Nunatsiaq News, 9 September 2010 | LIC: copyright Nunatsiaq News; short quotation for research
DESC: Markoosie Patsauq was twelve when his family was landed at Qausuittuq in 1955. Fifty-five years later, at the unveiling of Simeonie Amagoalik's monument, he described the arrival: "My impression of Resolute was that we had arrived in a dead land. There was no sign of any life, not even the seagull." The family went hungry for weeks until the sea ice made and his father could take a seal. Then in October the sun went down and did not come back until February: "We were blind hunters. The first winter was very, very, very hard for hunters . . . we barely survived that first winter. I've never had such a hunger after that." He added: "What happened should never be forgotten. Future generations should know what happened in this place."
