 desc: The outward sledge journey of Cook's 1907-09 expedition, from Annoatok in north-west Greenland across Smith Sound to Ellesmere Island, over the pass now called Sverdrup Pass from the Atlantic side at Flagler Bay to the Pacific side at Bay Fiord, then north down Eureka Sound and Nansen Sound to the north cape of Axel Heiberg Island. Eleven sledges, 103 dogs, Rudolph Franke and nine Inughuit men left Annoatok on 19 February 1908; parties turned back in stages, and the last two, Koolootingwah and In-u-gi-to, were sent home from the sea ice beyond Cape Stallworthy. Dates are Cook's own; the crossing of the pass on 27 February and 1 March, and the 14 March position in Nansen Sound, are interpolated between dated camps. The line stops at Cape Stallworthy: what Cook did north of here is a claim, not a route, and is argued elsewhere.

 pos: 78.3 -98.7667 | pages:  | people: ["Frederick Cook", "Ahwelah", "Etukishook"] | tags: ["cook,devon-island,jones-sound,hell-gate,canvas-boat,starvation"]

 desc: The line begins where Cook, Ahwelah and Etukishook came back onto known ground in mid-June 1908, in Hassel Sound between the Ringnes islands, far west of where they had set out. It follows them south down Norwegian Bay past Cornwall Island, across Penny Strait to Grinnell Peninsula, over the highlands of Devon Island in July, down to Eidsbotn where the last dogs were released, and then in a twelve-foot canvas boat through Hell Gate and Cardigan Strait into Jones Sound. They were carried east past Belcher Point on 7 August, beset in a glacier bay, drifted back, gave up the run for Baffin Bay and the whalers, and turned west to Cape Sparbo, reaching it early in September with four cartridges left. Dates in late June and July, and the arrival at Cape Sparbo, are interpolated from Cook's own sequence; the rest are his.

 pos: 75.8167 -84.0 | pages:  | people: ["Frederick Cook", "Ahwelah", "Etukishook", "Panikpah", "Harry Whitney"] | tags: ["cook,man-hauling,ellesmere,smith-sound,famine"]
 file: 
 desc: The spring march home. With no dogs left, the three men hauled a sledge rebuilt from the wreck of the old one, north across Jones Sound to the Ellesmere coast, then up that coast past Cape Tennyson, Talbot Inlet and Cape Faraday to Cape Sabine, and over Smith Sound to Annoatok, arriving in April 1909. They averaged about seven miles a day at the start and were starving for most of it, eating scraps of skin and boiled walrus line. Only the first two dates are Cook's; the rest are placed by his own sequence of capes and are approximate. The end point is Anoritooq (Annoatok) at 78.57N 72.70W; the shared database also carries this site as Anoatok at 78.733N 72.50W, from Kane's narrative, and the two rows are the same place.

cook-1908-axel-heiberg-054 | image | 1908-03-30 | 'Bradley Land discovered'
 pos: 85.0 -102.0 | pages: plate facing p. 244 (scan n300) | people: ["Ahwelah", "Etukishook"] | tags: ["cook,polar-sea,plate"]
 file: cook-1908-axel-heiberg/files/cook-1908-axel-heiberg-plate-n300.jpg
 desc: Three photographs on one plate: distant low land or ice on the horizon, a flat sea of ice, and the party with the dogs and sledges. The upper images are Cook's evidence for Bradley Land, the coast he reported at about 85 degrees north on 30 March 1908. No such land exists; it is generally explained as an ice island or a mirage, and the plate is a central exhibit in the dispute over his claim.

cook-1908-axel-heiberg-066 | text | 1911 | Copy of the field notes: the appendix observation log, page 1
 pos: 88.0 -95.0 | pages: appendix, 'Observations, Etc. (Exact copy from original Field Papers)' (scan n662) | people: [] | tags: ["cook,appendix,table"]
 file: cook-1908-axel-heiberg/files/cook-1908-axel-heiberg-plate-n662.jpg
 desc: The opening page of the nine-page verbatim reproduction of Cook's field papers printed as an appendix: his explanation of what the original notes were and how they were copied, followed by the first day's entries. The nine pages give date, weather, temperature, barometer and travel conditions day by day for the polar journey, and they are the raw material of the whole Cook-Peary controversy. Pages 2 to 9 are on disk beside this one as scans n663 to n670.
