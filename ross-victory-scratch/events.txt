575 | 1830-04  | The quarrel over the death of a boy | Boothia Peninsula | 69.9 -92.4 | people: ["Pow-weet-yah", "James Clark Ross", "Thomas Abernethy"]
     The only serious breach between the expedition and the Netsilingmiut. An adopted son of Pow-weet-yah, a boy of seven or eight, was killed in the night by a falling stone; the death was put down to the ship's people, who were credited with supernatural powers. Pow-weet-yah raised his weapon at James Clark Ross and the surgeon within a few yards and was held back by his own two sons. The party was nearly surrounded and Ross had the gun at his shoulder when a woman walked out of a hut unarmed, called to him not to fire, and came straight up to them: from her they learned what had happened. 309 | 1831-06-01  | The North Magnetic Pole reached at Cape Adelaide | North Magnetic Pole 1831 | 70.0881 -96.7792 | people: ["James Clark Ross", "Thomas Abernethy"]
     James Clark Ross's party reached the calculated place at eight in the morning of 1 June 1831, in latitude 70 deg 5 min 17 sec N, longitude 96 deg 46 min 45 sec W. The dipping needle read 89 deg 59 min, within one minute of vertical, and not one of the delicately suspended horizontal needles would move at all. They camped half a mile west of some recently abandoned Inuit snow houses, hoisted the British flag, and built a limestone cairn over a canister holding a record. Ross regretted that nature had raised no monument on the spot: it was a low limestone shore, rising to ridges of fifty or sixty feet a mile inland.
