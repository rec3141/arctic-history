# The families moved to the High Arctic, 1953 and 1955

Transcribed from the Qikiqtani Truth Commission's community histories. The QTC
notes that name spellings follow 1990 Makivik Corporation documents unless
otherwise marked. RCAP's own family table is Appendix 4 of *The High Arctic
Relocation* (1994).

## Totals (RCAP 1994, pp. 7-8)

- **1953**: 10 families, 54 people. Seven families from Inukjuak, three from
  Mittimatalik (Pond Inlet).
  - to **Qausuittuq (Resolute Bay)**: 3 Inukjuak families + 1 Mittimatalik
    family = 22 people.
  - to **Craig Harbour** (later Ausuittuq / Grise Fiord): 4 Inukjuak families
    + 2 Mittimatalik families = 32 people.
  - A third settlement was planned at **Cape Herschel**, on the east coast of
    Ellesmere Island opposite Greenland. The d'Iberville could not get through
    the ice; the three families destined for it (two Inukjuak, one Mittimatalik)
    were landed at Craig Harbour in early September instead. RCAP: "It was
    lucky that the Inuit were not put ashore at Cape Herschel, since game did
    not appear there that year, and the Inuit could not have survived."
- **1955**: 6 further families, 4 from Inukjuak and 2 from Mittimatalik. One
  Inukjuak family to Craig Harbour, the rest to Qausuittuq. After 1955 there
  were 7 families at Craig Harbour and 9 at Qausuittuq — about 92 people.
  The figures exclude the Inuit special constables and their families at
  Craig Harbour.

## Notes on the geography

- Distance Inukjuak to Qausuittuq: over 2,250 km (QTC, Resolute Bay, p. 25 —
  "approximately the same difference between Toronto, Ontario, and Miami,
  Florida"). Inukjuak to Ausuittuq: more than 2,200 km. Mittimatalik to
  Qausuittuq: 566 km; Mittimatalik to Ausuittuq: 433 km.
- Craig Harbour is on the south coast of Ellesmere Island, about 35 miles
  (56 km) east of Grise Fiord. The Inuit camp was on the Lindstrom Peninsula,
  40 miles (about 70 km) from the police post at Craig Harbour and 8 km west
  of present-day Ausuittuq (RCAP p. 8; QTC, Grise Fiord, p. 29).
- The Qausuittuq camp was put about 8 km from the RCAF base and weather
  station; the only stipulation given to Constable Gibson was that the Inuit
  not settle too close to RCAF property (QTC, Resolute Bay, p. 25).

## Discrepancy to note

The QTC's Grise Fiord history says "On August 29, 1953, six Inuit families
arrived at Craig Harbour." RCAP says the group that was to stay at Craig
Harbour went ashore at the end of August, and that the Cape Herschel party
was landed there in early September, bringing the total to six. Both are
recorded in the database, each dated as its own source dates it.
