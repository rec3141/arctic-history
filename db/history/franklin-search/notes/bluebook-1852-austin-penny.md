## THE AUGUST 1851 MEETING: THE QUARREL

**Date:** 11 August 1851  
**Location:** Resolute and Lady Franklin (in Assistance Bay)  
**Principal parties:** Captain Austin (Resolute, commander) and Captain William Penny (Lady Franklin, whaling master)  
**Key issue:** Whether Penny asked Austin for the steamer Pioneer to push north into Wellington Channel

---

## KEY TESTIMONY (with quotations under 70 words)

### 1. PENNY'S ACCOUNT OF THE MEETING (Final Statement, p. 145)
**Q. 1492:** Penny stated:
> "I went on board the Resolute for the express purpose of telling everything to Captain Austin, and of asking for the steamer... I left the Resolute very much hurt and angry."  
(57 words; Page 145, line 18105-18107)

### 2. PENNY: WHY HE ASKED FOR STEAMER (Q. 62, p. 7)
> "I asked him for a steamer in order to prosecute that search."  
(11 words; Page 7, line 6814)

### 3. PENNY: THE OPEN WATER AT WELLINGTON CHANNEL HEAD (Q. 1, p. 6)
> "My decided opinion that Sir John Franklin had gone north-west from Wellington Channel was given to Captain Austin at the same time... that I asked for the steamer to be sent up the channel, on the 11th of August."  
(39 words; Page 145, statement #3; line 18104-18106)

### 4. AUSTIN'S ACCOUNT (Final Statement, p. 145)
> "Captain Penny never asked for a steamer, nor did he ever give me the slightest reason to hope that either trace or rescue was to be obtained by sending a steamer up the Wellington Channel."  
(36 words; Page 145, statement point 2; line 18160-18162)

### 5. AUSTIN: HIS CONDITIONAL OFFER (p. 145)
> "I could put on board several surveyors, who would in a very few days fix positions and get some angles... I merely alluded to the possible event of my sending the steamers up."  
(33 words; Page 145, statement; line 18157-18164)

### 6. STEWART'S JOURNAL ENTRY (Q. 209, p. 16)
> "Communication was held with Captain Austin... The result was that Captain Austin considered the search to the westward was done, and Captain Penny that further search in the Wellington Channel was unnecessary, to which every one agreed."  
(39 words; Page 16, July 28, 1851 entry; line 7714-7728)

### 7. COMMITTEE'S CONCLUSION (Paragraph 1, p. iv)
> "Captain Austin could only put one construction on Mr. Penny's two letters... Captain Austin would not have been justified in commencing a fresh search in a direction concerning which he naturally considered himself to have received such authentic information."  
(40 words; Page iv, line 114-128)

### 8. COMMITTEE ON THE AUGUST 11 MEETING (Paragraph 3, p. v)
> "Some desultory conversation... certainly appears to have taken place... it was for this reason that the former (very prudently and properly, in our opinion,) pressed Mr. Penny so strongly for his opinion in writing."  
(35 words; Page v, line 200-206)

### 9. COMMITTEE ON WRITTEN VS. ORAL (Paragraph 4, p. v)
> "We think... that Captain Austin could only form his judgment on Mr. Penny's written communications, which, although very laconic, were sufficiently explicit."  
(25 words; Page v, line 217-222)

### 10. BEECHEY ISLAND DISCOVERY (Austin's Report, Paragraph 8, p. xii)
> "Captain Penny had found on Beechy Island three graves and various other conclusive evidence... that the bay between Cape Riley and Beechy Island had been the winter quarters of the expedition under Sir John Franklin in the season 1845-6."  
(42 words; Page xii; line 652-661)

## DIRECT CONTRADICTIONS

**THE QUARREL IN ESSENCE:**

1. **What happened on August 11?**
   - Penny's claim: He asked Austin for the steamer Pioneer to investigate Wellington Channel
   - Austin's claim: Penny never asked for a steamer; only desultory conversation occurred
   - Committee verdict: Desultory conversation did occur, but Austin was justified in trusting Penny's **written** statements, which clearly expressed the search as complete

2. **What was Penny's state of mind?**
   - Penny says: He had discovered open water and wanted to pursue Franklin that way
   - Austin says: Penny only offered negative opinions on the feasibility
   - Committee verdict: Penny's letters of August 4 & 11 clearly state the search is impossible; his subsequent change of language (in England) was due to public disappointment

3. **The chart issue:**
   - Penny later claimed he distinguished Wellington Strait from "Queen Victoria's Channel"
   - Austin says: The chart Penny gave him showed no such distinction
   - Committee verdict: "No such distinction appears in the track chart delivered by him to Captain Austin at the time... Captain Austin could not fairly be expected to recognize any distinction"

---

## BEECHEY ISLAND DISCOVERIES

**Who found the graves?**
- Penny's expedition found them first (before Resolute arrived)
- Austin went with Penny to examine them

**Who examined the cairn?**
- Ommanney examined it on August 23, 1850 (Q. 431, p. 50)
- Found loose shot inside, but no records
- Carefully dug under and around it without finding documents

**Missing Record:**
- Despite careful searching, no evidence of Franklin's route was found at Beechey Island
- This factored into both commanders' decisions to return home

---

## DEATHS, SCURVY, AND SLEDGE CAMPAIGN

**Health status:**
- Crews were remarkably healthy: "free from scurvy" (line 19578)
- Only one death recorded during the expedition (native of Dundee in Resolute's hold, line 873)
- "All their parties returned (with only one exception) in perfect health" (p. v, line 1348)

**Spring 1851 Sledge Campaign:**
- Multiple parties sent out from April-May 1851
- Extensive distance covered (records in sick list table)
- No traces of Franklin found anywhere
- All parties returned safely despite severe conditions

---

## COMMITTEE'S FINAL VERDICT

**On the quarrel (pp. iv-v):**
- Both commanders were justified given the information available
- Austin right to rely on written communications
- Penny's written statements clearly opposed further search
- "Desultory conversation" occurred, but was not the basis for Austin's decision
- Penny changed his tune only after returning to England and finding "everybody disappointed"

**On overall conduct:**
- "Most gladly express our highest and most unqualified admiration of the zeal, energy, intrepidity, and perseverance with which every individual performed their respective duties" (p. v)
- Recommended future search should concentrate on Wellington Strait upper reaches (where Penny had discovered open water)
