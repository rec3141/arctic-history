## Winter 1848-49 at Fort Confidence, Great Bear Lake

### Arrival and Departure Dates
- **Arrival at Fort Confidence: September 15, 1848** (packet sent off on September 18, "the third morning after our arrival from the coast")
- **Richardson's departure: May 7, 1849** (left with Mr. Bell to travel south)
- **Rae's return from summer 1849 expedition: September 26, 1849** (per postscript from Fort Simpson)

### Winter Conditions
- Fort Confidence at latitude 66° 54' N, longitude 118° 49' W (per Simpson's 1837-8 observations)
- Log dwelling house: 40 feet long by 14 feet wide with central dining-hall 16 by 14 feet
- Heating required 3-4 armfuls of 3-foot billets of firewood to fill the large fireplaces
- Roof: sloped, covered with slabs and 6-8 inches of loam
- Windows: officers had glazed glass; others closed with deer-skin parchment
- Occupation: temperature hourly recording 16-17 times daily, magnetic observations, star observations, weather recording

### Important Dates During Winter
- October 31, 1848: Second packet of letters sent to Fort Simpson
- April 12, 1849: Letter-bag from England arrived with news up to June 22, 1848 (10 months old)
- April 20, 1849: Signs of snow softening observed
- May 1, 1849: Rae's official instructions issued from Fort Confidence

### Temperature and Seasonal Notes
- Snow melt began April 27 in sheltered places
- Ospreys, gerfalcons, eagles, gulls appeared May 17
- Geese arrived from May 19
- Summer birds: snow geese, swans, teal, widgeon, scaup-ducks, shovelers, jagers by June 1-8
- Last thermometer reading mentioned as "lowest observed" at some point in winter

### Personnel and Crew
**Left at Fort Confidence with Richardson and Rae:**
- James Hope (Cree Indian, crew)
- Thomas Hope (Cree Indian, crew) - had a son about 7 years old who could read
- Albert One-eye (Eskimo interpreter, crew)
- Louis Olivier (Canadian)
- Neil M'Leod (Orkney man, Rae's crew)
- Halcro Humphrey (Orkney man, Rae's crew)
- Baptiste Paul (Canadian, left in charge of fort)
- Louis Dubrill (Canadian, left in charge of fort)

**Support personnel:**
- Bruce (guide, acted as general architect)
- Mackay and Brodie (sappers and miners, carpenters)
- Two sawyers, four wood-cutters, four fishermen
- Mitchell, Dore, Cousins, Plante, Mastegon, Narcisse (various roles)

## Handover to John Rae

### Richardson's Instructions to Rae (May 1, 1849)
From formal memorandum issued at Fort Confidence:
- Rae to explore shores of Wollaston and Victoria Lands, between Mackenzie and Coppermine Rivers
- These lands separated by strait between 111th and 113th meridians
- Exploration important for search as Franklin directed to steer SW and might be caught in complex channels
- Rae to attempt coasting voyage if ice conditions permit

### Rae's Summer 1849 Expedition

**Crew composition:**
- Neil M'Leod (Orkney man)
- James Hope (Cree Indian)
- Thomas Hope (Cree Indian)
- Halcro Humphrey (Orkney man)
- Albert One-eye (Eskimo)
- Louis Olivier (Canadian)

**Route and Results:**
- Crossed overland to the Coppermine River via Kendall River (April start)
- Depot at Flett's station with musk-ox and reindeer meat
- Descended Coppermine River to coast (August 19-26 narrative)
- **August 19**: Break through ice pack, 7 miles, stopped by thick ice
- **August 20**: Returned to shore near Point Lockyer
- **August 22**: From hilltop near shore, observed "nothing but white ice forced up into heaps" towards Wollaston Land
- **August 23**: Return orders given; commenced carrying baggage to Point Lockyer  
- **August 23-24**: Passed Cape Hearne; "worked seventeen hours" to reach mouth of Coppermine
- Cape Hearne: found lane of open water between beach and pack ice
- **August 26**: From Bloody Fall, began inland journey toward Great Bear Lake
- Covered 3 days easy march to Coppermine (lat. 67° 12' N) with 90 lb loads
- Met "upwards of thirty of the natives" who joined them at different parts of route
- **Return to Fort Confidence**: Had supplies ready packed
- Supplied Indians with ammunition for return to trading posts

## Richardson's Journey South 1849

### Initial Departure from Fort Confidence (May 7, 1849)
- Accompanied by Mr. Bell, Bruce, Mitchell, Brodie, M'Leod, Mastegon
- Crossed Great Bear Lake in 5.5 days (May 7-12)
- Northern shores: deep snow (5 feet)
- Southern shores: exposed sandy spots, pools forming
- **May 10**: Mastegon shot first goose of season (straggler at pool)

### Fort Franklin Stopover (May 12-14)
- Arrived May 12
- Men sent ahead on May 15 with baggage: Bruce, Dore, Cousins, Thomas Hope, Mastegon, Plante, M'Leod
- Barge from Fort Norman to be retrieved
- Assembled for Sunday prayers May 14

### Bear Lake River and Descent (May onwards)
- Rapid area: walls of ice remain late into season
- Bridge of ice giving way, river fell some feet
- Passed mouth of Bear Lake River
- Arrived Fort Norman, waited for Mr. M'Pherson (arrived June 14)

### Fort Simpson Onward
- **June 25**: Left Fort Simpson (having received Sappers, Miners, men from Great Slave Lake)
- Detained by drift ice at west end of Great Slave Lake until July 6
- **July 11**: Fort Resolution
- **July 19**: Fort Chepewyan  
- **July 26**: Methy Portage (crossed July 27 on horses hired from Indians)
- Met L'Esperance with English letters from Canada

### Return to Canada
- **August 13**: Arrived Norway House (discharged men engaged in 1847)
- Men sent down to York Factory for ship to England
- "Not a single act of disobedience" during service

### Final Journey Home
- **August 14**: Left Norway House in brigade of three canoes with returning voyagers
- **September 14**: Fort William
- **September 25**: Saut Ste. Marie
- Travel via Lake Huron, stage coach to Orillia, Lake Simcoe, Toronto
- **Montreal**: Embark in steam-packet
- **La Chine**: Revised accounts with Sir George Simpson
- **Boston**: Embarked in British mail steam-packet

### Arrival Home
- **November 6, 1849**: Landed at Liverpool after 19 months' absence (12 months incessant travel)
- Immediately presented himself at Admiralty
- Received letter of approbation for his conduct

## Richardson's Conclusions on Franklin (May-November 1849)

### Key Reasoning
Based on traces found on Cape Riley (August 1850), Richardson concludes:

**Position determined:** Franklin's ships found off Cape Riley "about a month after they were last seen" (estimated **August 29, 1845** or approximately that date).

**Evidence analyzed:**
- Salt-beef bones from Navy stores, exposed 4-5 summers
- Rope made at Chatham of Hungarian hemp, post-1841
- Canvas fragment with Queen's broad arrow (boat's swab)
- Ash wood chip (drift timber, axe mark recent)
- Five tent rings with stones and slabs in centers (fire-places)
- No cairn or memoranda found

**Interpretation:**
- Ships temporarily arrested by ice barrier
- Good anchorage between Beechey Island and Cape Riley
- Richardson infers they made term observations (term-day August 29, 1845)
- Four tents would be needed for instruments, fifth for observers
- "With the prospect of soon passing the Straits, he evidently had not thought it necessary to erect a cairn"

**Inferred Route:**
- Franklin's expedition "pursued its course to Cape Walker"
- Had he found the Strait barred and gone up Wellington Inlet, he would have left memorandum at Cape Riley departing from instructions
- Richardson concludes Franklin went west from Barrow's Strait toward the hoped-for open passage

## Statements from Indigenous People About White Men and Strangers

### General Observations:
- Reports of "white men is a proof of a fund of good-nature" (p. 3071)
- Dog-rib camped "opposite Fort Franklin" noted "approach of strangers" with sharp eyes while at great distance
- When arrival announced, "men, women and children crowded into the square of the fort"
- Young hunters welcomed with kindness ("It is long since we have seen you, my relation")

### Specific Incidents:
- Dog-rib from opposite side of lake came to Fort Confidence, said he'd "been of great use to our two fishermen stationed at Fort Franklin"
- Dog-rib reported he shared produce of nets at Fort Franklin fishery
- Dog-rib stated he had wife taken by larger man; when questioned why he didn't fight back: "You see, it was a big Indian that took her! I am a little fellow, what could I do!"

### Instructions for Expected Franklin Search Party (1850):
- Richardson's letter from Lake Winnipeg (August 19, 1849) to Rae instructed engagement of Martin Lake or Bear Lake Indians
- Tecon-ne-betah to receive three notes for "any Europeans he might meet"
- Notes requested strangers to "put themselves wholly in the hands of the Indians"
- Indians would "guide them by the best road and feed them by the way"
- If no Europeans met, notes with rough charts "to be placed on a pole in conspicuous stations"

## Quotations (Under 70 words each)

### Q1: Albert's Sledge Innovation
"Albert found more ready imitators in another practice which he taught the men... forthwith they requested him to prepare their sledges in the same way; and during the winter the sledge-men invariably dressed their runners in the Eskimo mode." (p. 83, winter 1848-49)

### Q2: Richardson's Judgment of Franklin's Position
"With the prospect of soon passing the Straits, he evidently had not thought it necessary to erect a cairn, or signal-post, at the threshold, as it were, of the enterprise." (p. 155, on Franklin at Cape Riley, August 1845)

### Q3: Dog-Rib Woman's Ordeal After Birth
"She then set out dragging a sledge, and having her first-born suspended between her shoulders, in a bag or Indian cradle. She was not suffered, however, to profit by the well-beaten path... but had to make a new track parallel to it through the loose snow." (p. 93, March 1849)

### Q4: Franklin's Likely Route
"These first traces of the Expedition are exceedingly interesting, and they lead directly to the inference that it pursued its course to Cape Walker." (p. 155, Richardson's conclusion on Franklin, 1849-50)

### Q5: Indigenous Peoples' Provision Sharing
"While his attention was thus engaged... one of the females having cut the strings of his knapsack would carry it off, amidst the laughter of the crowd." (p. 93, Fort Confidence March 1849, Martin Lake Indians)

### Q6: Rae's Coastal Journey - Ice Conditions
"As the fine weather had now evidently broken up, and as there was every appearance of an early winter, I thought it would be a useless waste of time to wait any longer in hopes of being able to cross to Wollaston Land." (Rae's Report, August 22, 1849)

### Q7: Instructions for Franklin Search Party
"Tecon-ne-betah was... engaged... to pass the summer at certain stations on the route between the Kendall and Bear Lake. He received three notes which were to be delivered to any Europeans he might meet, in which the strangers were requested to put themselves wholly in the hands of the Indians." (p. 233, Rae extract July 30, 1850)

### Q8: Richardson's Return to England
"Landing at Liverpool on the 6th of November, 1849, after an absence of nineteen months, twelve of them passed in incessant travelling." (p. 145, Richardson's arrival home)


## Death of Albert One-eye

**Albert One-eye drowned at the Bloody Fall on the Coppermine River during Rae's summer 1849 expedition (July 1849)**

**Circumstances:**
- Boat being towed up Bloody Fall (waterfall on Coppermine)
- Steersman (James Hope) "seized with a sudden panic" called to slack the line on a smooth, easily-passable section
- Bowman and steersman jumped ashore, boat "allowed to sheer out into the current"
- Line broke, boat overturned
- Albert and Richardson ran to two points of rock where wreck would pass
- Albert hooked boat with oar until Richardson came to aid, fixed pole in broken plank
- Albert "either did not hear me correctly, or thinking that he would be of more use on the bottom of the boat, sprung to it"
- Current turned and swept them into small bay, then back out again
- "the last I saw of our excellent interpreter was his making a leap towards the rocks; he missed them and disappeared, nor did he rise again to the surface."
- Richardson blamed James Hope's cowardice and carelessness with small towing line

**Character assessment by Richardson (p. 129-130):**
"This melancholy accident grieved me much, as the brave lad was universally liked for his activity, lively and amiable disposition, and extreme good nature."

## Inuit Statement About White Men

**Important Indigenous testimony from Rae's expedition, July 1849:**

When Rae's party reached the coast and met Eskimos at Richardson Bay:
"In the winter they had communicated, either directly or indirectly, with the natives of Wollaston Land, **none of whom had ever seen whites, large boats, or ships**."

(p. 123) This is significant as evidence that Franklin's expedition had NOT reached Wollaston Land during the 1848-49 period.

Also noted: The Eskimos visited showed signs of starvation - "they had nearly starved, owing to the scarcity of seals, having had to subsist for some time on the skins of the larger species of these animals, which they had reserved for making boots."

## Additional Notes on Winter 1848-49

### Letter Service Failures
- September 18, 1848: First packet of Admiralty despatches sent with Frangois Chartier and Louis la Ronde
- Intended route: Isle à la Crosse (Chartier's wife residence)
- Fort Simpson: no delay
- Fort Chepewyan: reached by open water
- Isle à la Crosse: **two months detention** (unexplained delay)
- Carlton House: kept two months (delay described as "unaccountable")
- Did not arrive England until mid-July (instead of April-May as expected)
- Families without intelligence for nearly 12 months
- Other letters not sent express, left to ordinary conveyance

### Arctic Information Received  
- April 12, 1849: Mail from England with news dated June 22, 1848 (10 months old)
- Same mail: newspaper (Galena Advertiser) with English news dated September 15
- Newspaper transmitted by telegraph from New York September 30
- Carried over plains to Red River by express steamer party
- Reported rebellion in Ireland to Fort Confidence
