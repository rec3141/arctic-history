## 1. Sir Edward Belcher's North-East Expedition

**Header:** Sir Edward Belcher; sledges Londesborough (Mr. Grove, 7 men, 2 officers), Dauntless (Grove, 7 men, 1 officer), Enterprise (Allard, 10 men, 1 officer), + Hamilton sledge-boat, 2 Cape York dogs, 3 pups. Departed 2 May 1853, 6 A.M.; returned 23 June 1853, ~5 P.M.; 40-50 days provision (40 days for Londesborough and Dauntless, 50 for Enterprise). Pages 248–328. Total duration: ~52 days. Total provision: 1,230 rations for 28 men (Allard turned back 13 May at Cape Disraeli; final party 20 men).

### Quotations

1. "Several seals were noticed on our journey towards Village Point, but all too wary to permit any one to get within shot. Indeed, unless these animals are instantaneously killed, they preserve such a convenient distance to the hole in the ice, that their dying agonies alone would cause them to slip or wriggle through before they could be secured." (Page 248-249, 2 May)

2. "It is yet an unexplained fact, but we found a broad and deep sledge-mark in the gravel from the cairn to the point. [No sledge] could recall to mind any sledge taking such an unusual direction, and dragged, too, entirely on gravel." (Page 249, 2 May)

3. "The key of Jones's Sound. Such it proved. A snore, and all again was silence." (Page 266, 14 May, at Cape Disraeli)

4. "Here we stood on this noble headland on the 20th of May, in a bright, warm sun, in latitude 76°30'42\" north, and eastward of 90° west, with an open navigable sea below us: yes! the 'Polar Sea,' for aught any man knows to the contrary, beneath us." (Page 274, 20 May, Britannia Cliffs)

## 2. Commander George Henry Richards's South-West Journey (April–July 1853)

**Header:** Commander George Henry Richards; 6 sledges with 57 men total, 40 days rations each; accompanied by commissariat sledges to 110° W (where he retained 40 days); also carried Admiralty despatches for Melville Island and despatches for Captain Kellett at rendezvous (77° N, 105° W). Departed 10 April 1853 (~4 P.M.); returned 12 July 1853 (3 P.M.). 95 days total absence. Carried light ice-boat. Pages 242–244, 345.

**Key event:** Parted from Lieutenant Osborn evening of 17 May; on morning of 18 May discovered and met Lieutenant Hamilton (from Resolute) 21 days absent from ship; Resolute and Intrepid wintering at Dealy Island, Bridport Inlet.

### Notes on Journey
- First sledge (Dauntless, Grove) returned 20 April with letter from Richards reporting all well, 45 miles progress despite gales, temperature −27°, high spirits, no casualties
- Commissariat sledges to aid to 110° W meridian
- Met Resolute party under Lieutenant Hamilton ~18 May near Cape Richards (context suggests south-west Bathurst Island area)
- Hamilton brought news of M'Clure's Discovery of records at Sandstone Rock, Parry's Winter Harbour (M'Clure reached Resolute 19 April)
- Returned with 95-day absence: 10 April to 12 July
- Brought complete reports from western division (Captain Kellett's command)

## Other Spring 1853 Journeys

### Depot-Laying Expedition: Loney and Allard (April)
**Header:** Mr. Loney and Mr. Allard; 2 sledges, north-east toward Exmouth Island. Departed 12 April 1853; returned by 20 April. Pages 244–246.
- Task: Lay first north-east depot; test ice conditions for boats beyond Exmouth Island
- Weather: Severe gales, temperatures −25° to −27°, snow-drift
- Result: Made good 45 miles despite adverse weather; water in Main-tide Channel (between Pioneer Island and Village Point) open for half mile (re-froze under −25°)
- One bear sighted but too wary

### Commissariat Sledges (April–May)
**Header:** Under Richards's command; 6 sledges with 57 men. Accompanied main south-west party from 10 April; scheduled to aid Richards until he reached 110° W meridian (~early May), at which point Richards would have 40 days remaining and depots secured for return.

### Lieutenant Cheyne's Despatch Run (April–May)
**Header:** Lieutenant Cheyne; single sledge under 'Victory' banner with despatches. Departed ~20 April with Admiralty correspondence for Cape Becher to meet Commander Pullen. Initially returned 28 April reporting error (despatches left behind). Re-dispatched to retrieve despatches with Dr. Lyall's party.

## Notes on Personnel, Game, Weather, Illness, and Deaths

### Personnel and Named Individuals
- **Sir Edward Belcher:** Commander, Eastern Division
- **Commander George Henry Richards:** Deputy commander, south-west division
- **Lieutenant Sherard Osborn:** Sledge officer (Dauntless initially)
- **Mr. Grove:** Officer (Dauntless; also served as commissariat officer)
- **Mr. Allard:** Officer (Enterprise; later returned to ship 13 May)
- **Mr. Loney:** Master, navigator
- **Dr. Lyall:** Surgeon, sledge officer (Lady Franklin)
- **Lieutenant Cheyne:** Despatches officer
- **Mr. Pym:** Navigator (mentioned for whale-boat operations)
- **Punch:** Cape York dog, described as brave and courageous (page 262, 292); now at Zoological Gardens
- **York bitch:** Lost dog, recovered after 22 days absence (page 302)
- **"Lady Fanny Disco":** Dog dispatched to ship as message courier (page 330, with note to steward)
- **Coxswain:** Officer's personal assistant (one of Sir John Richardson's land party, page 288)

**Inuit personnel:** No named Inuit encountered on Belcher's north-east journey; references to "Esquimaux" (Western Esquimaux, Huskies) structures and graves found (hollow cairn, pseudo-graves) but no direct contact reported in spring 1853 journeys reviewed.

### Game and Hunting
- **Seals:** Numerous but wary, slipped away into holes (2 May, page 248); also near Jones's Sound (14 May)
- **Walrus:** Two secured on 20 August (separate expedition, not spring sledge); noted as common predator on hare/ptarmigan remains; hunting attempted multiple times without success
- **Bears:** Frequent encounters; mother and cub observed hunting technique (breaking ice holes for seals, page 294); bears generally fear man but are curious; several hunts attempted with poor success
- **Wolves:** One persistent wolf followed party from early May; initially "tabu" (protected for possible Franklin connection); later shot or potential capture for museums
- **Ptarmigan:** Abundant on land; several killed (2 taken 28 May, 1 on 27 May); described as small birds
- **Hares:** Tracked, found abundant deposits; 1 specimen observed and fired at 19 June (page 323), escaped
- **Lemmings:** Two fine specimens taken (~24 June, page 324); resembled moles
- **Musk-oxen:** First clear proof of existence on this side of Queen's Channel, 3 sighted 4 June (page 299); not hunted during spring journey
- **Brent-geese:** Flock noticed 2 June at Cape Disraeli (page 296); numerous in open water 19 June (page 322)
- **Other birds:** Owl, plover, snow-buntings, eider-duck, gulls, kittiwakes, mollymoks noted variously
- **Game total (first 50 days):** 2 hares, 5 ptarmigan, 1 brent-goose, several plover—insufficient for subsistence; major concern for provisioning (page 326)

### Weather and Environmental Conditions
- **Temperatures (May–June):** Range from −25° (April gales) to +20°–+32° (peak June); typically 0°–15° during travels; tent temps up to 80° internal vs 30° external
- **Snow conditions:** Deep and soft 2–3 May; varied from smooth floe to heavy pack ice with hummocks; tidal rents and cracks frequent hazard; open water appearing by late May/June
- **Wind:** Westerly/north-westerly gales in April; variable by May; north-westerly strong winds 6–7 June
- **Sun:** Continuous in May–June; oppressive daytime heat; reflected rays in faces 6 A.M.–midnight difficult; fog and refraction common near land; "true fog" with vapor condensation observed mid-June
- **Ice conditions:** Unpredictably tender (5 May, Belcher fell through); pack ice progressive breaking up June; "Hamilton Depot" at low spit possibly washable if free from ice

### Health and Illness
- **Snow blindness:** Common 10 May onward; treated with vinum opii (tincture of opium in water), glycerine, tea-leaves, tobacco; Belcher's remedy (10 drops vinum opii in tablespoon water daily wash) most effective (page 259)
- **Fatigue:** Belcher personally affected by uncontrollable stupor ~2 A.M. or after luncheon; required 10–15 minute dozes; obtained little sleep at any time; attributed to high strain
- **Carbuncle:** Belcher troubled by swelling of right cheek (May 31–June 1), painful especially in cold weather; attributed by some to pemmican diet (page 292)
- **Eye weakness:** Multiple crew members affected by weakness/partial snow blindness by June (page 303)
- **Hypothermia risks:** Belcher's cold bath at Star Bluff (5 May) immersion in current-swept water; cold chronometer concern; nearly entrapped; no major injury reported
- **Deaths:** None recorded among Belcher's north-east party (spring 1853)
- **Commissariat:** Pemmican in multiple varieties (currants/herbs, sugared, plain, herbs); 3/4 pound per person per day typically; maccaroni or potatoes added; tea issued daily; alcohol (grog) luncheon allowance
- **Depot caches:** Bread damaged and buried with cairn at Star Bluff; spare provisions deposited at multiple stations for return (page 276)

### Other Observations
- **Whale remains:** Fossils discovered: imbedded whale head/skeleton ~500 feet elevation 11 May (Mount Parker); another whale skeleton ~80 feet elevation found June; whale vertebrae found as markers left by Richards's party
- **Geological:** Fossiliferous limestone abundant (Terebratulae, Pectens, Madreporites, Entrochi); grauwacke slate; clay ironstone; selenite; coal fragments
- **Vegetation:** Saxifrage patches on sandy points; moss abundant in valleys (dead black moss in "graves," containing fly larvae/chrysalis coverings); no substantial plant growth observed on exposed land
- **Water:** First fresh running water since previous August discovered 29 May at Wellington Channel region; major morale boost for crew
- **European structures:** Hollow cairn at Cape Disappointment (27 May), ~8 feet on axis, 40 courses of stone, internal height 4 feet, carefully filled with moss and sand—origin mysterious, deemed "recent construction" but purpose unclear; two pseudo-graves at Grave Mount (30 May), ~7–8 feet long, 3 feet wide, east-west orientation, heavy slabs of unknown origin, excavated into solid rock, contained only dead moss and fly larvae—possibly caches; peculiar delicately-balanced pile of 5–6 stones at Pitch Mount (31 May)
