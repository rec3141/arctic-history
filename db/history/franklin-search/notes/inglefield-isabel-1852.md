# The Isabel, 1852: positions, names and people

Source: Inglefield, *A Summer Search for Sir John Franklin* (London: Thomas
Harrison, 1853). File on disk: `files/franklin-search-inglefield1853-summer-clean.txt`
(Google-digitised copy, clean OCR) and `files/franklin-search-inglefield1853-summer.pdf`
(CIHM microform scan) and `files/franklin-search-inglefield1853-summer-bsb.pdf`
(Bavarian State Library scan).

## A. The voyage in outline (narrative, pp. i-xxiv and 1-)

- 4 July 1852: the Isabel moves out of the basin at Woolwich Dockyard.
- 5 July: off the Nore, in tow of HM steamer Lightning; Inglefield writes to the
  Admiralty asking to be towed beyond Peterhead (refused, 7 July).
- Peterhead: Manson (ice-master), Ogston (second mate), a carpenter and five
  young whalers join. A crow's nest, ice-saws and fresh beef shipped.
- Evening of 10 July: sails from Peterhead.
- 6 p.m., 12 July: takes departure from Fair Isle.
- 20 July: noon 58 deg 4 min N, 19 deg 44 min W; 796 miles to run to Cape Farewell.
- 30 July: off Cape Farewell (register).
- 12 August: at Godhavn (Goodhaven), Disko. Writes to the Admiralty. Learns
  Belcher's squadron passed on 12 June, having found the Waigat blocked.
  Determines to go direct to Smith Sound.
- Upernavik: dogs bought; no interpreter to be had.
- Melville Bay crossed with little difficulty; loose ice at Cape York.
- Morning of 22 August: the great glacier of Petowak (Pituffik). Becalmed close
  in; voices from the beach; Inughuit come down the glacier face. A woman draws
  the coast and the position of north Omenak on the snow.
- 23 August: Cape Athol at daybreak; steams round Wolstenholme Sound within
  pistol-shot. North Omenak found deserted for the season; a cairn of bones
  pulled down and dug into, on Abernethy's account of what Adam Beck had said;
  nothing European found.
- 25 August, afternoon: enters Whale Sound after being blown back three times.
- 25/26 August: a settlement 25 miles inside Whale Sound visited; a knife marked
  "B. Wilson, cast steel" in a narwhal-ivory handle, an axe, a tin canister
  obtained. Inglefield climbs 1000 ft and sees the north side of the sound is a
  group of islands. Names Northumberland Island, Herbert Island, Tyrconnel
  Island, and Murchison Inlet (Sound); estimates the current setting east 3.5
  knots.
- Midnight, 26 August: rounds Cape Alexander under sail and steam within half a
  musket shot, 145 fathoms. Names Cape Albert (the Queen's consort's birthday).
  Opens Smith Sound: "this must lead into the great Polynia of the Russians."
  Measures the strait at about 36 miles across, not the narrow neck of the charts.
- Noon 27 August: latitude 78 deg. Names the Prince of Wales range, Victoria Head,
  Cape Frederick VII, Cape Victoria, Hatherton Island (Bay), Cape Isabella.
- 28 August: highest latitude, 78 deg 28 min 21 sec N (observed altitude 21 deg
  15 min), officers called to witness. A landing to build a cairn and observe dip
  and variation is abandoned on the officers' advice; a gale blows the ship out
  of the strait; hove to 36 hours under close-reefed fore topsail.
- 29 August: drifted into the lee pack on the west side of the head of Baffin
  Bay; boiler leaking, rudder splintering; steam raised as the last resource and
  the ship worked out.
- 31 August: passes down the west coast to Coborg Island, through Glacier Strait
  and up Jones Sound; beats west to longitude 84 deg W, where the coast turns
  north-west and no land can be seen to the west.
- Evening of 1 September: thick fog, gale from the west, snowdrift; turns back,
  examining the south shore eastward. Passes through Lady Ann Strait; by
  midnight abreast of Cape Parker, and beset by floes over twenty feet thick
  within a mile of the shore; bores out with steam.
- Noon 2 September: a good offing in brash ice off Cape Horsburgh.
- Morning of 7 September: enters Erebus and Terror Bay, Beechey Island, and
  anchors a few hours beside HMS North Star. Commander Pullen gives him
  despatches (duplicates; the Prince Albert had sailed a fortnight before) and
  Inglefield hands over the mail from England, eight weeks old, and a tracing
  from his track chart.
- Then down the west shore of Baffin Bay, erecting cairns, firing guns and
  throwing up rockets at night. 15 September: off Cape Adair, writes his
  despatch and sends it home by a vessel bound to England.
- On the Hecla and Griper Bank the ice stops him and carries him into longitude
  61.5 deg W. Two days and a night in the pack.
- 22 October [Inglefield's own numbering; the register shows this belongs to
  September]: 120 miles run to the southward; 23rd, a tremendous SE gale, six
  days lying to under a storm sail, water down to a pint a man.
- 2 October: runs into the Hunde Islands to water and refit; sails on the 7th.
- Gales from SW then NE; scuds two days towards Cumberland Inlet, where he meant
  to winter; the ice-masters Abernethy and Manson advise against closing that
  coast so late; after two days' wait he bears up for England.
- Total: 1,473 miles under steam, 66 tons of fuel still on board; "600 miles of
  new coast line correctly laid down"; two months longer inside the Arctic
  Circle than the previous year's expedition.

## C. Names given on the voyage (Inglefield's despatch of 15 September 1852)

- Northumberland Island (for the Duke of Northumberland, First Lord)
- Herbert Island (for Sir Thomas Herbert)
- Tyrconnel Island
- Murchison Inlet / Murchison Sound (for Sir Roderick Murchison)
- Cape Albert (the north point, named on the Prince Consort's birthday, 26 Aug)
- Prince of Wales range (the mountains of the west shore of Smith Sound)
- Victoria Head
- Cape Frederick VII (for the King of Denmark)
- Cape Victoria
- Hatherton Island / Hatherton Bay (for Lord Hatherton)
- Cape Isabella (for the ship)
- Cape Sabine (for Sir Edward Sabine; the Cary Islands and Cape Sabine appear on
  the track chart with the other new names)

## D. People aboard

- Edward Augustus Inglefield, commander
- Peter Cormack Sutherland, surgeon and naturalist (previously with Penny)
- William Abernethy, chief ice-master (several government expeditions)
- Manson, ice-master (many whaling voyages; joined at Peterhead)
- Ogston, second mate (joined at Peterhead)
- Bardin, engineer (engaged for the vessel by Captain Beatson; superintended the
  building of the engines)
