## A. Chronology

1850
- 22 May, about one o'clock: the brigs leave the Navy Yard at Brooklyn under
  tow. Cheered from the wharves past the Battery. A squall off Sandy Hook.
- 25 May: Grinnell's pilot boat Washington takes the last letters and turns back.
- 23 June: off Sukkertoppen, 68 deg 22 min N, 53 deg 05 min W.
- 6 July: 72 deg 54 min N, beating between the pack and the land near Upernavik.
- Entered the middle pack at 74 deg 08 min N, 59 deg 04 min W and lay in it
  three weeks; on coming out, 73 deg 54 min N, 60 deg 06 min W.
- 29 July: 75 deg 02 min 27 sec N, 59 deg 50 min 42 sec W.
- August, near Cape York, 75 deg 58 min N: the frozen Inughuit families.
- 16 August: 76 deg 25 min N, the most northern point reached in Baffin Bay.
- 22 August: (De Haven's report opens from this date.)
- 23 August: off Port Leopold; falls in with the Prince Albert, Commander
  Forsyth, who says the harbour is still full of ice.
- Noon, 25 August: off Cape Riley. A boat lands and finds a cairn holding a
  record of HMS Assistance left the day before, and another saying the Rescue
  had been there at the same time. Fragments of painted wood and preserved-meat
  tins on the low point. The Advance grounds on the falling tide and is hauled
  off about midnight.
- Point Innes: traces of an encampment and more relics; Penny's people pick up
  a scrap of paper with the name of one of Franklin's officers on it in pencil.
  An old Inuit house, long abandoned.
- 27 August: the vessels made fast to the land ice on the north-west side of
  Beechey Island. The Felix (John Ross) and Penny's squadron join. A joint
  search is agreed; one of Penny's men comes back to report graves. Three graves
  with painted headboards: W. Braine, RM, Erebus, died 3 April 1846, aged 33;
  John Hartnell, AB, Erebus, aged 23; John Torrington, Terror, died 1 January
  1846, aged 20. Around them, rope and canvas, the blocks for the armourer's
  anvil, tent outlines, carpenter's shavings, and a great number of meat tins
  with the same label as those at Point Innes. The graves were not opened.
  De Haven's report gives 27 August both for making fast and for casting off;
  one of the two dates is a slip of his pen.
- 27 August: Griffin, with Midshipman Lovell and four men, returns from a
  48-hour boat search north; he found a large partly overturned cairn a few
  miles south of Cape Bowden, apparently a provision depot, with nothing in it,
  and reached Cape Bowden at two p.m. on the 28th, building a cairn there.
  The inlet north of Cape Bowden is named Griffin Inlet.
- Early on 28 August: HMS Resolute (Austin) and her steam tender arrive from the
  eastward. Several days' search with crows and picks at the cairn on the
  highest part of Beechey Island; no record found.
- To 3 September: held at Point Innes by ice packing in from the south.
- 3 September: stands west into a lead; stopped at midnight two thirds of the
  way across the channel.
- 5 September: reaches Barlow Inlet and secures at its entrance.
- 9 September: gets under way past Cape Hotham along the south side of
  Cornwallis Island.
- 10 September: reaches Griffith Island and makes fast beside the English ships.
  De Haven and Griffin decide, in accordance with the instructions, to extricate
  the vessels and return to the United States.
- 12 September: a heavy north-west gale; the Rescue is blown from her ice
  anchors, leaving a boat and two men behind, and springs her rudder under the
  lee of Griffith Island.
- 13 September: the ships rejoin and run east in tow; past Cape Hotham at eight
  p.m., then the wind fails, the snow on the water cements, and at eight p.m.
  they come to a dead stand about ten miles east of Barlow Inlet. This is the
  beginning of the nine-month besetment.
- 14-19 September: southerly winds set them up Wellington Channel.
- 18 September: above Cape Bowden, the most northern point Parry had seen on
  that shore.
- 19 September: 75 deg 20 min 11 sec N, about seven miles from the western
  shore. Murdaugh sets the artificial horizon on the ice for the observation.
- 21 September: 75 deg 20 min 38 sec N. At tea-time, hilltops seen to the north
  by west; "whether this Grinnell Land, as our captain has named it..." (Kane).
  De Haven names Grinnell Land, Maury Channel, Mount Franklin, Cape Manning
  and Murdaugh Island.
- 22 September: 75 deg 24 min 21 sec N, about sixty miles from Cape Hotham. The
  farthest north of the drift; the seat of the resting place was later computed
  at 75 deg 24 min 52 sec N.
- 23 September: the Advance heavily nipped, ice piled above the rail on the
  starboard side; all hands kept it out until near midnight.
- 4 October: the drift begins in earnest again.
- 7 October: 74 deg 54 min 07 sec N, drifting south.
- 20 October: fires lit below for the first time. Ten days later the housing
  cloth goes over and the Rescue's officers and men move aboard the Advance for
  the winter.
- 1 November: the new ice thirty-seven inches thick. Drifted to and fro in
  Wellington Channel through October and November, at one point five miles
  south-west of Beechey Island.
- 30 November - 1 December: a westerly gale; on the 1st they make out Gascoigne
  Inlet. Clear of Wellington Channel and in the fairway of Lancaster Sound.
- 3 December: two miles off Cape Hurd.
- 5-9 December: repeated cracks and a three-point nip that lifts the Advance's
  stern five feet; the Rescue's bowsprit and head carried away on the 13th
  (January, see below). Drift east averaging nearly six miles a day.
- 31 December: at the entrance of the sound, Cape Osborn bearing north.

1851
- January: out into Baffin Bay; the drift turns south, parallel to the west
  shore at forty to seventy miles off.
- 11-14 January: the floe breaks up around the ships; the Rescue's bowsprit and
  head broken off; much of the stores on the ice crushed and lost. The Advance
  is left with her stern raised and a list to starboard, and stays that way
  until the spring.
- 20 January: Cape Walter Bathurst, the last land seen for four months.
- 29 January: the sun rises its whole diameter after eighty-seven days' absence;
  three cheers.
- February and March: mercury freezes. Scurvy through the winter, held in check
  by Kane and the other medical officers; not a man was lost.
- 1 April: ice formed since the first besetment measured seven feet two inches.
- 17 April: 69 deg 52 min N, 63 deg 03 min W; the cabin lamps put out.
- 24 April: the Rescue's people move back aboard her.
- 26 April: 69 deg 40 min 42 sec N, 63 deg 08 min 46 sec W.
- 19 May: the land about Cape Searle made out, the first seen since 20 January.
- 27 May: 66 deg 42 min 40 sec N, 60 deg 54 min W, off Cape Walsingham; passed
  out of the Arctic Zone.
- 6 June, about five p.m.: the floe splits in every direction, silently. The
  Rescue is freed at once; the Advance's stern stays locked and takes
  forty-eight hours of saws, axes and crowbars. Three cheers when she righted.
- 9 June: the two part company in fog.
- 10 June: the Advance forces out into open sea in latitude 65 deg 30 min, about
  thirty-five miles from where she was liberated. Nine months in the ice.
- 16 June: Whale-fish Islands; 17 June Lievely (Godhavn), the Rescue joining
  next day. 22 June: sails north again to search one more season.
- 1 July: anchors at Proven. 8 July: communicates with Upernavik. 9 July: meets
  the M'Lellan of New London with letters from home.
- 11 July: the Advance grounds on a rocky shoal running between the Baffin
  Islands; hauled off after twenty-four hours by hawsers to a grounded berg
  measured at 245 feet above water, aground in 96 fathoms.
- 16 July: the Prince Albert reaches them; Kennedy comes aboard with letters.
- 27 July - 4 August: beset again; the Advance severely nipped, her rudder
  carried under and recovered.
- 13 August: loses sight of the Prince Albert.
- 18 August: works out between the bergs; the lead closes a few miles on, and
  De Haven gives up, under the clause in his instructions forbidding a second
  winter.
- 6 September: leaves Holsteinsborg for New York. The vessels separate in a gale
  south of Cape Farewell.
- 30 September: the Advance reaches New York; the Rescue on 7 October.

## B. People

- Edwin Jesse De Haven, lieutenant, USN, commanding, in the Advance. An invalid
  through the winter; Griffin took the executive duties.
- Samuel P. Griffin, acting master (passed midshipman), commanding the Rescue.
- Elisha Kent Kane, passed assistant surgeon, senior medical officer, in the
  Advance; author of the narrative.
- William H. Murdaugh, acting master of the Advance; took the observations on
  the ice. Murdaugh Island named for him.
- William J. Lovell, midshipman; with Griffin on the boat search to Cape Bowden.
- Henry Grinnell, of New York, who bought and fitted both brigs and gave them to
  the Navy to man. His pilot boat Washington saw them out to sea.

## C. Names given

Grinnell Land, Maury Channel, Mount Franklin, Cape Manning (for a friend and
supporter of the expedition), Murdaugh Island, Griffin Inlet, Advance Bluff.
