## C. The abandonments of 1854

### The order

- **7 Feb. 1854** — Belcher writes to Kellett "requesting" that he bring the officers and crews of *Resolute* and
  *Intrepid* to meet him at Beechey Island before 26 August. [M p. 448]
- Kellett replies (same date) that **"nothing contained in his orders would justify him in abandoning the ships under
  his command,"** and asks Belcher for **"final, decided, and most unmistakeable orders."** In a private letter of the
  same date he adds that it was the unanimous opinion of all on board the *Resolute* that they would not suffer from
  the first break-up, the ice being only of the previous year's formation. [M pp. 448–449]
- **21 Apr. 1854** — Belcher's answer directs Kellett to withdraw everything valuable and bring officers and crews to
  Beechey Island. [M p. 449]
- **28 Apr. 1854, 4.30 p.m.** — Commander M'Clintock returns to the *Resolute* (seven days from the *Assistance*, five
  from Beechey Island) bringing "decided orders from Sir Edward Belcher to abandon the ships," after landing a
  quantity of provisions at the depot near Cape Cockburn. [M p. 386]
- **30 Apr. 1854** — Kellett tells the assembled crews at divisions that he means to take the whole crew to Beechey
  Island, but orders clothes packed "so as to avoid confusion, in the event of re-occupying the ship." [M pp. 386–387]
- The Admiralty's own letter of **28 Apr. 1854** (which reached Beechey only with the *Phoenix* in August) called for
  "at once withdrawing, if possible, the whole of the force now employed in the search of Sir J. Franklin from the
  Polar Seas." [B pp. 8–10]

### The march home and the homeward passage

- 15–28 May 1854: the *Resolute*'s four sledges cross Barrow Strait — Cape Cockburn depot (18th), Moore Island (20th),
  Baker Island (22nd), Browne Island (23rd), Cape Martyr and Resolute Bay (25th), Assistance Harbour depot (26th),
  Cape Hotham/Rugged Bluff (27th), *North Star* (28th). Sledge loads averaged **215 lb per man**; officers limited to
  45 lb of personal effects, men to 30 lb. [M pp. 387, 390, 392–404]
- Thomas Morgan (seaman, late *Investigator*) died on board the *North Star* on 22 May 1854 and was buried on Beechey
  Island on the 24th beside the *Erebus* and *Terror* graves. [M pp. 404, 434]
- **26 Aug. 1854, 1.10 p.m.**: *Phoenix* (Capt. Inglefield) and *Talbot* (Cdr. Jenkins) appear off Cape Riley — an hour
  and a half after Belcher reached the *North Star* with news of the abandonment. Inglefield brings word of the war
  with Russia. Crews are divided equally among the three ships. [M pp. 437–438]
- **27 Aug. 1854**: de Bray erects the marble tablet to Bellot on the Beechey monument, three volleys fired; at 1 p.m.
  the three ships slip from the ice, *North Star* in tow of *Phoenix*. Belcher did **not** call at Port Leopold, which
  M'Dougall regrets. Navy Board Inlet 29 Aug.; Lievely 10–11 Sept.; parted from *Phoenix* and *Talbot* in a gale after
  Cape Farewell; home the **first week of October 1854**. [M pp. 439–447]
- **Court martial**, H.M.S. *Waterloo* at Sheerness, **18–20 Oct. 1854**, Admiral the Hon. George Gordon presiding.
  M'Clure acquitted, the Admiral returning his sword with "your conduct throughout your arduous exertions has been
  most meritorious and praiseworthy." Kellett **honourably acquitted** — "This order released Captain Kellett from all
  responsibility connected with the abandonment" — and the Admiral restored his sword "observing that he experienced
  much satisfaction in returning a sword which the owner had worn with so much credit, satisfaction, and advantage to
  his country." Belcher was acquitted too, but the finding added that "if circumstances had permitted, it would have
  been advisable that he should have consulted with Captain Kellett previously"; M'Dougall records only that "the
  president then returned Sir Edward Belcher his sword, and the court broke up" — no compliment of any kind is
  reported. [M pp. 447–451]

### What Kellett and M'Dougall thought

Kellett's position is on the record only through the court-martial evidence (quoted above): he did not think his orders
justified abandonment and forced Belcher to put the order beyond doubt. M'Dougall never attacks Belcher in print — he
was a serving officer writing in 1857 — but his sympathies are unmistakable: he prints Kellett's protest at length,
calls the *Resolute*'s abandonment "without precedent," records that the ice around her was only one season old, and
troubles to correct the press on where and in what condition she was left. See the quotes in §F.

## D. Kellett's sledge work from Dealy Island, spring 1853

### Pim's journey to the *Investigator*

- **10 March 1853, 8.30 a.m.** — Lieut. **Bedford Pim** (sledge *John Barrow*, motto "Hope on, Hope ever"; 7 men, 88
  days' provisions) and **Dr. Domville** (*James Fitzjames*; 2 men, 5 Esquimaux dogs, 25 days) leave Dealy Island, with
  Mr. Roche (*Beauty*, 8 men, 10 days) as auxiliary as far as Cape Providence. Roche returned to the ship 20 March.
  [M pp. 199–200, 520]
- **29 March** — Pim's sledge breaks down about 20–25 miles S.W. of Cape Dundas. He transfers his party and gear to
  Domville and pushes on with the dog sledge and two men. Domville, an inexperienced traveller, double-trips 15 cwt.
  over 25 miles of rough ice and reaches Cape Dundas only on **9 April**. [M pp. 217–218, 521]
- **6 April 1853** — Pim reaches the *Investigator* in the **Bay of Mercy, Baring (Banks) Island, lat. 74°06′ N, long.
  117°56′ W** — **28 days out, 160 miles as the crow flies, 427 statute miles actually travelled (181 direct)**.
  M'Dougall's footnote: *"This is the earliest extended spring journey on record."* M'Clure had fixed 15 April for
  abandoning his ship — nine days later. [M pp. 217–219, 521]
- **7 April** — M'Clure leaves the Bay of Mercy for the *Resolute*, arriving **19 April**, 160 miles in twelve days.
  Pim's own journey closes 19 April 1853. [M pp. 220, 520]

Kellett went out with the parties for a week and came back on **11 April** having "until this, been ignorant of the

weather on 17 May, about 110 miles from the *Resolute*. [B p. 82; M pp. 246–247]

## E. Lieutenant Joseph René Bellot, died 18 August 1853

**Belcher, vol. I, does not mention Bellot at all** — a search of the whole file returns zero occurrences of the name.
The account below is M'Dougall's.

- Bellot, lieutenant in the French Imperial Navy, had already served with Kennedy in Lady Franklin's *Prince Albert*
  (1851–52); in 1853 he was attached to H.M.S. *Phoenix* (Cdr Inglefield), which reached Beechey Island on 14 August
  1853 with the transport *Breadalbane*. He **volunteered** to carry despatches up Wellington Channel to Belcher — "a
  service of extreme risk, at this season of the year." [M pp. 269, 381]
- **18 August 1853**, in a heavy gale — the same gale that drove the *Resolute* out of Dealy Island. The party was
  about to land to tent **near Point Hogarth**; a lane of water lay between the marching ice and the shore, their line
  was too short to haul the sledge onto the beach, and they were forced to stay on the moving floe. Bellot was last
  seen **going round a hummock, out of sight of his companions**, and never returned. "The prevailing idea is that he
  was blown, or stumbled into the sea, and was unable to regain the ice." [M pp. 381–382]
- M'Dougall calls it "this sad accident, the first and last, which has occurred in all the Searching Expeditions"
  (i.e. the only death by accident, as opposed to disease). [M p. 382]
- Two days later, on 20 August 1853, the *Breadalbane* was crushed and sank in fifteen minutes half a mile south of
  Beechey Island. [M p. 383]
- A head-board was erected on Beechey Island in 1854 ("…UNFORTUNATELY DROWNED, BY A SUDDEN DISRUPTION OF THE ICE, IN A
  HEAVY GALE OF WIND, ON THE MORNING OF THE 18TH DAY OF AUGUST, 1853, WHILST GALLANTLY LEADING A SMALL PARTY OF
  BRITISH SEAMEN FROM THE 'NORTH STAR' UP WELLINGTON CHANNEL…"), and on the forenoon of **27 August 1854** de Bray set
  the marble tablet sent out by John Barrow on the monument, with three volleys fired. [M pp. 434, 439]

**M'Dougall, Appendix `p. 496`:**

| Officer | New coast line discovered, 1853 |
|---|---|
| Cdr M'Clintock | 768 geographical miles |
| Lieut. Mecham | 680 |

| **Total** | **1,618 geog. = 1,870 statute miles** |

**M'Dougall, `p. 423`** (summary of all sledging by the *Resolute* and *Intrepid*, autumn 1852 – June 1854) gives the
same total, 1,618 geog. / 1,867 statute of new coast line, against roughly 13,337 geog. / 15,399 statute miles of

|---|---|---|---|
| 1848–49 | Sir James Ross | 990 / 1,143 | 200 / 230 |
| 1850–51 | Capt. H. T. Austin | 6,087 / 7,025 | 1,076 / 1,225 |
| 1850–51 | Capt. Penny | 1,700 / 1,965 | 500 / 578 |
| 1850–53 | Capt. M'Clure | 2,350 / 2,710 | 1,591 / 1,838 |
| 1851 | Dr. Rae | 1,030 / 1,190 | 617 / 712 |
| 1852 | Cdr Inglefield | — | 180 / 208 (from the vessel, no travelling) |
| **1852–54** | **Capt. Kellett, C.B.** | **9,432 / 10,881** \* | **1,618 / 1,870** |
| | **Total** | **21,589 / 24,914** | **5,782 / 6,661** |

\* "Exclusive of the march of the officers and crew to Beechey Island on abandoning the ship." Penny's new land in the
table is noted as approximate, "being measured from corrected chart of Wellington Channel, **by Sir E. Belcher**."

**Belcher vol. I gives no aggregate figure** for the *Assistance*/*Pioneer* division's survey — presumably it is in
vol. II. What vol. I offers instead is the running claim: the Queen's Channel, Northumberland Sound, Exmouth Island,
Table Island, Pell Point and the "Land's End of North Cornwall" in 1852; and in 1853 North Yorkshire (Capes Derby and
Stanley), North Kent, Prince Edward's Cape, Britannia Cliffs, the Victoria Archipelago (Buckingham Island, Mount
Windsor), and Cape Ogle. He is repeatedly scornful of the existing charts — "we were sailing over a great deal of hard
land of the published charts, without injury to the '*Pioneer*' or ourselves!" (`p. 86`) — and he redrew the coastline
between Cape Osborn and Cape Becher and corrected Penny's placing of Cape Sir John Franklin (`pp. 86–88`). M'Dougall,
for his own part, notes that the *Resolute*'s enforced drift let him correct the coast between Consett Head and Point
Griffiths and shorten Byam Martin Island by eleven miles (`p. 310`).

## Notes on the sources and their reliability

- The Appendix "Table of Positions" in M'Dougall (`pp. 481–486`) is heavily scrambled in OCR: the columns (date /
  lat ° ′ ″ / long ° ′ ″ / variation / bearings) are broken into one token per line. Rows quoted above were checked
  against the accompanying bearings text and against the narrative wherever possible; a handful of intermediate
  1852 rows were left out rather than risk mis-assembly.
- The drift table `pp. 359–360` (10 Sept – 12 Nov 1853) is legible and internally consistent, and is the best source
  for the *Resolute*'s second winter.
- Meteorological journal headers (`pp. 512–522`) independently confirm the winter position: "wintering in the Pack, in
  Lat. 74° 41′ 34″ N., Long. 101° 22′ 06″ W.," S.W. by S. 28 miles from Cape Cockburn.
- One OCR date is certainly corrupt: the sledge journal entry after the abandonment reads "Tuesday, I0ih June" where
  the sequence requires **Tuesday 16 May 1854** (`p. 393`). Likewise Belcher's printed record form at `p. 85` is dated
  "Saturday, 14th August, 1853" where the body of the entry and the surrounding narrative make it plainly **1852**.
