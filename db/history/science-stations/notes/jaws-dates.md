## Alert in detail (Johnson 1990, who was there as airstrip mechanic)

- 30 July 1948: USS *Edisto* and USCGC *Eastwind* leave Thule for Cape Columbia.
- 2 Aug 1948: *Edisto* anchors off Cape Sheridan, near Nares' Floeberg Beach.
- 5-6 Aug 1948: cache landed at Dumbbell Bay (a T-9 bulldozer, 12 drums of fuel, a Jamesway hut).
- 17 Nov 1948: F.W. Reichelderfer, chief of the US Weather Bureau, proposes the names *Alert* (Nares' ship) and *Belknap* (her naturalist-surgeon).
- 6 Jan 1949: the Board of Geographical Names in Ottawa adopts **Alert**.
- 28 Feb 1949: the spring 1949 airlift is postponed for want of cargo aircraft.
- 3 April 1950: the Alert crew are all assembled at Thule.
- 9 April 1950 (Easter Sunday): advance party lands on Dumbbell Bay in a USAF ski-wheel C-47; -46 C.
- 14 April 1950: 1000 m of ice runway cleared.
- 18 April 1950: first aircraft lands on the ice strip, a C-54 under Capt. M. Buls.
- 1 July 1950: synoptic weather observations begin, three times a day.
- **31 July 1950, shortly after noon**: an RCAF Lancaster on an air drop has its parachute foul the elevator; the aircraft dives into the ground about 450 m west of the station and explodes. No survivors. The nine killed: **Charles J. Hubbard** (US Weather Bureau, director of the Arctic Operations Project), **Dr. D.W. Kirk** (Canadian ice observer), and seven RCAF: W/C D.T. French DFC, F/L L.M. MacLean, F/L J.F.L. Swinton, F/O T.D. Martin, F/O J.F.L. Dube, F/O J.E. McCutcheon, LAC R.L. Sprance.
- 4 Aug 1950: an RCAF Canso flying boat brings the investigating officer, is beached and damaged.
- 7 Aug 1950: joint military funeral; Hubbard buried at Alert at his family's request, the Canadians' bodies flown out. A monument and plaque were put over Hubbard's grave a year later.
- 24 Aug 1950: 1350 m of gravel airstrip serviceable, year-round.
- 8 Sept 1950: first landing on the gravel airstrip (USAF C-54, Capt. Donahue).
- 11 Sept 1950: rawinsonde observations begin; the station is operational.
- 18 Oct 1950: first crew change.

## Names to watch

- Johnson 1990 spells the DOT controller **Thompson**; Heidt and Lackenbauer, and the
  Meteorological Service's own records, spell him **Andrew Thomson** (b. Owen Sound
  1893), who also planned Canada's part in the second International Polar Year of
  1932-33. Thomson is the correct spelling; database rows use Thomson.
- The 12 men who built Alert in 1950 (Johnson 1990 p. 25): C.J. Clifton (XO, US),
  S.M. Dow (radio, Can.), W.W. George (cook, US), Sam Griggs (mechanic, US),
  J.P. Johnson Jr. (airstrip mechanic, US), J. Leo Lafranchise (OIC, Can.),
  E.J. McCormick (airstrip, US), W.G. Morgan (senior airstrip mechanic, US),
  James Scovil (carpenter, Can.), G.A. Toole (rawinsonde, Can.),
  Stanley Whiteman (radio technician, US), George Zahary (rawinsonde, Can.).
