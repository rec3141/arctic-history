# Tyson's Ice-Floe Drift Positions, 15 Oct 1872 – 30 Apr 1873

Source: E. Vale Blake (ed.), *Arctic Experiences...* (1874), bibkey `blake1874arctic`,
extracted from George E. Tyson's journal as printed in Chapters XVI–XXVIII
(text file `arcticexperience00blakrich.txt`). Page numbers are the printed book
page nearest the quoted passage, read from OCR page-marker lines; where a full-page
plate falls between two marked pages, the page number is my best estimate and is
flagged "(approx)".

IMPORTANT: Tyson gives an explicit numerical latitude (sometimes with longitude)
only intermittently — roughly every 1–4 weeks, not daily. Between those dates he
gives directional/qualitative drift information (compass bearing of the current,
sighted landmarks, "we must be drifting fast to the southward," etc.) without a
number. The table below includes every entry where Tyson states a number, plus
the most important qualitative/landmark position references, so the story of the
drift is continuous. Rows with no number in the "lat as given" column are
qualitative-only. Gaps of a week or more between numeric fixes are called out.

Notes on longitude: Tyson gives an explicit longitude only twice in the whole
journal — 7 Dec 1872 (Meyers, 67°53′W, which Tyson doubted) and 19 Jan 1873
(Meyers, 60°0′36″W). No further longitude is ever stated. All other decimal
longitudes above marked "(interp.)" are my own linear interpolations between
the nearest dated fixes/landmarks (Littleton Island for the start; the real-world
position of Grady Harbor, Labrador for the rescue), NOT anything in the text —
treat them as approximate track-shape aids only, not sourced positions.

Note on the separation site: Tyson (in testimony and in his journal, p. 214)
believed the ship parted from the floe near **Littleton Island**; the ship's
sailing-master/Meyers thought **Northumberland Island**. The book's editor
resolves this in Ch. XXIX/XXX (pp. 352–353): when the *Tigress* searched the
coast in Aug 1873 she found Buddington's abandoned winter camp at **Life-boat
Cove, near Littleton Island, lat. 78°23′ N., long. 73°46′ W.** (p. 405: the
Polaris party's camp coordinates), confirming Tyson right. I used that position
to anchor the 15 Oct 1872 waypoint.
