[[person:Elisha Kent Kane]] was a naval surgeon of thirty-three, small, consumptive and rheumatic, when he took command of the Second Grinnell Expedition. The plan was to sail up Smith Sound, find a way to a supposed open sea at the top of the world, and look for [[topic:franklin-1845|Franklin]] there. The brig *Advance* was 144 tons and carried seventeen men. "We left New York on the 30th of May, 1853."

## Going in

At Fiskernaesset in south Greenland in July he shipped an eighteen-year-old Kalaallit hunter, [[person:Hans Hendrik]] Suersaq, as hunter and dog driver. It was the start of twenty-three years of Arctic service under four commanders, and it is the reason the only Inuit-authored book in this topic exists ([[source:hendrik1878memoirs]]).

On 7 August the brig passed [[place:Cape Alexander]] and entered Smith Sound. No ship had been so far up the channel. Kane pushed north into worsening ice, was driven back, and in early September warped the *Advance* in among rocky islets on the Greenland shore at about 78 deg 37 min N, 70 deg 40 min W — [[place:Rensselaer Harbour]]. He wrote afterwards, knowing what came next:

> "We found seven-fathom soundings and a perfect shelter from the outside ice; and thus laid our little brig in the harbor, which we were fated never to leave together, — a long resting-place to her indeed, for the same ice is around her still." ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-003]])

He set up an observatory ashore and left permanent marks so a later traveller could recover his instrument stations: lead poured into a crevice in the gneiss with a copper bolt at its centre, a chiselled arrow twenty-nine feet above the mean tide of that winter, a glass jar of documents five feet due west of it, and a cairn ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-007]]).

## Two winters

The sun set behind the southern ridge about 10 October 1853 and was gone until late February — Kane calculated the astronomical sunset at 24 October but the ridge took it earlier. The dogs died of a distemper that winter; the sledge parties failed and had to be rescued; men came back frostbitten.

On 24 June 1854 William Morton and Hans Hendrik, sledging north along the Greenland coast, ran out of ice at [[place:Cape Constitution]]. Morton climbed to 480 feet and looked out on water breaking in surf.

> "Not a speck of ice, to use his own words, could be seen." ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-004]])

Kane published this as the discovery of an open polar sea and it shaped Arctic exploration for a generation. What the two men had actually reached was the edge of the recurring polynya of Kennedy Channel — real, remarkable, and not an ice-free ocean.

Hans Hendrik was the other man on that shore, and twenty-four years later he described the same day without a trace of the rapture: "Again we went on, and fell in with a large open water. We followed the shore-ice, but when this ceased we were obliged to stop... The next afternoon we ascended the hill to build up a beacon, showing the point we had reached, and we marked the rock." ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-050]]) Open water, the end of the ice-foot, a beacon, and a bear shot on the way.

The brig did not get out in 1854. On 24 August Kane called all hands and told them he meant to stay: "At noon to-day I had all hands called, and explained to them frankly the considerations which have determined me to remain where we are." He gave leave to any man who wanted to walk south, on condition that he renounce all claim on the commander in writing. Nine men left on 28 August under [[person:Isaac Israel Hayes]] and Carl Petersen. Eight stayed with Kane: Brooks, McGary, Wilson, Goodfellow, Morton, Ohlsen, Hickey and Hans Hendrik.

The nine got no further than the Etah district. They wintered in a hut at [[place:Anoatok]], were fed all winter on frozen seal and walrus by the Inughuit, and were finally carried back to the brig on Inughuit sledges. At three in the morning of 12 December 1854, at fifty below:

> "They stopped at the gangway, and, as I was about to challenge, one of them sprang forward and grasped my hand. It was Doctor Hayes." ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-006]])

Kane then had to settle the Inughuit's complaint that his own men had taken clothing and fox skins from [[person:Kalutunah]]'s hut while sheltering in it. The whole company survived the second winter chiefly on walrus and seal bought or begged from [[person:Metek]], [[person:Ootuniah]], [[person:Myouk]] and the other hunters of Etah. Two men died of what Kane called "an anomalous spasmodic disorder, allied to tetanus", and by spring every man aboard was scorbutic.

## Leaving the brig

They abandoned the *Advance*. Every man signed a paper concurring in an attempt to reach the south by boat; Kane nailed a memorial to a stanchion by the gangway as his vindication, hoisted the flags and hauled them down, and the party walked once round the brig.

> "I regard the abandonment of the brig as inevitable. We have by actual inspection but thirty-six days' provisions... Scurvy has enfeebled more or less every man in the expedition." ([[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-005]])

**The date is not settled, and this page does not settle it.** Kane's memorial and the signed pledge are dated **20 May 1855**. But Kane's own report counts **eighty-three days** from leaving the *Advance* to reaching Upernavik on 6 August, which puts the departure at **15 May**. Both dates are in the record, both are Kane's, and a question put to a person for a ruling was answered *denied*, so both stand here. The likeliest reconciliation — not a certainty — is that the sick were moved forward to Anoatok in stages during the week before the formal ceremony of the 20th.

The retreat itself is [[artifact:kane-hall-polaris-002]]: two boats and a whaleboat dragged over the ice to open water, then some 1,300 miles rowed and sailed down the Greenland side of Baffin Bay. [[person:Metek]] hauled the sick south on his own sledge in the first stage. They rounded the north Greenland coast near Horse's Head on 3 August and came into [[place:Upernavik]] on the 6th.

> "We drank coffee that night before many a hospitable threshold, and listened again and again to the hymn of welcome, which, sung by many voices, greeted our deliverance."

One man, Christian Ohlsen, died on the way. Every other man came through — an outcome with almost no parallel in the period, and one that Kane owed to Inughuit food and Inughuit sledges at least as much as to his own discipline. He sailed from Upernavik in the Danish barque *Mariane* on 6 September 1855, carrying the boat *Faith*: "Except the furs on our backs, and the documents that recorded our labors and our trials, it was all we brought back of the *Advance* and her fortunes."

Kane died at Havana on 16 February 1857, aged thirty-six, less than a year after [[source:kane1856arctic|Arctic Explorations]] appeared. His funeral train from New Orleans to Philadelphia was the largest American public mourning between Washington's and Lincoln's. The *Advance* was never seen again.
