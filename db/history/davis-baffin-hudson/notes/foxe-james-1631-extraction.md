# The Rival Voyages of 1631: Luke Foxe and Thomas James

Source: Miller Christy, ed., *The Voyages of Captain Luke Foxe of Hull and Captain Thomas James of Bristol, in Search of a North-West Passage, in 1631-32* (Hakluyt Society, 1894), Vol. I. Local file: `db/history/davis-baffin-hudson/files/davis-baffin-hudson-christy1894-foxe-james-v1.txt`.

**Source-critical note**: Volume I of this edition prints Christy's long biographical/historical Introduction (pp. liv-ccxxii) plus a reprint of the earlier voyages (Frobisher through Hawkridge, to p. 248) from Foxe's 1635 book *North-West Fox*. The Contents page states explicitly: "For conclusion of 'North-West Foxe', see Volume II" — meaning **the actual day-by-day text of Foxe's 1631 voyage and the whole of James's narrative are NOT in this file**; they are in Volume II. Everything below about the 1631-32 voyages themselves is therefore drawn from Christy's Introduction, which narrates both voyages in detail and quotes Foxe's and James's own words at many points, citing the original 1635/1633 page numbers. Page citations below give the Introduction's own (Roman-numeral) page as it appears in this OCR file; where Christy also cites the original edition's page number that is given in brackets.

## Roster of named people

**Aboard the Charles (Foxe's pinnace), 1631** — crew of 20 men and 2 boys (p. lxxxvi):
- **Luke Foxe** — Captain and Pilot. Born Hull, 20 Oct 1586; son of Richard Foxe, mariner; a Hull burgess since 1607; "sea-bred from my boyes-time," self-taught in navigation. Died at Whitby c. 15 July 1635, buried 20 July 1635.
- **?Dune** — Master
- **?Yourin (or Urin)** — Master's Mate
- (unnamed) — Chirurgeon
- **Andrew Hume**, **Peter Nesfield** — Quartermasters
- (unnamed) — Boatswain
- **John Coatesworth** — Boatswain's Mate
- (unnamed) — Carpenter, Gunner, Cook
- **?Russell** — "Exposer"
- "My Boy" — a boy, per Foxe's own MS journal
- Foxe says his crew was chosen for him by the officials of Trinity House (at the apparent instigation of his patron Sir John Wolstenholme), against his own preference in at least the case of Master and Master's Mate, with whom his relations were poor.

**Aboard the Henrietta Maria (called "the Maria" by Christy; Bristol's own 1631 letter calls the intended ship "the Mary"), James's ship, 1631-32** — crew of 22 (p. clv):
- **Thomas James** — Captain. Probably born 1595, of a Bristol/South Wales family, likely a nephew of Alderman Thomas of Bristol (d. c.1619); unlike Foxe, of good family and well educated.
- **William Clements** — Lieutenant
- **Arthur Price** — Master
- **John Warden** — Master's Chief Mate (**died 6 May 1632**)
- **John Whittered** — Master's Second Mate (became Chief Mate after Warden's death)
- **Nathaniel Bilson** — Chirurgeon
- **John Palmer** — Boatswain
- **Richard Edwards** — Gunner (lost his leg 20 Aug 1631; **died 22 Nov 1631**)
- **John Barton** — Gunner's Mate (**drowned 17 Oct 1631**)
- **William Cole** — Carpenter (**died 18 May 1632**)
- **George Ugganes**, **David Hammon** — named among the Seamen
- Cooper, Cockswain, Baker, Brewer, Butcher, three further Seamen, two Boys — names not recorded

**Promoters and background figures**:
- **Sir Thomas Roe** — diplomat, chief London promoter of Foxe's voyage; also corresponded with the Bristol backers of James; supplied Foxe with the manuscript abstract of Button's lost journal (see companion note); wrote a sharp letter of 25 Nov 1631 to the Bristol Merchant Venturers assessing both captains' results.
- **Sir John Wolstenholme (the elder)**, Custom-House "farmer," principal London backer, contributed at least £400 to Foxe's voyage and, by Foxe's estimate, at least £1,100 to Arctic discovery over his lifetime across nine voyages; jointly charged (with Roe) by the King to "expediate" Foxe's voyage.
- **Sir John Wolstenholme the Younger** — appointed Treasurer of Foxe's expedition.
- **Society of Merchant Venturers of Bristol** — backers of James; named "husbands and stewards" for the voyage: **Humfrey Hooke** (Master of the Society), **Andrew Charlton**, **Miles Jackson**, **Thomas Cole**.
- **King Charles I** — lent the Charles, a King's pinnace, to the London Adventurers; both captains received royal letters of instruction.

## Luke Foxe in the Charles, 1631

- The Charles was "a pinnace of seventy or eighty tons burden" (p. lxxxvi), a "cast ship" the Navy had been about to sell, lent to the Adventurers; her tonnage is uncertain and elsewhere given as about 150 tons in an Admiralty entry of Aug 1630.
- Foxe had petitioned for a ship as early as 1630 but red tape delayed him a full year; he finally **set sail from Deptford on 5 May 1631**, proceeding by way of the Orkneys.
- **22 June 1631**: entered Hudson's Strait, keeping near the north shore; ice was troublesome but not seriously damaging (he broke his cutwater on ice near Salisbury Island on 12 July — "the first harm, and all I received [on my whole voyage]").
- **10 July**: reached Salisbury Island, beset by ice.
- **17 July**: passed Mansell's Island.
- **21 July**: passed Cary's Swan's Nest, turning north-west.
- **27 July**: reached an island off Cape Fullerton which he named **Sir Thomas Roe's Welcome** (the name has since attached to the strait, not the island).
- Instead of pushing north up that strait, he turned south (probably per instructions) down the west shore of Hudson Bay — "then called Button's [Bay]; now Hudson's" — the coast Button had discovered and sailed in 1612-13, "to search [for] the Passage diligently all the Bay about until I come to Hudson's [i.e., James's] Bay."
- **5 August**: in Churchill Bay, which he calls **"Hubart's Hope,"** where both he and James separately hoped to find a westward passage.
- **8-9 August**: reached and entered **Port Nelson**, remaining until the 20th to overhaul his ship, assemble a knock-down pinnace, and search for a new mainyard. Here he found extensive relics of Button's 1612-13 wintering, including an inscribed cross and half an inscribed board left by Button (see companion Button note) — Button had wintered here 19 years earlier almost to the week.
- **27 August**: declared, in his journal, that all hope of finding the passage on this shore was at an end.
- **29 August**: sighted Captain James's ship. The two captains met and exchanged visits — see below.
- **31 August**: the two parted; Foxe continued east.
- **2 September**: reached Cape Henrietta Maria, which he named **Wolstenholme's Ultimum Vale** (a dig at his patron, since he believed — correctly — that Wolstenholme would spend no more money searching this bay). James, arriving the same day from the west, named the same cape after the Queen; the two men did not meet here.
- Having "joined his own discoveries with those of Hudson," and with time and no further instructions to search elsewhere, Foxe turned north to explore the then-unknown channel north of Nottingham Island.
- **7 September**: sighted the south part of Southampton Island.
- **7 September** (his journal): first mention of sickness — "Now the Master and three men more are downe. God better it!"
- **15 September**: landed on Mill Island.
- Continuing north up what is now called **Foxe Channel**, along the west side of what is now called **Fox Land** — surpassing the exploration Bylot and Baffin had begun here in 1615 — sickness continued to spread among his crew.
- **22 September 1631**: reached his most northerly point, **lat. 66° 47' N., long. 76° 55' W.** (per the table of place-names Christy compiles, p. lxxxiii), naming it **"Foxe's Farthest."** He turned south on account of his men's sickness. (This latitude corresponds to the entrance of what is now called Foxe Basin, the body of water named after him.)
- **28 September**: eased rationing — "appointed 4 beefe dayes in the week" — now homeward-bound.
- **1 October**: "The Lord, for his mercy sake, look upon us, for we are all in weak case."
- **8 October**: "Our sick men are as yet able to do nothing."
- **15 October**: passed Cape Chidleigh (Chidley), clear of the ice, into the open ocean.
- **31 October 1631**: "I came into the Downes with all my men recovered and sound, not having lost one man nor boy, nor any manner of tackling, having been forth neere six months. All glory be to God!" — the closing words of his printed narrative. Every man survived; **no one wintered ashore.**
- Foxe was criticized on his return by James's partisans for turning back the same year rather than pressing on or wintering; he defended himself in an appendix, "The benefit ensuing by my coming home this year." Sir Thomas Roe's letter of 25 November 1631 to the Bristol merchants, assessing the Master and Mate's report of Foxe's voyage, is sharply critical of them (though not, on reflection, fair to Foxe — he had followed his instructions) and states of the tide near Nottingham Island: "I never knew men seeke a North-west passage on a North-east shore."

## Thomas James in the Henrietta Maria, 1631-32

- Bristol's Merchant Venturers had "already provided a meete shipp for Captaine James, of Fourscore tons burthen," which their own letter of 26 Feb 1630-31 says they intended to name the **Mary**; the ship is elsewhere named the **Henrietta Maria** (or "Maria") throughout — Christy uses "Maria" as shorthand. James himself chose "a well-conditioned, strong ship... of the burthen of seventy tons," provisioned for eighteen months, and picked a crew of 22 — deliberately excluding, like Foxe, any man with prior Arctic experience, though the merchants had advised otherwise.
- Preparations complete by **1 April 1631**; James travelled to London, received the King's Instructions via Sir Thomas Roe, and **set sail from Bristol on 3 May 1631** — two days before Foxe left Deptford.
- **4 June**: Greenland sighted; first ice damage to the ship.
- **17 June**: Resolution Island sighted, entering Hudson's Strait; several days of extreme danger from ice followed.
- **24 June**: got free of the ice.
- **15 July**: between Digges Island and Nottingham Island; finding it impossible to search north-west from there because of ice, turned W.S.W.
- **16 July**: reached Mansell Island.
- **11 August**: reached the western shore of the bay, "nearly three degrees and a half" south of the latitude (about 63°N) he had aimed for.
- Two days later, ran onto rocks (probably Nelson Shoal), escaping after a time of extreme peril.
- **17 August**: passed Port Nelson — where Foxe was, at that very moment, refitting his pinnace ashore, though neither yet knew the other was there.
- **20 August**: an accident with a rope fouling the capstan injured several of his men, one of them (the gunner, Richard Edwards) fatally in the long run.
- **29 August 1631**: sighted Foxe's Charles; the captains met and exchanged visits (see below). James tried to persuade Foxe to winter with him in the bay; Foxe declined.
- **2 September**: reached and named **Cape Henrietta Maria**, after the Queen — the same day Foxe reached the same cape from the other direction and named it differently; they did not meet here.
- September was spent cruising the many islands of what is now James Bay, running aground more than once.
- **Early October 1631**: found himself off **Charlton Island** and, overtaken by winter, resolved to stay. He stripped the ship, landed most of the provisions, and built three houses ashore.
- **17 October**: John Barton, gunner's mate, drowned.
- **22 October**: Richard Edwards, the gunner injured in the capstan accident of 20 August, died.
- **29 October 1631**: unable to secure the ship any other way, James **deliberately sank her** at Charlton Island to preserve her hull over the winter — an extraordinary expedient, in Christy's words.
- The winter of 1631-32 is described by James "in great detail" as "a harrowing, but interesting, story"; much of it was spent building a pinnace for the return voyage in case the ship could not be raised again.
- **April 1632**: efforts to raise the sunken ship began, and succeeded.
- **6 May 1632**: John Warden, chief mate, died.
- **18 May 1632**: William Cole, carpenter, died.
- June was spent refitting; on **25 June**, a man set fire to the forest in which they had wintered, and James himself, up a tree, narrowly escaped burning along with many of their effects; mosquitoes plagued the party badly.
- **2 July 1632**: set sail from Charlton Island.
- **23 July**: reached Cape Henrietta Maria again, after great difficulty with ice.
- Detained by ice until **7 August**, then coasted west, almost constantly beset.
- **22 August**: reached Cary's Swan's Nest, and turned north up the channel — unknown to James — that Foxe had explored the autumn before, reaching, he claimed, **at least lat. 65° 30' N.** — about a degree and a quarter south of Foxe's Farthest — before turning for home on account of ice.
- **3 September**: passed Resolution Island.
- **22 October 1632**: **arrived at Bristol.**
- James's own account of his hardships, published in 1633 as *The Strange and Dangerous Voyage of Captaine Thomas James*, is treated at length in this volume's Introduction (pp. clxxxi-cxc) as a likely literary source, alongside other influences, for **Coleridge's "Rime of the Ancient Mariner."** This is presented as a claim made by specific named people, not as settled fact: it was "first pointed out," per Christy, by **J. F. Nicholls**, Librarian to the City of Bristol, in his *Bristol Biographies* (1870), and was investigated "very minutely" by **Ivor James**, of University College, Cardiff, in a dedicated pamphlet, *The Source of "The Ancient Mariner"* (Cardiff, 1890). Christy's own view, after weighing the arguments: James's narrative "undoubtedly formed an important source" — "perhaps the chief source, but certainly only one among several" — of Coleridge's poem, not its sole origin.

## The meeting of the two captains, 29-30 August 1631

Christy's Introduction narrates the meeting itself in summary rather than by direct quotation (the anecdote of Foxe's mocking account of James's dinner — which Christy cites to "p. 358" and "p. 360" of Foxe's own text — belongs to the body of Foxe's 1631 narrative, which is printed in **Volume II**, not present in this file, so it cannot be verified or quoted here):

> "On the 29th, Captain Foxe in the Charles was sighted, and visits were exchanged... James endeavoured to persuade Foxe to winter in the Bay with him, but Foxe wisely declined." (Introduction, p. cxxxviii [ref. Foxe original ed. p. 358]) "...Foxe made a number of contemptuous and ill-mannered remarks about his host (see p. 360). They parted on the 31st..." (Introduction, p. c-ci)

Sir Thomas Roe's letter of 25 November 1631 gives the merchants' understanding of the encounter at second hand: "They meete and left him about fiftie-and-seaven degrees, about Port Nelson, resolued to winter there, and desirous that they would stay with him." Roe goes on to hope everything now rests on James, who "will attempt it next yeere."

## The upshot: the search through Hudson Bay effectively ends for a century

Christy states this directly of Foxe's return: his "newes" that no passage existed through the bay "was not sufficiently promising to induce anyone to send out any other expedition until nearly a century later, when the search was resumed by the Hudson's Bay Company" (Introduction, p. cviii). After 1631-32, serious English search for a North-West Passage through Hudson Bay lapsed until the Hudson's Bay Company voyages of Christopher Middleton and William Moor in 1741-42 and Moor and Smith in 1746-47 — attempts Christy notes elsewhere "did not meet with approval" and "without any great result" (p. 182, note).

## Verbatim quotations (verified by direct grep against the source file)

All eight quotations below were confirmed present, word-for-word as transcribed (OCR spelling/spacing preserved), by grepping the exact string out of `davis-baffin-hudson-christy1894-foxe-james-v1.txt`. None were dropped — all eight passed verification.

1. **Foxe, on choosing his crew** (before departure, spring 1631; Deptford, ~51.48N -0.02W): "godley conversation and such as (their yeares of time not exceeding 35) had gained good experience" — Introduction p. lxxxvi.
2. **James, on his crew of 22** (preparations, spring 1631; Bristol, 51.45N -2.60W): "a small number to performe such a businesse, yet double sufficient to sayle the ship." — Introduction p. clv [orig. ed. p. 457].
3. **Foxe, mid-voyage, on sickness aboard** (7 September 1631; Foxe Channel, inferred ~64.0N -78.0W): "Now the Master and three men more are downe. God better it!" — Introduction p. ciii.
4. **Foxe, homeward-bound** (1 October 1631; Hudson Strait, inferred ~63.0N -71.0W): "The Lord, for his mercy sake, look upon us, for we are all in weak case." — Introduction p. ciii-civ.
5. **Foxe, closing words of his 1631 narrative** (31 October 1631; the Downs, 51.2N 1.4E): "I came into the Downes with all my men recovered and sound, not having lost one man nor boy, nor any manner of tackling, having been forth neere six months. All glory be to God!" — Introduction p. civ.
6. **Sir Thomas Roe, on the Master and Mate's northward search** (letter dated 25 November 1631; London, 51.5N -0.1W): "I never knew men seeke a North-west passage on a North-east shore" — Introduction p. cvi.
7. **James, letter left at Charlton Island** (winter 1631-32; Charlton Island, 52.0N -79.25W): "by my directions, I was to search especially two places — one from Digges Island to the northwards, and, failing there, to go to the Checks and Hubbert's Hope, and so to search it to the Southward." — Introduction p. clx.
8. **J. F. Nicholls, on the Coleridge connection** (published 1870; Bristol, 51.45N -2.60W): "It is very likely indeed that S. T. Coleridge, who was a regular and daily frequenter of our old City Library, derived his marrow-chilling scenes, depicted in that unique and immortal poem, The Ancient Mariner, from Captain James's Strange and Dangerous Voyage" — Introduction p. clxxxiv, quoting Nicholls's *Bristol Biographies*, No. 2, p. 76.

No quotations were dropped for failing verification; all candidates checked were confirmed in the source text. (Note: an additional candidate passage — Roe's remark that James was "resolued to loose his life or returne with more honour" — was located and is genuine, but its OCR rendering ("rcsolued... rcturnc... hce") is degraded enough that it is reported here only in paraphrase rather than presented as a clean verbatim quotation.)

## Unresolved / not found in this volume

- The full text of Foxe's own 1631 log (with his sardonic asides in full) and the whole of James's 1633 narrative are in Volume II, not available here; only Christy's summaries and selective quotations could be used.
- No verbatim text of "Foxe's mocking account of James's dinner" could be located or verified in this file; Christy cites it to pages of Foxe's original text (pp. 358, 360) that are not reproduced in this volume.
- Exact coordinates for several waypoints (the meeting point with James, Cary's Swan's Nest, Hubart's/Hubbart's Hope as a point on a modern map) are not given as numbers in the source and have been inferred from named-place identifications in Christy's footnotes.
