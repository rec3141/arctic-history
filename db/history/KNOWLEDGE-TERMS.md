# Reuse terms for Inuit and Inughuit knowledge recorded by community projects

A survey of the terms — consent, ownership, reuse, citation, credit — carried by
every community-knowledge document this layer holds or wants, and of the
protocols the owner asked about. Made in answer to requests **#64**, **#66** and
**#69**. Compiled 10 September 2026; documents fetched once and cached in
`knowledge-terms-scratch/`.

**This run read terms only.** No observation, quotation or image from any of
these documents was entered in the database.

Every licence code and credit line below is a **recommendation for the owner to
accept or change**. Nothing has been changed in the database on the strength of
this report.

---

## 1. Summary for the owner

### Which documents carry an open licence

**None of them.** Not one community-knowledge document surveyed carries a
Creative Commons, Crown, Open Government or other open licence. The two
non-community documents in the list do carry terms: the Canadian Museum of
Nature grants non-commercial personal use with attribution, and the Nunavut
Wildlife Harvest Study's data-sharing regime is set by the Nunavut Agreement
rather than by copyright.

### Which carry an explicit request to ask

- **Inuit Bowhead Knowledge Study (NWMB 2000)** — transcripts released only
  "pending written approval from the HTO for that community" (p. 8); the
  Inuktitut glossary and the place names "must await verification by Inuit
  elders" (p. 8).
- **Arctic Eider Society SIKU** — the platform's Terms of Use forbid reusing any
  user content without the author's explicit permission, and name quotation in
  research work product as a prohibited use.
- **Inuit Siku Atlas (ISIUOP, Laidler/Aporta, now hosted by Carleton's GCRC)** —
  the atlas's own rights metadata: "Materials are under copyright. Contact
  admin@gcrc.carleton.ca".
- **Inuit Heritage Trust** — "Please contact us to access our most up-to-date
  place names data."
- **Qikiqtani Truth Commission (2024 editions)** — reproduction of any part
  "without written consent of the publisher, is an infringement of copyright
  law". **This contradicts twelve source rows in the database that carry
  `open-access`; see §11.**
- **Canadian Museum of Nature** — non-commercial personal use only, with
  conditions; "All other uses are prohibited without the express written
  authorization of the museum".

### Which are silent

- **NWMB as a publisher.** The bowhead study carries a bare "©Nunavut Wildlife
  Management Board" and no terms of use; the Nunavut Wildlife Harvest Study
  final report carries **no copyright statement at all**; nwmb.com's footer
  "Copyright" link is dead and its Disclaimer says nothing about reuse.
- **Pikialasorsuaq Commission, *People of the Ice Bridge*** — "© Inuit
  Circumpolar Council Canada … 2017" and nothing else.
- **Unikkaaqatigiit (ITK 2005)** — no reuse terms, but a prescribed citation
  that names the three communities as first authors.
- **Igloolik Oral History Project** — no published terms anywhere reachable;
  the collection is held by Nunavut Arctic College and access to the archive is
  by appointment.
- **Nunavut Arctic College *Interviewing Inuit Elders* / *Inuit Perspectives***
  — the publisher states no rights terms; the volumes are sold in print.

### What the protocols ask of *this* project

This project is a research tool aboard a government ship, non-commercial, citing
published works. On that description:

1. **No Nunavut research licence is required.** The Nunavut Research Institute's
   own guide: "Secondary research, based exclusively on the consolidation,
   review, and synthesis of existing published information, is not generally
   licensed under the Act" (p. 4). A licence *would* be required the moment
   anyone on this project interviewed a Nunavummiut, by phone or online, or
   worked from archived personal materials.
2. **Credit is not optional.** The ITK/NRI guide names "lack of recognition or
   compensation" as a standing Inuit criticism and asks that researchers "Give
   credit to community members who have worked on the project and whose
   knowledge or information shared may form the basis of the study. This
   includes citations or potential co-authorship" (p. 8). Every observation
   drawn from these documents should name the elder or hunter, their community,
   and the project that recorded them — not just the report's editor.
3. **ITK's National Inuit Strategy on Research** asserts Inuit ownership and
   control over Inuit Nunangat data and names Inuit representational
   organisations as "the rightful gatekeepers" (p. 12). It does not carve out an
   exception for secondary use of published reports, and it says consent "should
   be sought by researchers, governments, and research institutions by engaging
   Inuit representational organizations" (p. 11).
4. **The QIA does not publish a standalone IQ protocol or research guideline.**
   What QIA does publish that binds a project like this is in its Inuit Impact
   and Benefit Agreements (§16) and in the copyright terms of the QTC reports.
5. **If the layer were published more widely** — beyond the ship, or with any
   cost recovery — the picture changes materially for every document in §2-§13:
   the QTC terms, the museum terms and the SIKU terms are each breached by
   republication, and the NISR asks for the consent of the regional organisation
   (QIA for Qikiqtaaluk, ICC Canada for the Pikialasorsuaq material). See §17 and §21.

### Recommended posture, in one line

Facts and observations may be entered now with the knowledge-holder named as
observer; verbatim quotation and images should wait for a short permission
letter to five addresses (NWMB, ICC Canada, ITK, GCRC Carleton, QIA), which
would clear almost everything at once.

## 2. Final Report of the Inuit Bowhead Knowledge Study (NWMB, March 2000)

- bibkey `hay2000bowhead`; held at `db/history/whales-and-narwhal/files/whales-and-narwhal-hay2000-bowhead-knowledge-study.pdf`
- https://www.nwmb.com/en/publications/bowhead-knowledge-study/1819-bowhead-knowledge-study-eng/file

**Copyright statement** (title-page verso, unnumbered; PDF p. 3):

> "©Nunavut Wildlife Management Board
> March, 2000"

and on the same page:

> "Cover photo: Glenn Williams/Ursus
> Illustration on cover, inside of cover, title page, dedication page, and used
> as a report motif: "Arvanniaqtut (Whale Hunters)", sc 1986, Simeonie Kopapik,
> Cape Dorset Print Collection."

There is **no terms-of-use statement, and no statement of what others may do
with the report.**

**Authorship — the communities are named as the authors** (title page,
unnumbered; PDF p. 1):

> "By Inuit Study Participants from: Arctic Bay, Arviat, Cape Dorset,
> Chesterfield Inlet, Clyde River, Coral Harbour, Grise Fiord, Hall Beach,
> Igloolik, Iqaluit, Kimmirut, Kugaaruk, Pangnirtung, Pond Inlet, Qikiqtarjuaq,
> Rankin Inlet, Repulse Bay, and Whale Cove"

**Dedication** (p. ii):

> "The Final Report of the Inuit Bowhead Knowledge Study is dedicated to our
> ancestors and elders, whose knowledge, wisdom, and spirit continue to guide
> and inform us. This report is also dedicated to the memory of the elders who
> participated in this Study and have since passed away."

**Consent, and the choice each speaker made** (Appendix VI, p. 87):

> "I have been fully informed of the objectives of this Study being conducted by
> the Nunavut Wildlife Management Board. I understand these objectives and
> consent to being interviewed for this Study, for the Inuit Bowhead Knowledge
> Study Committee."

> "Consent to be interviewed and tape-recorded"

> "I desire that my identity and the information I provide be confidential.
> OR
> I desire that my identity be non-confidential and that the information I
> provide be attributed to me."

**How that choice appears in the text** (p. 4, and p. 6):

> "the beginning of the interview whether or not he/she wanted his/her identity
> to be confidential, and signed a consent form to that effect (Appendix VI)."

> "Anon. (n) indicates an informant who wished to be anonymous (these informants
> were assigned an identification number, n)."

> "Quotations in this report and in the 'Source Book of Quotations' are
> referenced by quotation number, name and community code of the informant" (p. 7)

**The explicit request to ask — the key passage for request #64** (p. 8):

> "Transcripts of the interviews or workshops for any community are available
> upon request from the NWMB, pending written approval from the HTO for that
> community."

> "A preliminary glossary of more than 300 words has been assembled, but its
> release or publication must await verification by Inuit elders. Similarly, the
> collection of Inuit place names (from the 1996 interviews and the eight
> community workshops) must be verified,"

That second passage bears directly on the `inuit-knowledge-of-nature` topic: the
study's own compilers held back the Inuktitut vocabulary and the place names
pending verification by elders. **This layer should not publish an Inuktitut
sea-ice or wildlife vocabulary drawn from this study that the study itself
withheld.**

**Ownership of the knowledge** (Acknowledgements, p. 78):

> "Very special thanks go out to all Inuit who were interviewed or participated
> in the workshops, for sharing their personal knowledge, life experiences, and
> wisdom, which form the basis of this report. We owe a special debt of gratitude
> and respect to all Inuit who participated in the Study. Many if not most of
> them are named in this report. This is their report, and the Study Coordinator
> and the IBKS Committee assume responsibility for any errors of fact, omission,
> or interpretation."

**Recommended credit line**
> Inuit Bowhead Knowledge Study, Nunavut Wildlife Management Board, 2000.
> Recorded from [name], [community], by the Inuit Bowhead Knowledge Study.
> "This is their report" (p. 78).

**Recommended licence code:** `fair-dealing` for short quotation, with a
rights-note naming the study committee (David Aglukark, David Igutsaq, Joannie
Ikkidluak, Meeka Mike) and the eighteen communities — **but** a one-page letter
to the NWMB, copied to the relevant HTOs, would upgrade this to `permission` and
is the course the report's own p. 8 invites. Do not enter the Inuktitut glossary
or place names from this study under any code.

## 3. The Nunavut Wildlife Harvest Study — Final Report (NWMB, February 2004)

- Not yet a source row. Cached at `knowledge-terms-scratch/nwmb-harvest-2004.pdf`
- https://www.nwmb.com/en/publications/harvest-study/1824-156-nwhs-report-2004-156-0003/file

**There is no copyright statement of any kind in this report.** A search of the
whole PDF finds no "©", no "Copyright", no "All rights", no ISBN.

**What governs it instead is the Nunavut Agreement**, quoted in the report at
§2.4 "Privacy and access policies" (p. 14):

> "raw and interpreted data produced from the Study shall be fully and freely
> available to the Government of Canada, the Territorial Government and Inuit"
> (NLCA, 1993, Article 5.4.6)

> "The NWMB shall ensure that the names of individual harvesters are not
> revealed when making available data pursuant to Section 5.4.6" (NLCA, 1993,
> Article 5.4.7)

> "Without the prior written permission of the DIO and affected individuals,
> evidence obtained through the Study relating to an individual shall not be
> admissible in any proceeding where the individual may be held civilly or
> criminally liable" (NLCA, 1993, Article 5.4.8)

**How the anonymity was actually engineered** (p. 15):

> "In order to fulfill obligations pursuant to Article 5.4.7 of the NLCA relating
> to the release of any names of individual harvesters, the Harvest Study
> database was designed in such a way that any harvest data, whether it be in
> hard-copy form (datasheet) or within a computerized table, referred to hunters
> only by alpha-numeric code, not by name."

> "Individual harvest data were never released unless written permission was
> received from the hunter."

**Access to anything beyond the published report** (p. 15):

> "In the interim, any readers wishing access to data not provided in this report
> may apply to NWMB with their request. Fulfillment of each data request will be
> at the discretion of the NWMB and may be dependent on factors such as staff
> availability and the complexity of the request and will be decided on a
> case-by-case basis."

**Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit inside the study** (p. 14):

> "An especially important component of existing data is Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit
> - Inuit traditional ecological knowledge (TEK). … The collection of place name
> information has created a Nunavut–wide repository of traditional place names
> and their approximate geographic locations. Hunters were also asked to provide
> the Fieldworkers with comments on the general health and abundance of wildlife
> during the monthly interviews. This information was recorded in the comments
> field of the harvest data table and although it is not analyzed or summarized
> in this report, it is available for reference by interested individuals."

**Credit to the harvesters** (§1.3 Acknowledgements, p. 9):

> "It must be stressed however, that the success of the Harvest Study was
> directly related to its participants. The harvesters of Nunavut, who took part
> in the Harvest Study by sharing their data every month for five years, deserve
> great praise and recognition for their part in this important study."

**Recommended credit line**
> The Nunavut Wildlife Harvest Study, Nunavut Wildlife Management Board, 2004
> (Heather Priest and Peter J. Usher). Compiled from the monthly returns of
> Nunavut harvesters, who are named in the report by community, not individually
> against their data.

**Recommended licence code:** `crown-open` is *not* right (the NWMB is an
institution of public government under the Nunavut Agreement, not a Crown
publisher, and states no Open Government terms). Recommend `fair-dealing` for
quotation. The harvest *numbers* themselves are facts and may be entered as
observations with the study cited; the unpublished place-name repository and the
comments field are not held here and should not be sought without a request to
the NWMB.

## 4. NWMB community Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit reports and the nwmb.com site

- https://www.nwmb.com/en/publications and https://www.nwmb.com/en/lang/2-uncategorised/126-disclaimer

The publications page lists folders for Annual Reports, Bowhead Knowledge Study,
Harvest Study, Southeast Baffin Beluga, and Wildlife of Nunavut Books. The site
footer carries a link labelled "Copyright" whose href is `#` — **it goes
nowhere, and no copyright or terms-of-use page exists on the site.**

The Disclaimer page is the only terms document, and it is about accuracy and
liability, not reuse. Its whole statement of user obligation is:

> "The use of the NWMB's website indicates your unconditional acceptance of the
> above disclaimers and statements. You further agree that the NWMB and its Staff
> have no liability for direct, indirect, incidental, punitive, or consequential
> damages with respect to the information, services, or content contained on or
> otherwise accessed through this web site."

**There is no statement about copyright, consent, ownership, reuse, citation or
credit anywhere on nwmb.com.** The NWMB does publish a "NWMB Media Request Form"
(`/images/stories/NWMB_Media_Request_Form.pdf`), which is the address for an ask.

**Recommended licence code:** per document, not per site. Treat each NWMB report
on its own front matter; default `fair-dealing`.

speaker named; `not-cleared` for the photographs, which carry individual
photographers' credits and no licence. A permission note to ICC Canada would

- bibkey `iohp2026igloolik`; entered `not-cleared`; no file held

**No terms of use, copyright page or reuse statement for the IOHP could be found

> acquisition from other archives. NAC holds the Igloolik Oral History Project

The site footer reads "© Nunavut Arctic College".

Anijaarniq — a Nunavut Arctic College site built **entirely** from IOHP

> "All of the information you see here comes from tape-recorded interviews
> collected by the Igloolik Oral History Project. Igloolik Inuit elders working

> "The Inuktitut audio interviews used throughout derive from the archives of
> Igloolik Oral History Project, an ongoing project started in 1986 by the
> Inullariit Elders Society and the Igloolik Research Centre."

That is the elders' own stated purpose, and it is narrower than open reuse: a
reservoir **for future generations of Iglulingmiut**. The Anijaarniq site carries

**Recommended credit line**
> Igloolik Oral History Project (Inullariit Elders Society and the Igloolik
> Research Centre, from 1986), interview IE-[n], [elder's name]; collection held
> by Nunavut Arctic College.

**Recommended licence code:** leave at `not-cleared`. This is the document in
the whole survey where a person, not a licence, is the only route: the terms are
the elders' society's to state and they have not stated them in public. If the
owner wants this material, the ask is to Nunavut Arctic College Media (which
holds the collection) and to the Inullariit Society, and it should be a real
letter, not a form.

## 9. Arctic Eider Society — the SIKU platform

- Not a source row. https://siku.org/terms (cached `knowledge-terms-scratch/siku-terms.html`)

SIKU has the **most explicit and most restrictive** terms in this survey, and
they are restrictive *in favour of the knowledge holders*. From the Terms of Use
page (a single web page; no printed pagination):

The four headline principles at the top of the page:

> "Intellectual Property"
> "Never use others' data without permission."
> "You own your data. Nobody else does – not even SIKU."
> "You control how you share your data."
> "Projects must follow all permits, licensing and Indigenous research policies."

The framework, stated at length:

> "Intellectual property ownership maintained through an informed data
> stewardship framework."

> "Ownership and control by users over stewardship and sharing of knowledge and
> data is a core part of the Platform's framework. In contrast to most platforms,
> sharing data and content on the Platform does not provide SIKU with a license
> to otherwise use that content, and SIKU and The Arctic Eider Society are
> required to acquire permissions for use in the same way as any other
> individual, project or organization using the Platform. Appropriate permissions
> must be obtained from rights holders (e.g. users, communities, projects and
> organizations) before any content shared on the Platform can be used in any
> other context. Sharing knowledge and hunting stories does not imply legal
> permission for others to use or replicate that information in any way. Posting
> a photo, data or other content does not imply others can repost or otherwise
> use that content, and explicit permission must be obtained from the owner of
> the content along with any other requirements (permits, ethics, etc.) from the
> region."

> "You retain all intellectual property rights in and to any User Content you
> post, upload or otherwise make available through The Platform, including the
> copyright in and to any User Content. SIKU does not claim any ownership, right,
> title or interest in and to your User Content."

**The prohibition that decides the matter for this layer.** Under "Users shall
not under any circumstances by any means or for any purpose whatsoever, except
as otherwise expressly permitted by the User who authored/created the User
Content or expressly provided in these Terms of Use":

> "provide, transmit or distribute printed or electronic copies of any User
> Content to any other person;"

> "quote, customize and incorporate User Content, in whole or part, in documents,
> memoranda, articles and other work product in connection with research or
> educational activities; or"

> "modify any such User Content."

Quoting a SIKU post in a research tool is named, in terms, as a prohibited use
absent the author's permission. The platform's own purpose statement explains
why:

> "It is a subtle but important distinction that the Platform is not a citizen
> science tool (citizens providing data to scientists) but rather is a tool to
> empower Indigenous self-determination (Indigenous peoples collecting data for
> Indigenous peoples and maintaining control over data stewardship)."

**Recommended credit line:** not applicable — nothing should be taken.

**Recommended licence code:** `not-cleared` if a SIKU row is ever created.
SIKU may be **cited as a platform** (that it exists, what it does, who runs it)
from its own About page; **no post, observation, photograph or ice report from
it may be entered**, and the per-post "Open Access Stewardship" setting is not a
general licence — it is currently limited, in SIKU's words, to "limited meta-data
(e.g. species, location)".

## 14. Qikiqtani Inuit Association — the Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit protocol and research guidelines

**Finding: QIA publishes no standalone Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit protocol or
research-guidelines document.** I searched qia.ca's Documents index (which lists
annual reports, benefits-fund reports, newsletters, IIBAs and the Saimaqatigiingniq
Fund reports), the Who We Are pages, and the open web. What QIA does publish that
governs a project like this one is in three places:

**(a) The department that holds the mandate** (https://www.qia.ca/who-we-are/):

> "Department of Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit and Engagement Responsible for
> protecting Inuit rights, advancing Inuit priorities, managing Inuit-led
> research and for using Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit in decision making, research,
> wildlife protection and community engagement"

QIA is also, in its own words, "a Designated Inuit Organization under the Nunavut
Agreement" — which matters, because several of the reuse conditions quoted in
this report (NLCA Article 5.4.8, §3 above) run to the DIO.

**(b) The Qikiqtani Truth Commission copyright terms** (§11), which are QIA's
operative statement about the reuse of Qikiqtani Inuit testimony: written consent
of the publisher, info@qia.ca.

**(c) The Inuit Impact and Benefit Agreements**, which are on qia.ca and which
state, in binding terms, how researchers must handle IQ in the areas they cover.
From the Qausuittuq National Park IIBA
(https://www.qia.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Qausuittuq-IIBA.pdf) — Qausuittuq
being on Bathurst Island, inside this layer's area:

Article 8.1.8 (p. 26):

> "Parks Canada recognizes that Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit and Inuit oral histories
> are of integral importance to the management of the historical and
> archaeological resources of the Park and shall accord significant weight to
> such knowledge in any decisions about these resources."

Article 9.1.2 (p. 28):

> "Parks Canada shall require that researchers establish and maintain an
> effective and mutually beneficial relationship with Inuit and Inuit communities
> throughout the research process."

Article 9.2.2 (p. 28), on what the research strategy must contain:

> "Include principles on how researchers should review any collected and
> published Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit, seek such knowledge from Inuit and
> communicate their findings to Inuit and the QPMC;"

> "include wildlife, ecosystem, cultural and social research components, as well
> as a communication strategy (each of these components shall include Inuit
> Qaujimajatuqangit in the planning and conduct of the research); and"

Article 9.2.3 (p. 28):

> "The Superintendent shall include in permits to researchers such terms and
> conditions as are necessary to secure the researcher's compliance with the
> research strategy, including principles therein."

**What this requires of this project.** Nothing directly: the IIBA binds Parks
Canada and its permit-holders, and this layer holds no research permit. But
9.2.2(c) is the closest thing in print to a QIA rule about *published* IQ — and
it does not say "leave it alone", it says researchers should **review collected
and published Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit** and **communicate their findings back**.
That is a licence to read and a duty to report back, which is a fair description
of what this layer could offer.

**If published more widely:** QIA's own reuse rule (written consent, info@qia.ca)
would need to be satisfied for anything QTC-derived, and the NISR route (§17)
would make QIA the body to ask for the Qikiqtaaluk material generally.

## 16. Nunavut Research Institute — licensing under the Scientists Act

- https://www.nri.nu.ca/research-licencing-nunavut
- https://www.nri.nu.ca/sites/default/files/Licence%20Application%20Guidelines_Dec2021NEW.pdf
  (*Obtaining a Research License under Nunavut's Scientists Act: A Guide for Researchers*, Dec 2021)

**The Act, quoted in the guide** (p. 3):

> "No person shall carry on scientific research in or based on Nunavut, or
> collect specimens in Nunavut for use in scientific research, unless: (a) he or
> she is the holder of a licence issued under this Act; or (b) the research
> consists solely of archaeological work for which a permit has been issued under
> the subsection 51(1) of the Nunavut Act (Canada)."

**Its reach is wider than fieldwork** (pp. 3-4):

> "The Scientists Act explicitly applies to research in or based on Nunavut.
> Licensing under the Act is required both for research activities that take
> place within Nunavut, and for research about Nunavut and/or involving Nunavut
> residents that is conducted from outside the territory. For example, you would
> require a scientific research license to gather information from Nunavut
> residents remotely via phone interviews or online surveys if you intend to use
> this information in research, even if you never actually travel to Nunavut. You
> would also require a research license to conduct research using archived
> biological materials or personal information collected from Nunavut residents
> (such as human blood or hair samples or patient medical chart information) even
> if such materials are stored in a facility outside of Nunavut."

**The exemption that covers this project** (p. 4, and the list at p. 5):

> "A license is required for most types of primary research involving the
> collection and analysis of new (original) information."

> "Secondary research, based exclusively on the consolidation, review, and
> synthesis of existing published information, is not generally licensed under
> the Act."

> "secondary research based entirely on existing publicly available information
> (e.g. systematic literature reviews)"

**Consultation, if a licence ever is needed** (pp. 21-22):

> "Evidence of prior community consultation is a required component of your
> research licence application." (research-licensing page)

> "Consultation with select community groups, Inuit organizations, government
> departments, and any other representative groups that may be impacted by a
> proposed research project."

> "You are free to travel to Nunavut without a research license to meet
> informally with community members and seek feedback on your research plans, or
> to conduct consultations, workshops, or other engagement activities to
> co-design a research project."

**Reporting back** (p. 20):

> "In addition to providing Annual Summary and Final Reports to NRI, you are
> expected to share your research results directly with any community members and
> organizations in Nunavut that participated in or supported your research, and
> you should make research results available to other organizations that may have
> an interest in your study."

**Ownership of data and IQ:** the guide asks applicants to describe "Intellectual
property rights/ownership of data" in the application form (pp. 12, 15) but
**states no NRI policy of its own** on who owns data or traditional knowledge. It
refers researchers instead to TCPS2 Chapter 9 and to the NISR.

**What this requires of this project.** No licence, on the present description —
a research tool built from published works is the secondary research the guide
exempts. **The line is bright and worth writing down: the moment anyone on this
project interviews a Nunavummiut, by phone, email or in person, or works from
archived personal materials, a licence is required before the work begins,** and
the NRI is allowed up to a year to decide.

**If published more widely:** publication does not itself trigger the Act; the
Act follows the *collection* of information, not its dissemination. But NRI's
research-licensing page names the two documents it expects any researcher
working in Nunavut to have read, and they are the ones in §17 and §18:

> "Please ensure that you read and become familiar with the National Inuit
> Strategy on Research (NISR) and with the Tricouncil Policy Statement: Ethical
> Conduct for Research Involving Humans - TCPS2 (2022)"

## 17. Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami — *National Inuit Strategy on Research* (2018)

- https://www.itk.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/ITK_NISR-Report_English_low_res.pdf
- Copyright statement (front matter, unnumbered): "Copyright © Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami, 2018"

(p. 12):

> "Inuit representational organizations are the rightful gatekeepers of Inuit
> Nunangat research and are best positioned to determine how our information
> should be utilized and shared to maximize benefits and minimize harm."

**Consent** (p. 11):

> "Inuit have the right to self-determination, including in the area of research.
> This means that researchers and research institutions must acquire the free,
> prior, and informed consent of Inuit prior to research activity being
> undertaken in our homeland."

> "Consent should be sought by researchers, governments, and research
> institutions by engaging Inuit representational organizations or our appointed
> institutions."

(p. 8):

> "The majority of Inuit Nunangat research is carried out without the consent of
> Inuit, as represented by Inuit representational organizations at regional,
> national, and international levels."

**Publishing without asking** (p. 32):

> "Inuit-specific data is inconsistently shared by researchers who may act
> unilaterally to publish and disseminate data without first seeking the consent
> of Inuit representational organizations or Inuit-appointed institutions."

> "Researchers and their affiliated institutions must partner with Inuit in order
> to seek consent and guidance about the political and social context of research
> and the potential impacts of sharing or publishing results."

**What the strategy says about researchers taking and not returning** (p. 3, from
the President's letter):

> "For far too long, researchers have enjoyed great privilege as they have passed
> through our communities and homeland, using public or academic funding to
> answer their own questions about our environment, wildlife, and people. Many of
> these same researchers then ignore Inuit in creating the outcomes of their work
> for the advancement of their careers, their research institutions, or their
> governments. This type of exploitative relationship must end."

**Access to existing research, in Inuit hands** (p. 21):

> "At the most basic level, Inuit seek access to existing research but do not
> always have access because our institutions cannot afford the expensive search
> engines and platforms that universities and governments enjoy. There is a
> continued need to improve methods for sharing data with Inuit in ways we would
> like to receive it."

The strategy commits ITK to "Develop Inuit-specific guidelines on data
accessibility, ownership, and control" (Action 4.3, p. 32); those guidelines,
if published since, would supersede much of the above and should be checked
before any wider release.

**What this requires of this project.** The NISR is a strategy, not a statute:
it binds nobody by force of law and it does not carve out secondary use of
published reports. Read honestly, it asks three things this project can do
without asking anyone's permission: (1) name the Inuit knowledge holders as the
observers they are, not as sources quoted by a southern editor; (2) do not
present Inuit knowledge as the project's own finding; (3) be ready to give the
material back — the layer is, in NISR terms, exactly the kind of "access to
existing research" p. 21 says Inuit institutions are often denied, and offering
it to QIA or NAC costs nothing.

**If published more widely:** the NISR's answer is unambiguous — seek the free,
prior and informed consent of the representational organisation. For this layer
that means QIA (Qikiqtaaluk), NTI (Nunavut-wide), and ICC Canada for the
Pikialasorsuaq material shared with Greenland.

## 19. Government of Nunavut — the Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit principles
