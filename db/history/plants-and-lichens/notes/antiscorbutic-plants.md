# The plants the searching expeditions gathered against scurvy
Extracted from Armstrong 1857 (Investigator), Belcher 1855, Osborn, Richardson, Ross 1819.

## Armstrong, HMS Investigator, Mercy Bay
- Mid-July: sorrel (given as Rumex domesticus) and scurvy grass (Cochlearia officinalis) in
  considerable abundance; men employed daily gathering sorrel; a small daily allowance to each man
  after the sick were supplied.
- 1 August: "improvement in the general appearance of the men ... the majority expressed themselves
  as feeling generally better than they had done a month previous." (p. 539-540)
- "The Sorrel, when eaten alone, or with the addition of a little vinegar and mustard, forms a most
  agreeable and excellent salad ... When boiled, and eaten as a vegetable, it was a valuable addition
  to our scanty meal, but appeared to me to be less efficacious as an antiscorbutic agent." (p. 540)
- Of the Inuit: "as an antiscorbutic they eat the sorrel, grasses and mosses" (p. 198).
- Appendix: the plants collected by Robert Anderson, surgeon of HMS Enterprise, presented to Kew,
  examined by J. D. Hooker and described in the Journal of the Proceedings of the Linnean Society,
  November 1856. Lists for the coast of North America, Banks Land and Prince Albert Land.

## Belcher, HMS Assistance, Northumberland Sound, 1853
- 1853-01-19: mustard and cress seed sown in nine boxes, in soil taken from the mosses ashore.
- 1853-01-31: first crop issued to the crew, about one ounce to each person.
- to 1853-04-30: 3 lb mustard and 3 lb cress seed consumed, about 30 lb produced.
- 1853-06-20: early York cabbage and spinach sown in a box on Mount Beaufort.
- 1853-06-27: cabbage and spinach began to show.
- 1853-07-10: pulled up on leaving Northumberland Sound, and "filled a large dish".
- summer 1853: about 25 lb more mustard and cress issued to the travelling parties.

## Osborn
- 25 July, Griffith Island: poppies, saxifrage and sorrel in full flower on the sheltered terraces.
- 22-25 August, lat. 76 deg 52 min N: "Scurvy-grass in great abundance, as well as another
  antiscorbutic plant, bearing a small white flower"; also London-pride, poppies, sorrel, dwarf
  willow, crow-feet grass, saxifrage and tripe-de-roche.

## Ross 1819 (Isabella and Alexander) plant list
Cochlearia fenestrata nov. sp.; Salix arctica nov. sp.; Gyrophora hirsuta; Gyrophora erosa;
Cetraria islandica; Cetraria nivalis; Cenomyce rangiferina; Cenomyce fimbriata; Dufourea rugosa
nov. sp.; Cornicularia bicolor; Ulva crispa.

## Richardson
- Herald Island, lat. 71 deg 19 min N, long. 175 deg 23 min W: eight species gathered from the
  terraces - Hepatica, Poa arctica, another grass, Artemisia borealis, Cochlearia fenestrata,
  Saxifraga laurentiana, a moss and a red lichen.
