# Route and dates, British Arctic Expedition 1937-38

Extracted from Haig-Thomas, "Expedition to Ellesmere Island, 1937-38",
*Geographical Journal* 95(4) (1940) 265-274 [HT], the printed discussion
274-277 [D], and Wright, "South-East Ellesmere Island", same issue 278-288 [W].
Coordinates from the Canadian Geographical Names Database except where noted.

## The dating problem (important)
HT, p. 271: "I have been careful to leave out all dates, for the day I started
and the day I returned are the two only accurate dates that I know for
certain. The dates in my diary, which I had kept at each camp, were five days
out." He blames broken sleep in the cold, a watch stopped by a fall on the
ice, and hazy skies. So the western sledge journey has exactly two firm
dates: departure from Etah, 18 March 1938, and arrival at Robertson Bay,
15 May 1938. **Every intermediate waypoint on that track is undated and its
position along the line is inferred from the order of the narrative.**
Wright's survey journey, by contrast, is fully dated, because Hamilton kept
the deck watch on time signals.

## Haig-Thomas and Nukapinguaq, western sledge journey (HT 266-271)
Order of the narrative; only the endpoints dated.
1. Etah 78.314 -72.610 - leave 18 Mar; the MacGregor expedition wintering there
2. mid Smith Sound c. 78.60 -73.60 - pack edge an arc from Rensselaer Bay to Pim Island; first half easy going
3. old polar pack near Bache Peninsula - deep snow, very small daily mileage, mean about -30 F
4. Bache Peninsula police hut 79.13 -76.00 - Wordie's walrus meat; clothes dried at a blubber fire
5. Flagler Bay 79.17 -77.00 - start of the land crossing
6. Flagler-Bay Fiord land crossing c. 79.10 -80.00 - some 40 miles of rocks, musk-oxen, -52 F, detour round a glacier from the southern ice cap
7. Bay Fiord 78.92 -83.50 - smooth ice, reindeer tracks from the west, wolves in pairs, no musk-oxen by the sea
8. junction of Bay Fiord and Eureka Sound c. 78.90 -85.20 - eight bears in one day, two killed; food depot left
9. Eureka Sound, windswept, sledge running free
10. between Ulvingen Island and Axel Heiberg 78.33 -88.20 - five wolves at the tent in the night; Nukapinguaq shoots the bitch
11. Hyperite Point 78.15 -88.88 - third bear killed; Nukapinguaq finds Sergeant Joy's cairn message; depot left
12. unnamed bay, south coast of Axel Heiberg c. 78.15 -90.30 - about 15 miles across and 10 deep, missed by Sverdrup and MacMillan
13. Cape Southwest 78.20 -92.03 - sea ice frozen c. 100 yards up the cliff; depot left; first sight of a low island
14. Cornwall Island 77.62 -94.63 - cairn beside Constable Hamilton's 1932 Krueger-search message
15. south-east corner of Amund Ringnes Island c. 77.85 -95.60 - the "island" proves to be a sandspit; cairn message
16. south-west corner of Amund Ringnes c. 77.90 -97.20 - cross the island north-eastwards
17. east coast tide crack c. 78.20 -95.30 - the island seen ahead, out of the ice
18. Haig-Thomas Island 78.23 -94.50 - climb the highest point in the northern half, build a cairn with a sleigh stick beside a snow-filled gully
19. Cape Southwest again - depot robbed by bears and wolves; bear-skin soup
20. Hyperite Point - over 24 hours' sledging non-stop; depot also robbed; a bear walks up to the igloo and is shot
21. Trold Fiord 78.25 -85.28 - crossed to on foot from Eureka Sound; recognised from 1935
22. south of Bay Fiord - four days searching in vain for the fossil bones Nukapinguaq and Joy saw
23. ice cap south of the Bay Fiord-Flagler crossing, over 4000 ft
24. Bache Peninsula police post; Fram Havn 78.77 -74.72
25. Robertson Bay (Siorapaluup Kangerlua) 77.73 -70.73 - arrive 15 May

Nearly 1500 miles, sixteen dogs, no dog killed, one puppy born on the journey
and brought home. Team averaged 80 lb before and 70 lb after.

## Wright and Hamilton, south-east Ellesmere survey (W 282-288)
Thule 26 Feb - Robertson Bay (delay, difficulty getting a guide) - Etah -
leave Etah with HT in March - part in mid Smith Sound c. 21 Mar - Cape Sabine
78.72 -74.12 - Fram Haven 78.77 -74.72 (disappointing as an advance base: no
coal) - back to the Greenland depot - 3 Apr second crossing, two sledges, six
dogs each, about 500 lb per sledge - Cape Dunsterville 77.95 -75.88, survey
begins 10 Apr - Cape Faraday 77.87 -76.77 - Talbot Inlet 77.92 -77.67 -
"Easter Island" (Wright's name) - Cape Mouat 77.60 -77.68 - Eskimo Bluff
77.43 -78.30 - Boger Point 77.31 -78.83 (station 729 m; met an Inughuit
bear-hunting party) - back north, iceberg stations - Orne Island 77.87 -76.37
on 1 May - Lyman glacier - Cadogan Inlet 78.20 -76.17 - Wade Point 78.53
-74.95 on 13 May - Alfred Newton Glacier 78.58 -75.00 up to the ice cap -
three days west over the ice cap about 4 miles from the north side of Baird
Inlet 78.47 -75.83 - camps C-F, up to about 1400 m - Baird Island - Wade
Point depot - Fram Haven 26 May - Etah.

About 500 miles sledged before the survey even began. Fourteen dogs at the
start, eight at the end: four shot for want of food, one shot for biting a
trace, one lost down a crevasse.

## Where the two papers disagree with the run's brief
- The brief has Haig-Thomas, Wright and Nukapinguaq sledging west together.
  Both papers are explicit that only Haig-Thomas and Nukapinguaq went west;
  Wright and Hamilton turned south-west after three days and surveyed the
  east coast of Ellesmere between Cape Dunsterville and Wade Point.
- The brief has the party wintering at Etah. They wintered at Thule
  (Uummannaq/Dundas), about 150 miles south; Etah was the jumping-off point
  in March 1938 and the MacGregor expedition's winter quarters.
- The expedition had a third member, Richard C. Hamilton, meteorologist.

Other facts only in the Polar Record: the motor boat failed and was replaced by
an 18-foot boat "of a type designed to replace the Eskimo umiaks"; three dog
teams were bought at Jakobshavn and Upernivik and compared badly with Thule
dogs, the worst being exchanged for a Thule Inughuk's team; the Americans lent
an Alaskan sledge when the Nansen sledge proved almost useless on the ice foot;
Wright made a large-scale map of Wolstenholme Fjord and measured a year's
movement of the Moltke and Knud Rasmussen glaciers (published in Medd. om
Groenland 125 (1939) pt 3, p. 42); Nukapinguaq is called "a veteran of
Macmillan's, Lauge Koch's and Edward Shackleton's expeditions"; the new island
is given as "about 200 sq. miles" and "about 20 miles long"; and Hamilton is
"R. A. Hamilton, physicist", which settles the initials against the OCR of the
printed RGS discussion.
