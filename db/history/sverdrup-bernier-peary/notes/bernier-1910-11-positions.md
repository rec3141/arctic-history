# Bernier 1910-11 (D.G.S. *Arctic*) — Positions, Events, and Persons

Source: OCR of *Report on the Dominion Government Expedition to the Northern Waters and
Arctic Archipelago of the D.G.S. "Arctic" in 1910* (Ottawa, Dept. of Marine and Fisheries,
1911), under command of Capt. J. E. Bernier.
File: `db/history/sverdrup-bernier-peary/files/sverdrup-bernier-peary-bernier-report-1911.txt`

Page numbers below are the **printed report page numbers** (recovered from OCR page-break
markers and the table of contents), not OCR line numbers. Where a page number could not be
established with confidence, "?" is given.

Extraction complete: main narrative (Chapters I-VII, pp. 5-84), Appendices 1-3 (Lavoie's and
Morin's sledge/launch survey reports, pp. 85-113), Appendix 5 (Morin, pp. 132-137), Appendix 6
(Janes, pp. 138-144), and Appendix 11 (distances memo, pp. 156-157). Appendix 4 (meteorological
tables, pp. 113-131) is numeric and its OCR is too garbled to mine reliably — see note below the
position table. Appendices 7-10 (Mines Dept. analysis, English's and Mathe's mineral reports,
Cape Crauford cairn report) were not searched in detail as they contain no further ship positions
beyond what appears above.

## 2. Dated events for a timeline

**Outbound and Lancaster Sound approach (1910)**
- **1910-07-07** — Departs Quebec, piloted by Arthur Koenig; crew of 36 all told, provisioned for two years. (p. 8-9)
- **1910-07-15** — First iceberg sighted; whaling schooner "Grayling" reports two men lost overboard in fog off Labrador; "Arctic" searches without success. (p. 9)
- **1910-08-14** — At Possession Bay, Bylot Island vicinity: Bernier deposits records on a 900-ft peak, searching for/re-checking earlier Ross-expedition caches; finds an old broken cairn with a stove dated 1848 and broken bottles; a second cairn examined nearby had had its record removed. (p. 16)
- **1910-08-18/19** — At Erebus Bay / Cape Riley and Cape Spencer (Beechey Island): stores landed to augment the earlier cache; second officer Janes finds and the report transcribes **Sir John Ross's original 1850 record** left with the yacht "Mary" (a refuge vessel hauled up for the crews of any vessel wrecked near Cape Spencer/Beechey Island), including the 1852 endorsement by Capt. Edward Belcher and Capt. H. Kellett confirming the deposit. A second cairn examined by Bernier held nothing. (p. 17, 21-23 — full text of Ross's record and Belcher's endorsement quoted in report)
- **1910-08-30** — At Dealy Island: visits Capt. Kellett's 1852-54 cairn (repaired by Bernier in 1908-09); recovers the ~57-year-old boat left by Kellett for the Ottawa museum, leaving a new boat in its place. (p. 26)

**Winter Harbour, Melville Island — hut and cache (1910)**
- **1910-08-31** — "Arctic" enters Winter Harbour, anchors in 8 fathoms, guided in by 1909 beacon ranges. (p. 27-28)
- **1910-08-31 to 09-02** — A small house ("Cache No. 3" per photo captions) is built at Winter Harbour and **provisions landed and cached** to protect them against loss of the ship; captain seals his own records in a tin case fixed to the staff on "Parry's rock." The report does not itemize the cached provisions' exact quantity in the main narrative (cf. the more detailed Albert Harbour cache of "three months' provisions" on 1910-08-04, p. 14) but states the building was completed and the cache left exactly as Bernier had left it when departing in 1909. (p. 27-29)
- **1910-09-02** — Ship departs Winter Harbour northward (fog hid the beacons, so a southward course could not be taken); finds solid ice to Banks Island.

**Farthest west — the McClure Strait attempt (1910)**
- **1910-09-02** — Second Officer R. S. Janes's mast-head account (quoted verbatim in the report, p. 29-30): at noon the ship was "about 30 miles southward from cape Providence"; ice was "50 to 60 feet thick," unbroken, "as old as Adam." At 8pm from the crow's nest: **"We were now 30 miles S.W. of cape Ross, Melville island and had reached the farthest point in this direction of any vessel."** The ship's head was turned back east that evening; retreat driven by an impenetrable barrier of very old, thick Arctic ice stretching east-west as far as the eye could see, with an easterly wind pushing ice onto Melville Island's shore. (p. 29-30)
- **Independent confirmation, Appendix 6 (Janes's own cairn record of Sept. 12, 1910):** "the ship having crossed McClure strait, or **within 25 miles of Prince of Wales strait**, in the attempt to make the northwest passage... The ship then steamed west for about fifty miles to ascertain if it would be possible to get around the floe... after a hard fight of ten days, contending with heavy ice the whole way, she arrived at [Arctic Bay]." This is the fullest, most specific statement in the report of how far west the ship penetrated — within about 25 miles of Prince of Wales Strait (the strait between Banks and Victoria Islands), well west of Melville Island. (p. 138)
- The main narrative's summary later states flatly: "The 'Arctic' was unable to proceed in 1910 farther than the west coast of Melville island owing to the extremely heavy bodies of ice which filled the western entrance of McClure strait." (p. 5) and reiterates in the closing summary that heavy ice at "the entrance of the Polar sea" made westward passage impossible in the Second Officer's endorsed opinion. (p. 83)

**Retreat, wintering site selection, and freeze-in at Arctic Bay (1910)**
- **1910-09-03** — 23 musk-oxen killed near shore on Melville Island's coast, for food. (p. 30)
- **1910-09-10/11** — Ship anchors off Kikertoo, then finds the inlet ahead choked with old ice from the masthead; **turns back and enters Arctic Bay** instead, anchoring near the bottom of the bay, 1/4 mile off the west shore, in 25 fathoms — this is the decision point that fixed the wintering site. (p. 31-32)
- **1910-09-13** — Bernier visits the Eskimo chief, "Nasso," and takes a census. (p. 32-33)
- **1910-09-23** — Arctic Bay recorded as frozen over (Adams Sound still open). (p. 35-36)
- **1910-10-01** — Everything reported "in readiness for the winter." (p. 37)
- **1910-12-07** — Snow embankment around the ship completed: 9 ft high, 7 ft wide, 20 ft longer than the ship — kept interior 60-70°F vs. 50°F in prior winters with similar embankments. (p. 45)

**Sledge/exploring parties from Arctic Bay, autumn 1910 and spring 1911**
- **1910-10-10** — Lavoie (surveyor), Morin (1st officer), Mathe (geologist), with Eskimo guides Koudnow and Monkey-shaw (Mack-Kie-Shasse), depart to survey Prince Regent Inlet from Cape Kater to Cape Hallowell; Morin splits off to survey the south end of Admiralty Inlet alone. (p. 38, 85)
- **1910-10-20/28** — Cairn built and record left at Shinik Islands (Sassiloet); observed latitude 71°03'33"; Morin turns back for the ship, Lavoie continues to Agoo. (p. 87, 136 — see dating note in position table above)
- **1910-10-31** — Morin returns to ship having surveyed the southern end of Admiralty Inlet solo, guided by Eskimo "Mack-Ka-Ta-Wii" (Muckwawee), praised in both his own and Janes's reports as an outstanding guide. (p. 43, 137-139)
- **1910-11-01** — Lavoie, unable to reach Cape Hallowell itself due to ice, builds a cairn and leaves a record at a substitute campsite (names it "All Saints' Cove"); decides ice conditions too rough to continue to Cape Kater and turns back, reaching the ship on **1910-11-17** after 37 days and 580+ miles. (p. 87-89)
- **1910-12-12** — 2nd Officer Janes departs with 3 sledges, 30 dogs, and one Eskimo guide for Button Point (Bylot Island) with mail, reaching Ponds Inlet whaling station; learns Capt. Adams of the "Morning" had already left with earlier mail on Sept. 10, 1910; finds Ponds Inlet natives starving but their government cache untouched. (p. 46, 52-53, 138-141)
- **1911-03-11 to 04-20** — Quartermaster Alfred Tremblay (called "Arthur Tremblay" once in the main text, likely an OCR/transcription slip for the same man identified as quartermaster in the appendix) + 3 Eskimos sent to Cape Crauford to build a cairn/deposit records and observe ice movements; returns 1911-04-20. (p. 58, 62, Appendix 10)
- **1911-03-15 to 05-11** — **Lavoie's spring 1911 sledge journey** (the major exploring trip of the winter): departs with Eskimo guide Koudnou and (part-way) Proumictou and wife on a relief sledge; adopts Inuit dress/diet entirely. Crosses Brodeur Peninsula, reaches Port Bowen (Prince Regent Inlet) by March 25 (108.75 geographical miles in 6 days from the ship's side of the peninsula); reaches Cape Kater by April 7 (fixed at lat 71°55'N, long 90°05'W); discovers and **names Bernier Bay** (after the Commander) on April 12, with its points named for Morin, Van Koenig (1st Engineer), and capes named for Janes, Macdonald (3rd Officer) and Bolduc (the doctor); reaches and fixes **Cape Hallowell** (lat 69°57'30"N, long 85°26'W) on April 27 — the **southernmost point of the whole spring journey**; crosses to and sets foot on **Hall Island** on April 28, which Lavoie believed made him the first white man there; begins the return leg April 30, is **severely burned in a gasoline-can explosion** the night of May 6 near Moffet Bay, and reaches the ship at 3am on **May 11, 1911**, "feverish, chilled and exhausted," nursed by Capt. Bernier for a week. Total distance covered: 816 geographical miles (939.6 statute miles) — 508 out, 308 on the return through Admiralty Inlet. (Appendix 2, p. 89-106)
- **1911-05-15** — Chief Engineer John Koenig surveys Strathcona Sound; observed position at the bottom of the sound: lat 73°01'15"N, long 82°37'00"W; sound found to be 30 miles long. (p. 64)
- **1911-08-08 to 08-14** — Lavoie and 1st Officer Morin (with Mercier, Nolet, Wm. Morin, Mathe, and English) survey Milne Inlet and the unnamed sounds/Arctic Sound area by steam launch "Arctic Junior" out of Albert Harbour; nearly lose the launch in fog among reefs on Aug. 10 (build "Beacon Reef" cairn and leave a note in case of disaster); return to the ship at Milne Harbour 4:45am Aug. 14. (Appendix 3, p. 106-113)
- **Distances travelled, per Bernier's own summary memo (Appendix 11):** total of **4,341 miles** travelled on foot and by dog sledge/launch by all parties operating out of Arctic Bay during the wintering — consistent with the main report's rounder claim of "four thousand miles ... on foot and with dog sleds." (p. 156-157)

**Freeze-in, ice, and the breakout at Arctic Bay (1910-11)**
- Ship frozen in at Arctic Bay from ~1910-09-23 (bay recorded frozen) through the winter; ice reached 52 in. thick by mid-May 1911 (p. 64).
- **1911-06-14** — Ice becoming porous; work begins to free the ship; visits to Victor Bay for copper specimens. (p. 67)
- **1911-06-20** — Ice ~4 ft thick; propeller raised for examination via shears. (p. 67)
- **1911-07-06** — Exactly one year since departing Quebec; ship still frozen in; compass variation re-established at 96° W. (p. 69-70)
- **1911-07-09** — At 1pm the ship **breaks free of the shore ice**, given 60 fathoms of chain — the effective "breakout" date, though the ship does not clear Admiralty Inlet's pack ice for another three weeks. (p. 70)
- **1911-07-19 to 07-29** — Repeated attempts to leave the bay/inlet; ship makes only 5 miles on 07-20; finally forced clear of the ice with boat-hooks on **1911-07-29**. (p. 72-75)

**Return voyage turning point — Prince Regent Inlet / Fury and Hecla Strait (1911)**
- **1911-08-17** — Ship reaches Port Bowen, Prince Regent Inlet, in a second attempt (via Fury and Hecla Strait and Fox Channel) to reach Hudson Bay and return to Quebec that way. (p. 78)
- **1911-08-17/18** — Approaching Cape Kater, finds heavy old ice blocking the way to Fury and Hecla Strait; after consulting officers, **Bernier abandons the attempt** on **1911-08-18** and turns the ship north. Lavoie (who had just spent March-May in the area) furnishes a written opinion, dated Prince Regent Inlet, Aug. 19, 1911, endorsing the decision: the ice on the 17th was "absolutely impassable ... one solid mass extending from the east to the west shore," and would likely have required at least 10 more days' wait to break up — time the expedition did not have. (p. 78, full letter quoted)
- **1911-08-19** — Ship clears Prince Regent Inlet, off Cape York. (p. 78-79)

**Homeward voyage highlights (1911)**
- **1911-08-06** — Arrives Albert Harbour; 2nd Officer Janes rejoins the ship here, having left it at Arctic Bay on 1910-12-12 — over 7 months in the field visiting Ponds Inlet, Button Point, and prospecting/fishing trips. (p. 77)
- **1911-08-25** — Fails to hail an unlicensed Newfoundland whaling schooner off the Baffin coast (flag raised, whistle blown, no response); Bernier notes photographs were taken to help identify her for a Fisheries Act inquiry. (p. 80)
- **1911-09-02** — Kekerten whaling station, Cumberland Gulf: fishery licenses and Customs business conducted. (p. 80)
- **1911-09-03 (approx)** — Blacklead Island: Bernier distributes books given him by Rev. Dr. Peck for the mission; visits a school; notes Dr. Peck (now in Toronto) had been succeeded in the work by Dr. Grenfell. (p. 80-81)
- **1911-09-06** — Port Burwell: stores cached on an earlier voyage found untouched in the Moravian church loft; Bernier recommends they be moved to a proper cache. (p. 81-82)
- **1911-09-25**, 00:15 — Anchors off Allan's Wharf, Quebec — voyage ends, "all hands well after a fifteen months' cruise." Total distance sailed: about 10,000 miles. (p. 83)

**Proclamations, tablets, cairns, and documents of possession**
- **1850 (found 1910-08-19)** — Sir John Ross's original record at Cape Spencer, Beechey Island, re-discovered and transcribed in full (see above). (p. 21-23)
- **1910-08-14** — Records deposited on high peak, Possession Bay. (p. 16)
- **1910-08-25** — Cairn built ~60 ft above sea level at Pioneer Bay, Cornwallis Island, with a record of the "Arctic's" visit. (p. 23-24)
- **1910-08-31** — Bernier's sealed tin-case record fixed to the staff on "Parry's Rock," Winter Harbour. (p. 29)
- **~1911, date not specified** — A cross (**"Holy Cross"**) is erected on East Point, Arctic Bay, with a copper plate inscribed with the date and particulars of the wintering — a permanent marker of the 1910-11 wintering; Bernier gives sailing directions keyed to the cross for future vessels entering Arctic Bay. (p. 53-54)
- **1911-02-24** — St. George Society cairn built at the western entrance of Arctic Bay; the Society's flag (given to Bernier before leaving Ottawa) hoisted on top, with a record of the Society's request enclosed. (p. 57)
- **1910-10-20/28** — Cairn and record left at Shinik Islands (Sassiloet). (p. 87, 136)
- **1910-11-01** — Cairn/record left at "All Saints' Cove" (Lavoie could not reach Cape Hallowell itself). (p. 88)
- **1911-04-10** — Cairn built at "Leah Point" near Bourassa Bay — intended for Cape Kater per Bernier's original instructions, but built here instead as the party could not detour back. (p. 97)
- **1911-04-29** — Record left (no cairn possible — no stone on the island) on Hall Island near Cape Englefield. (p. 102-103)
- **1911-08-10** — "Beacon Reef" cairn and safety record left during the Milne Inlet launch survey. (p. 109-110)

### 3c. Inuit named in the text (spelling as the report gives it)

- **Nasso** (also "Nassa," and possibly "old Nassau" in one anecdote) — chief of the Eskimo settlement/village at Arctic Bay; Bernier paid him a formal visit and took a census on 1910-09-13; told Bernier where minerals might be found; invited to Christmas dinner aboard ship; attended Sunday services.
- **Macatowee** and **Macashaw** — two Eskimos hired at the village of Albert (Albert Harbour) on 1910-08-12, engaged for the voyage.
- **Koudnow / Koudnoo / Koo-Noo** — Lavoie's principal guide on both the Oct. 1910 and March-May 1911 sledge expeditions; an expert dog-handler; his young son also accompanied him on the first trip; caused (accidentally, via a leaking gasoline stove) the igloo fire of March 21, 1911; his sister and her husband were met returning to Igloolik on the return leg.
- **Monkey-shaw** (also "Mack-Kie-Shasse") — Eskimo guide on Lavoie's Oct. 1910 trip; Janes's report explicitly ranks him as inferior in ability to Mack-Ka-Ta-Wii/Muckwawee (see below).
- **Mack-Ka-Ta-Wii** (also spelled **Muckwawee**, and probably the "Macket-a-Oui"/"Macket-a-we" of other captions) — guide to First Officer Morin on his solo Oct. 1910 Admiralty Inlet survey; both Morin and Janes praise him highly ("the sterling qualities of my excellent guide... the best type of a faithful and kind guide"); a photo caption reads "Grave of Taveng Macket-a-we, Arctic Bay" (p. 68), suggesting this same man (given name possibly "Taveng") died and was buried at Arctic Bay before the ship left in 1911 — see the (undated) death-and-burial episode in the main narrative, p. 65-66, which does not itself give the deceased's name.
- **Proumictou / Pioumictou / Pioomictoo** — Eskimo who, with his wife, drove a relief sledge for part of Lavoie's spring 1911 trip, then continued with Lavoie all the way to Cape Hallowell and back; his wife nursed Lavoie after his May 1911 burns.
- **Sigailto** — chief at Agoo (Autridge Bay); Lavoie lodged in his igloo for several days in Oct.-Nov. 1910 to learn the language; a "Sigailto" (b.) [boy] also appears as a child's name in the Agoo family census.
- **Outoucoutou** — an Eskimo of Igloolik encountered en route to Prince Regent Inlet, March 1911.
- **Okey** — native met near Moffet Bay, March 1911, "entirely exhausted" and badly frostbitten with his wife and child; revived with hot coffee by Lavoie.
- **Omming / Ooming** — native who brought 450 lbs. of fresh salmon to the ship on 1911-07-10 (with his son) and left more at a cache; also reported (per Koudnoo's sister) to be prospecting with Mathe at Moffet Bay in May 1911.
- **Massan** — an Arctic Bay Inuk whose igloo and family are twice photographed ("Inside Massan's igloo," Nov. 1910; "Massan and near relatives," June 23, 1911).
- **Tom** — identified only as "Tom, the Interpreter" (photo caption, p. 71); presumably an Inuk interpreter attached to the ship.
- **Otumjua** and **Ooming** [as a family, distinct mention] — noted as away from the main Agoo group for the winter with a few families (about 20 people), per the Appendix 1 census.

### 3d. Whaling masters, traders, and missionaries met

- **Captain Milne** — master of the whaler "Diana" (Newfoundland), met at Ponds Inlet/Albert Harbour, Aug. 1910; paid fishery license fee to Bernier.
- **Captain Adams** — master of the whaler "Morning" (Dundee); left Ponds Inlet with mail on 1910-09-10 before the "Arctic's" own mail arrived; boarded again by Janes on 1911-07-10 near Button Point — reported a very hard ice season, beset 21 days, no whales seen, left Dundee 15 April 1911; declined to pay a whaling license on the spot pending owners' instructions. (NB: a different, earlier "Captain Adams" — of the whaler "Arctic" — is credited in the historical background with the 1872 survey of Admiralty Inlet, naming Richards and Yeaman Islands and Adams Sound; not the same man.)
- **The "Diana"'s captain (1911, unnamed)** — boarded by Janes on 1911-07-12 near Button Point; declined a whaling license, citing no funds, company to pay if insisted upon.
- **Captain Comer** — "the well known American whaler and explorer," mentioned (not met directly by Bernier) by an Inuk visitor at Arctic Bay as someone he had encountered, associated with whaling/trading voyages to Hudson Bay.
- **Rev. Dr. Peck** — missionary at Blacklead Island, Cumberland Gulf; credited by Bernier with teaching the Blacklead Inuit to read and write; "now resides in Toronto."
- **Dr. Grenfell** — noted as having taken up Dr. Peck's mission work; his Labrador work "well known the world over."
- **Rev. Mr. Greenshield** — missionary at Blacklead Island (named in a photo caption, Aug. 1911).
- **Mr. Forsyth Grant** — operates a trading station at Cape Haven, Baffin Island; "Arctic" stopped briefly, Sept. 4, 1911.
- **Captain Jackson** — master of the "Harmony" (St. John's, Newfoundland), visited by Bernier at Port Burwell to enquire after mail, Sept. 1911.
