# The Roosevelt, 1905–06: an index to Peary's "Nearest the Pole"

Source: Robert E. Peary, *Nearest the Pole: A Narrative of the Polar Expedition of the Peary Arctic Club in the S.S. Roosevelt, 1905–1906* (New York: Doubleday, Page & Co., 1907), from the archive.org OCR at
`db/history/sverdrup-bernier-peary/files/sverdrup-bernier-peary-peary1907-nearest.txt`.

**On page numbers.** The OCR carries the printed running head ("N  NEAREST THE POLE") at the top of every even page, which anchors ~100 fixed points across the book. Page numbers below are interpolated between those anchors against the OCR's own line numbers, and confirmed against the table of contents' chapter-opening pages. They should be accurate to within a page or two, not laser-precise. Where Peary gives no direct observation, coordinates are supplied from general knowledge of these places' modern names/positions and are marked "(approx.)" where confidence is limited — real gazetteer figures were not separately verified in this pass.

**On coverage.** This index covers Chapters I–XIV (the 1905–06 narrative), Chapter XVI ("The Roosevelt"), and Chapter XVII ("My Eskimos") plus its appended Census and the book's index. Chapter XV, a reprint of Peary's report on his separate 1898–1902 expedition, is out of scope and was not read.

## Section 4 — People

### Ship's officers and crew (Chapter I roster, pp.11–14, and passim)

- **Robert A. Bartlett** — Captain, sailing master and ice navigator, 30, Newfoundlander; came from a family of Arctic ice masters (great-uncle master of the *Tigress* on the Polaris search; uncles Samuel, John and "Cap Sam" on other Arctic voyages); had served as mate on the *Windward* 1898–99. Commanded the ship throughout, remained in the fore rigging during ice emergencies, later led one of the spring 1906 supporting divisions to Cape Hecla (Feb. 19). p.11.
- **Moses Bartlett** — Mate, the captain's second cousin, 47; had been to Cape Sabine three times, wintered once in Hudson Bay with the Canadian government. p.12.
- **George A. Wardwell** — Chief Engineer, of Bucksport, Maine, 44; had worked in the shipyard where Roosevelt was built. p.12.

### Inuit people named in the text

- **Sipsu** — sledge-party member; nerves badly shaken by an ice crack opening near him at the Big Lead (p.116); pictured with his family "returning to the ship from Fort Conger" (illustration, p.240); he and his wife came aboard from Fort Conger, he partly dressed in a captured cavalry officer's uniform (p.249); his old black dog killed for food during the return march (index, p.230).
- **Egingwah** — hunter and dog-driver on the western Grant Land sledge party; scouted for game; sent alone ahead to the Roosevelt from View Point with a letter for help, July 1906 (p.235–237); pictured with "the Morris K. Jesup sledge" (p.174) and with reindeer at Cape Hubbard (p.220).
- **Ooblooyah** — hunter/driver on the western party; husband of Akatingwah; mended Peary's trousers after a five-hour repair job; pictured as "a young Eskimo man of about twenty-three" (p.276).
- **Akatingwah** — Ooblooyah's wife; pictured with their baby, captioned "Akatingwah, Wife of Ooblooyah" (p.379).
- **Koolootingwah** — hunter on both the northern and western sledge parties; killed musk-oxen; sent back to the ship with dispatches and specimens; his sledge dog slipped its harness on the return march (p.196, p.231).
- **Panikpah** — "the lightest of us all and most experienced," led the crossing of the Big Lead's young ice on both the outward and return legs (p.121, p.145).
- **Ahngmalokto** — companion on the farthest-north march; made tea, joked that the ship's heavy chief engineer would have drowned crossing the thin lead ice, warned Peary during the Grant Land musk-ox hunt; called "faithful Ahngmalokto" (p.115, p.140, p.156).
- **Ootah** — found and ate a dropped scrap of pemmican "like an Eskimo dog"; first spotted the Nares Land musk-oxen, shouting "Ooming-muksuef!"; exclaimed "we have arrived at last, thank God" on reaching Cape Union; part of a supporting party that cached musk-ox meat at Britannia Island in 1900; his wife bore a child en route to the ship during the 1906 southbound voyage (p.142, p.154, p.167, p.169, p.249).
- **Ahngodoblaho** — crossed the Big Lead with Ryan's party; became very lame on the return march and was left behind in a camp in Robeson Channel (p.121, p.150, p.169).
- **Itookishoo / Itukashoo / Itookashoo** (spelling varies in the text; probably one man) — one of Ryan's three men at the Big Lead (p.121); sent with Marvin and Koodlooktoo to move loads on the sea ice (p.178).
- **"Teddy" / Teddylingwah** — a sledge-party man nicknamed "Teddy" during the farthest-north march (p.121), likely the same "Teddylingwah" who came in with Marvin's relief party in July 1906 (p.236) — flagged here as a probable but unconfirmed match of spellings.
- **Koodlooktoo / Kudlooktoo** — sent back to the ship for stoves during the western journey; later worked with Marvin's sounding party (p.176, p.178).
- **Pewahtoo / Pewahto** — sent ahead with Henson near the Big Lead (p.130); an older man than Sigloo, went with Peary on the final relief march to the ship (p.240).
- **Sigloo / Siglu** — came in with Marvin's relief party; guided Peary from a support camp toward the ship but got lost, reaching it only the next morning (p.236, p.239).
- **Ahwegingwah** — came in with Marvin's relief party; started back for the ship with Sigloo and Teddylingwah (p.236, p.240).
- **Pooblah / Publa** — a lame member of Clark's party; repeatedly failed to reach camp with the rest during the return march (p.154, p.169).
- **Clark's three (unnamed) Inuit companions** — found with him nearly starved a few miles from Peary's camp near Cape Neumeyer, having lived on the soles of spare skin boots (p.149–150).
- **Ahmah** — identified as "Merktoshah's wife"; an active older woman who walked overland to Anoritok and spotted the ship's smoke in Kane Basin three days before its arrival (p.262; index p.259).
- **Merktoshah** — named only as Ahmah's husband; not otherwise described.
- **Kyo** — an angakok (medicine man) whose accurate predictions of coming deaths made him feared; murdered by a hunting party — Peary's only recorded instance of an Eskimo killing another (p.383).
- **Nupsah** and **Pooadloonah / Puadloonah** — rivals in a friendly stone-lifting contest of strength (p.383).
- **Inaloo** — suffered a "pihlocto" (a hysterical fit) off Cape D'Urville aboard the *Windward* in 1898: stripped and ran onto the sea ice at night, later mimicked local bird and animal calls (p.385).
- **Megipsu** — described as the settlement's "village gossip," discussing the ship's goods with other women (p.376).
- **Ahweahgoodloo / Ahweagoodlu** — a four-year-old girl, pictured (p.368–369).
- **Int'aho / Inuaho** — a girl, pictured, captioned "Inuaho, Eskimo Girl" (p.376); possibly distinct from Inaloo above — flagged as an open question given the similarity of the OCR'd spellings.
- **Ahnighite / Ahnighito** — a name in the Census list (p.391); note the same name was given by Peary to the great Cape York meteorite and to his daughter Marie Ahnighito — this census entry is a distinct living person, not otherwise described in the narrative.
- **Narksami** — named only in the book's index (p.266), with no narrative content recovered in this pass; could not confirm whether person or place.
