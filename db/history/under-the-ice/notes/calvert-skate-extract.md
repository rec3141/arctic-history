## 4. QUOTES

### August 1958 Events

> "This ice pack isn't one solid piece. It's filled with lakes that open and close as the ice floes shift. I've seen pictures of them." (Dennis Wilkinson on polynyas, p. 21)

> "The sea is his, and he made it; and his hands prepared the dry land. O come, let us worship and fall down...." (From Book of Common Prayer, Sunday service under ice, p. 75)

> "Heavy ice, ten feet," he said laconically. At the plotting table, the black pencil line continued to follow the path of the moving light. Then the stylus pattern suddenly converged to a single narrow bar. "Clear water," he called out. (Ice detection operations, p. 1-2)

> "Stand by to surface!" The words went out on the loudspeaker system, electrifying the rest of the ship. (Calvert ordering surfacing from periscope depth, p. 10)

> "The Skate surfaced in a large polynya. Members of the crew use a rubber lift raft to ferry back and forth between the ship and the ice." (Caption illustration, p. 6/578)

### North Pole and March 1959 Events

> "At one forty-seven Greenwich time on 12 August 1958 the Skate has reached the northernmost part of our planet. My congratulations to each one of you, but this is only the beginning." (Calvert's announcement at North Pole, p. 91)

> "We were like a whale awash, with just the hump of our sail out of the water." (Description of partial surfacing near North Pole, p. 95)

> "The clarity of the water and the amount of light were startling. At this same depth in the Atlantic the water looks black or at best a dark green, but here the sea was a pale and transparent blue like the lovely tropical waters off the Bahamas." (Periscope observations at Arctic depth, p. 3)

> "In the lurid glow of the torches, I read the committal: Unto Almighty God we commend the soul of our brother departed, and we commit his ashes to the deep; in sure and certain hope of the Resurrection unto eternal life..." (Wilkins memorial service, p. 7948-7954)

> "There was not a cloud in a sky brilliant with stars. I have never seen them look so close or so clear. There was a three-quarter moon which, reflected by the snow and ice, gave a surprising amount of light..." (Moonlit scene after surfacing March 19, 1959, p. 191)

> "The ice in this lead is really flat. Looks like you're swimming under a glass ceiling." (Skindiver Sam Hall describing underwater ice ceiling, p. 230)

> "Only 40 miles from the North Pole we found a lead that opened up. Only 40 miles from the North Pole we found a lead that opened up... it was clear that the North Pole area did not have sufficient ice-free areas..." (Location and ice conditions north of Pole, p. 5585)
