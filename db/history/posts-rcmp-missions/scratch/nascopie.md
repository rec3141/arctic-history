Every post, mission and detachment in this topic existed at the end of one
thread: a ship that came once a year. For thirty-five years that ship was the
[[artifact:posts-rcmp-missions-005|Nascopie]].

## What she was

A steel screw semi-icebreaker of 2,520 gross tons, 285 feet long with a 43-foot
beam, built at the Neptune Works at Wallsend-on-Tyne in 1911-12 by Swan Hunter
and Wigham Richardson to a design by G. S. Goodwin of Liverpool. Triple
expansion engines of 2,300 brake horsepower, twelve or thirteen knots, fifty
passengers and forty-five to fifty crew. She was built for the Nascopie
Steamship Company, a joint venture of the Hudson's Bay Company and Job Brothers
of St John's; the Company bought Job Brothers out in 1916. She was named for
the Naskapi of Labrador.

None of that is why she matters. What matters is the schedule.

## Ship time

A post like [[place:Mittimatalik]] or [[place:Kangiqtugaapik]] was resupplied
once. The whole year's flour, tea, tobacco, ammunition, lumber, coal and mail
came ashore in a few days in August or September, and the fur went the other
way. If the ice was bad and the ship could not get in, the post did without for
another twelve months. The RCMP annual report for 1924 records the ordinary
consequence: after the *Arctic* sailed on 3 September 1923 and the *Nascopie*
on the 18th, "thereafter Ponds Inlet had no communication with the outside world
until August of 1924."

For Inuit, ship day was the largest event of the year — a fact the photographs
of the period record without meaning to.
[[artifact:posts-rcmp-missions-014|Library and Archives Canada's caption]] on
one of them reads simply: *Inuit women in new parkas awaiting the arrival of
passengers from the RMS Nascopie.*

## The work she did

The [[source:hbca2005nascopie|Hudson's Bay Company Archives ship history sheet]]
lays out the range of it. She supplied the eastern Arctic and worked the
Newfoundland seal fishery from 1912 to 1930. From 1916 to 1921 she was on French
government business, running to the White Sea. In 1921 she carried a reindeer
herd and its Sami herders from Norway to Baffin Island, part of a scheme to give
Inuit a herding economy that never took. She was laid up in 1931-32 and refitted
to take tourists. From 1933 to 1947 she carried the government's Eastern Arctic
Patrol — the annual circuit that brought a doctor, a dentist, a magistrate and
an inspecting officer to every enclave on the coast, and which is the direct
ancestor of every government programme that came north afterwards. In 1937 she
and the motor schooner *Aklavik* between them completed a transit of the
Northwest Passage. In 1940-42 she was chartered for voyages to Greenland and the
Caribbean.

Thomas Smellie commanded her longer than anyone: 1917-19, 1922-25 and, without a
break, 1933 to 1945. Her last master was James Waters.

## The reef

On 21 July 1947 the *Nascopie* was entering the harbour at
[[place:Kinngait]] (Cape Dorset) when she struck an uncharted reef off Beacon
Island. She grounded hard and flooded, and was declared a total loss. Everyone
aboard got off, and so did the cargo. She sat on the reef for two months until a
storm broke her in two, and on 15 October another storm carried the remainder
into deep water.

She was replaced by aircraft and by other ships, but not by anything that did
the same job in the same way. The Company's own archives keep the ledger of the
end: insurance claims for hull, for loss and for personnel, 1947-48, and a
scrapbook of press cuttings.

The wreck itself belongs with [[topic:wrecks-disasters]].

## Why she is worth a page

Because the annual ship is the mechanism of this whole period. A trading post is
only viable if a ship can reach it; a police detachment is only occupiable if a
ship can provision it; a mission only functions if a ship brings the books.
[[place:Bache Peninsula]] was called the loneliest post in the empire precisely
because in some years no ship could reach it at all and it had to be supplied by
dog team from [[place:Craig Harbour]], two hundred miles away. When Ottawa
wanted to establish a presence on Ellesmere Island in 1922, the limit on the
plan was not money or manpower but hull capacity: the CGS *Arctic* could not
carry building material for three posts, so the Devon Island stores sat in
Quebec for two years and [[place:Dundas Harbour]] was not founded until 1924.

The map of the eastern Arctic between 1900 and 1950 is, in a real sense, a map
of where a ship could get to in August.
