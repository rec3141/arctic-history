## Corrections to assumptions in the research brief — read first

1. **Ouangwak / Ou-ang-wak is NOT one of the Janes accused.** He is a Padlimiut
   (Paddlemuit) man of the Baker Lake district who shot two brothers,
   Angalookyouak and Alecummick, in autumn 1919; arrested Feb 1920 by Sgt Douglas;
   absconded from Chesterfield Inlet ~Jan 1921 and died in a blizzard; his remains
   were found in 1922. Entirely separate case from Pond Inlet.
   The three Janes accused were **Noo-kud-lah (Nuqallaq), alias Ki-wat-soo**;
   **Oo-roo-re-ung-nak (Ululijarnaaq / Ouangwak-lookalike spelling — but printed
   consistently as Oo-roo-re-ung-nak)**; and **Ah-tee-tah (Aatitaaq)**.
2. **"Bache Peninsula" never appears in these five reports.** The northern
   Ellesmere sub-post established in summer 1924 is called **"Kane Basin
   detachment"**, on **Rice strait, opposite Pim island, at the southern end of
   Kane basin** — i.e. the Bache Peninsula locality, but under a different name.
   Cape Sabine is named once (1924 report, p.34) as the intended but unreachable
   site.
3. **Craig Harbour was founded in 1922, not "commanded" by a series of officers in
   these years** — Insp. C. E. Wilcox 1922–23; then Cpl Michelson with Cst Lee and
   Cst Anstead 1923–24.
4. The killing of Janes is dated **March 1920** in the 1923 report (and "April 1920"
   in the 1922 report — the reports disagree; the coroner's jury said "on or about
   the end of March … the precise date being to the jurors unknown"). Cape Crawford
   is spelt **Crawford** in 1922–23 and **Crauford** in 1925.
5. Craig Harbour co-ordinates differ between reports: **lat 76°12′, long 80.5°**
   (1923, p.20) vs **lat 76°10′ N, long 80°55′** (1925, p.38).

# 1921 report (year ended 30 September 1920)

No Baffin Island or Ellesmere presence yet. Eastern work = Hudson Bay
(Fullerton, Chesterfield Inlet, Baker Lake), the new Port Burwell post, and the
Belcher Islands.

## Detachments

- **Port Burwell** (Hudson strait) — "New outposts were established in the Far
  North: One at Port Burwell on the Hudson Straits, to act for the Department of
  Customs and collect duties on foreign vessels entering the waters of Hudson bay"
  (p.9). No exact founding day given in this report; Sgt J. E. F. Wight's arrival
  report is dated **25 September 1920** (given in the 1922 report, p.23).
- **Chesterfield Inlet** — "A new post for 4 men is being constructed at
  Chesterfield Inlet." (p.32). No date.
- **Fullerton** — existing; "over 400 miles farther north than Churchill, and 100
  miles up the coast from Chesterfield Inlet"; patrols based on it covered nearly
  16,000 miles Sept 1916 – Jan 1919 (p.11).
- Police power boat **Lady Borden** stationed on Chesterfield Inlet (p.32).

# 1922 report (year ended 30 September 1921)

**The year Pond Inlet was founded.**

## Detachments

### Ponds Inlet (established 1921)

- Reason: HBC established a trading post at Ponds Inlet in summer 1921; "in
  addition to general considerations as to the desirability of extending the
  administrative jurisdiction of the Dominion to these regions … there was the
  special reason" of the Janes killing (p.20). **This is the closest the reports
  come to an explicit sovereignty rationale.**
- **Staff-Sergeant A. H. Joy** appointed to command — sent in the HBC steamer
  *Baychimo*; billeted at the HBC post. Commissioned as justice of the peace for
  the NWT, coroner, special customs officer, and **postmaster** of a new post
  office at Ponds Inlet.
- Position given as "approximately in latitude 72-40, longitude Tfi-llO" (p.20) —
  the longitude is **OCR-garbled and unusable**; latitude 72°40′ is right for Pond
  Inlet. "It must be the northernmost post office in North America."
- Instructions from Commissioner A. Bowen Perry dated **6 July 1921** (pp.20–21).
- Detachment strength in the first winter: **Joy alone** (the only other white men
  in the vicinity were at the two trading stations) — stated retrospectively in the
  1923 report, p.35.
- The detachment site chosen: "about half a mile to the east of Salmon river."
- Number of Far North detachments rose "from five in 1920 to nine in 1921"; Ponds
  Inlet replaced Tree River as "our most remote outpost" (p.6).

### Port Burwell

Sgt J. E. F. Wight in charge, appointed special customs officer; he and one
constable boarded with the Moravian Mission during the year; "in future a small
building originally erected by the customs authorities will be used, with some
additions" (p.23). ~150 Eskimo in the district.

### Chesterfield Inlet / Baker Lake

Insp. A. E. O. Reames reached Chesterfield Inlet **17 September 1920** after a
54-day voyage from Montreal; too late in the year to build quarters. He could not
leave for the murder scene until **early January 1921**; held the inquests and
viewed the graves. **Ou-ang-wak fled in the night when Reames left to hold the
inquest and was never seen again**; believed to have perished in the blizzard
(pp.35–36).

# 1923 report (year ended 30 September 1922)

**The year Craig Harbour was founded and the Janes accused were arrested and
committed for trial.**

## Detachments

### Craig Harbour, Ellesmere Island — founded August 1922

Headline notice (p.20): "A new post has been opened by Inspector C. E. Wilcox at
Craig Harbour, on the south shore of Ellesmere island, approximately in latitude
76′ 12, longitude 80.5, and he and his party will spend the winter there."

Voyage and founding (pp.21–22):

|---|---|

| **15 Aug** | Reached the entrance to Ponds Inlet; pack ice prevented entering to Salmon river |
| **17 Aug** | Staff-Sgt Joy, summoned over the ice, reported to Wilcox aboard the *Arctic*. Wilcox decided to push on to Ellesmere and land the Ponds Inlet stores on the return voyage |
| **18 Aug** | *Arctic* sailed from Ponds Inlet |
| **20 Aug** | *Arctic* entered Jones Sound. Fram Fiord (the intended site, the most easterly inlet on the south coast of Ellesmere) blocked with ice; two attempts to enter failed |
| — | "A fairly suitable place, which was named **Craig Harbour** (after Mr. J. D. Craig, the officer representing the Department of the Interior), was found on the shore of Ellesmere island **opposite Smith island in Jones sound**" |
| **22 Aug** | Wilcox determined to winter there; landing of stores began |
| **28 Aug** | Wilcox left the *Arctic* and "entered upon his sojourn there"; "he bade farewell to the ship in a blinding snowstorm." **With him: one non-commissioned officer and five constables** |

| **6 Sept** | Could not begin landing stores at Ponds Inlet until this date |

- A post office was established at Craig Harbour, Wilcox postmaster.
- Original plan was **three** posts — Ponds Inlet, North Devon Island, Ellesmere
  Island — but the *Arctic* could not carry material for all three; the North Devon
  materials were left stored in Quebec for the following year.
- Wilcox judged Craig Harbour **unsuitable as sub-district headquarters** and
  recommended **Dundas Harbour**, south coast of North Devon island,
  "approximately latitude 74′ 35″, longitude 82′ 20″", because Lancaster sound is
  usually ice-free longer than Jones sound. On the return voyage the *Arctic*
  reconnoitred Dundas harbour and J. D. Craig reported it suitable — "well
  protected from all winds … room and shelter for buildings and a good anchorage
  close at hand."
- "Cordial relations existed between Inspector Wilcox and the police party on the
  one hand, and Mr. Craig and Captain Bernier on the other." Both Craig and
  **Capt. J.-E. Bernier** wrote Wilcox commendatory letters on landing.
- "No further news has been received from Inspector Wilcox, nor can any be
  expected until late in 1923."

### Ponds Inlet

Wilcox "on his way north left a corporal and two constables with him, so that
during this winter the detachment will number four" (p.35). Detachment strength
table (p.~46, badly column-scrambled in OCR) reads Ponds Inlet 1+1+2 = **4** and
Ellesmere Island 1+1+5 = **7** — consistent with the narrative.

### Chesterfield Inlet

Post at Churchill closed; **Insp. E. G. Frere** stationed at Chesterfield Inlet,
"about four hundred miles further north, with a detachment of some strength"
(p.36). Other post in the area: Port Nelson.

## The Robert Janes killing and the 1922 investigation (Appendix A, pp.33–35)

of 1910–11; established himself as a trader at **Patricia River, Eclipse sound,
about thirty-five miles from Ponds Inlet**, in 1916. Trade failed; backing in
St John's fell through.

## Craig Harbour, winter 1923–24 (pp.38–39)

- Position given here as **"approximately latitude 76′ 10″ north, longitude 80′
  55″"**.
- **"remained isolated for very nearly a whole year, the ship having left them
  there on August 15, 1923, and having returned on August 9, 1924."**
- **Detachment building destroyed by fire, 22 February 1924**, during a blizzard
  from the northeast. All three men were indoors. Fire started from the Bose Cone
  heater in the bedroom or its pipes. Pyrene extinguishers frozen; very little
  water; nothing could be done from outside. Bedding and arms saved first;
  "within a very short space of time the house was a red hot shell." The storehouse
  narrowly escaped. The **blubber shed** became temporary quarters. All the men
  were frostbitten in the salvage. Articles were later found scattered over the ice
  nearly two miles away. The day after the fire registered **55° below zero**.
- **26 February 1924** — near-asphyxiation in the blubber shed from a badly fitted
  stove: **Cst Lee** fainted at the door, **Cpl Michelson** collapsed, **Cst
  Anstead** got outside and revived the others.
- **Personnel: Cpl Michelson, Cst Lee, Cst Anstead.**
- **Two Inuit families brought from Etah, North Greenland**, served the detachment
  through the year. (No individual names given.)
- Patrols were made and a portion of the coastline surveyed; **records deposited by
  Captain Sverdrup were found**; remains of former Eskimo dwellings noticed and some
  excavation done.

## Ponds Inlet, winter 1923–24 (pp.37–38)

- **Staff-Sgt A. H. Joy** in charge. After the trial, the C.G.S. *Arctic* sailed
  south **3 September 1923**; the HBC ship *Nascopie* left **18 September 1923** for
  Montreal; "thereafter Ponds Inlet had no communication with the outside world
  until August of 1924."
- Unusually mild winter; lowest temperature **49.5° below zero**, early February, at
  the return of the sun.
- **Epidemic of an unrecognised disease from the beginning of May 1924 — 13 deaths
  in a few days**; prevalent through May and part of June; almost every Eskimo in
  the district infected.
- The dog disease of 1921–23 had run its course.
- Scientific work: ~30 plant specimens, ~50 insect specimens, and **two old Eskimo
  house sites excavated** — the first yielding 167 specimens, the second (dug with
  **Cst Must**) some 527 specimens including several human skulls; the whole
  collection sent to the Victoria Memorial Museum, whose Chief of the Division of
  Anthropology called it "magnificent material."

## Pangnirtung, winter 1923–24 (pp.36–37)

- **Insp. C. E. Wilcox** wintered at Pangnirtung fiord, north shore of Cumberland
  gulf. The detachment covered **about 2,000 miles by dog team and over 500 by
  whaleboat**; Wilcox himself travelled over 1,750 miles in the C.G.S. *Arctic*.
- **December 1923** and **March 1924** — two errands of mercy to the Eskimo
  settlement at **Kekerten**, a small island near the mouth of Kingnait fiord: the
  first to relieve want, the second to help a woman ill with tuberculosis.
- **January and February 1924** — two longer patrols covering the entire coastline
  of Cumberland gulf.
- Weather damage: buildings partly unroofed **17 November 1923** and **1 May 1924**;
  on **15 May 1924** the detachment canoe was carried about 60 feet by a gust and
  thrown on rocks.
- **31 January 1924** — Cst MacGregor visited fissures a mile northeast of Coolee
  river emitting jets of hot air that melted snow at −30° (Wilcox's diary).

**Distances as estimated by McInnes:** Pangnirtung → Kevetuk via north and south
Pangnirtung fiords, 200 miles; Kevetuk → Ametooahgoogoose Island, Padlee,
Merchants Bay, 110 miles; thence to Pangnirtung detachment by Padlee and Kingnait
fiords, 200 miles. **Total ≈ 510 miles, 47 days.**

**Findings.** Depositions from twelve natives including **Kidlappik**, who killed
**Neakoteah**, and **Kowtuk** and **Kedluk**, the slayers of **Mungeuk** and
**Seeming** (spelled Lemik in the 1924 report). The community numbered ten families,
35–40 people. Neakoteah's derangement first became dangerous **at a dance on
Christmas Day 1921**; the murders fell between **Christmas 1921 and January 1922**.
Wilcox recommended **no charges be laid** — the expense of bringing a court would
not be warranted, and the perpetrators had been warned.

## Port Burwell and Chesterfield Inlet

- **Port Burwell** — Sgt J. F. Wight; principal incident a patrol in **March 1924**
  to **Georges river**, about **300 miles** (p.39). *Month only.*
- **Chesterfield Inlet** — headquarters for northern Hudson Bay shifted from
  Churchill. Patrols south to Churchill, west to Baker Lake, north to Fullerton;
  police buildings maintained at Fullerton and Baker Lake. **Cpl O. G. Petty** spent

# Gaps and cautions

- No exact day is given for: the founding of Port Burwell (1920); the Chesterfield
  Inlet post construction (1920); Dundas Harbour and Kane Basin (summer 1924); the
  Igloolik patrol of winter 1922–23; Munday's Baker Lake patrol (April–May 1923);
  Wight's Georges river patrol (March 1924); the Kekerten mercy trips
  (Dec 1923, March 1924); the Cumberland gulf patrols (Jan–Feb 1924).
- The Janes killing is dated **April 1920** in the 1922 report and **March 1920** in
  the 1923 report; the inquest verdict says "on or about the end of March … the
  precise date being to the jurors unknown"; the 1923 report separately says Janes
  arrived at Cape Crawford on **14 March 1920**.
- The 1922 report spells the accused **Noo-Kad-Lak**; 1923 **Noo-Kud-Lah / Noo-kud-lah,
  alias Ki-wat-soo**; 1924 **Noo-kud-lah, alias Ki-wat-soon**.
- Craig Harbour longitude differs between the 1923 and 1925 reports (see head note).
- The Ponds Inlet longitude in the 1922 report is unreadable in this OCR.
- Detachment strength tables in all five files are column-scrambled by OCR; use the
  narrative figures, not the tables.
- Wilcox is said both to have "wintered" at Pangnirtung and to have been picked up
  at Blacklead Island "who had wintered there" (1925 report, pp.36–37) — the
  Blacklead phrasing is loose; the substantive statement is that he wintered at
  Pangnirtung fiord.
