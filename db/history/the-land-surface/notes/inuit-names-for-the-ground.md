# Words for the ground, from the vocabularies on disk

Sources: John Ross's 1835 appendix (Boothia), Parry's 1824 second-voyage vocabulary
(Iglulingmiut), Boas 1888 glossary, Rasmussen's Netsilik report, the Greenland Pilot's
place-name glossary (Kalaallisut), Peary and Rasmussen for Inuktun, and the Geographic
Board of Canada's ninth report.

## Land, earth, ground
- Kalaallisut nuna, nunat, nunaa (Greenland Pilot 245-247)
- Inuktitut (Iglulingmiut, Parry) Noona, land or country - and the same word for a plant
- Inuktitut (Boothia, Ross) Nuna, also Irbsok for "earth"; nunamine, my land
- Netsilik nunatorLEq, manured soil with sappy green grass, at old house ruins (Rasmussen)

## Hill, mound, pingo
- Kalaallisut Pingu, older orthography Pingo: elevation, seagull mound (Greenland Pilot 860)
- Kalaallisut Pinguarsuit, smaller elevations; Pingaajik, the small gull mound
- Inuktitut (Boothia, Ross) **Pinguit, glossed "Marsh" and "Meadow"** - the same root, applied
  to the wet ground a frost mound stands in
- Netsilik Pingolasait, the small mountains
- Inuktitut (Ross) Kingiksusek, eminence of land
- Inuktitut (Boas) Kingnait, the high land; Igpirto, with many hills

## Beach and shore
- Kalaallisut Sissarissoq, place with good beach; Sineriak, coast
- Inuktitut (Boas) Tininiqdjuaq, the large beach

## Sand, gravel, clay, mud, stone
- Kalaallisut Sioraq, sand; Siorataliip Kangerlua, the fjord of the sandy place
- Inuktitut (Iglulingmiut, Parry) Seokat, sand; Oo-yar-ra, stone
- Inuktitut (Boothia, Ross) Siorarsoit, gravel (probable)
- Netsilik siorArto-q, the sandy one; siorAi'JuAq, the big sandy one
- Kalaallisut Marraq, clay; Isortoq, the turbid, muddy one
- Inuktitut (Boas) Issortuqdjuaq, the large one with muddy water; Ujaraqdjuin, the large stones
- Kalaallisut Qaarsorssuaq, the large rock surface; Qaarsut, rock surfaces
- Inuktun (Inughuit, Peary) iveeaksuc, rocks

## Valley, plain, cliff, slope
- Kalaallisut Narsaq, plain; Qooroq, valley; Ippik, bluff or cliff; Innaarsuaq, large cliff wall;
  Innaarsulik, place with steep slope; Portussoq, high-lying
- Inuktitut (Iglulingmiut, Parry) Nak-seak, valley or low land
- Inuktitut (Boothia, Ross) Narksak, plain; Iinnak, precipice
- Inuktitut (Boas) Nerseqdjuaq, the large valley; Sulung, the valley through which the wind blows
  howling; Aulitiving, glossed as hills lying at the foot of steep cliffs

## River, delta, mouth
- Kalaallisut Kuuk, river; Paa, mouth
- Inuktitut (Boas) Koukdjuaq, the large river
- Netsilik Akule, the delta bay, the bay into which many rivers empty
- Inuktun (Rasmussen) Kuggup Panga, the river mouth

## Uneven and broken ground, fissures
- Kalaallisut Maniitsoq, the uneven
- Inuktitut (Boas) Manituling, with uneven places
- Netsilik Kitingujait, a fissure with steep rocky sides with a stream running through it;
  kitigkat, the ravines

## Thaw, lake, pond
- Kalaallisut Aakkat, the thawing, the deliquescent; Tasilik, place with lake
- Inuktitut (Boothia, Ross) Tessek, lake
- Inuktitut (Boas) Tessiujang, similar to a pond

## NOT recorded in any of these sources
No word is recorded in the vocabularies on disk for: permafrost or perennially frozen ground;
a plateau or tableland; scree or talus; a hummock or tussock; polygon ground; a landslide,
mudflow or slump; a dry lake bed; the first bare ground of spring. Parry's "Frozen, it is
(as meat)" could not be reliably isolated from the OCR column drift and is not reported.
These gaps should be tested against the Asuilaak living dictionary, the Nunavut Arctic College
elders' volumes and the Oqaasileriffik resources when the reuse terms are settled.
