# Extracts on the land surface, wave 1

## Hattersley-Smith and Long 1967, Postglacial Uplift at Tanquary Fiord (Arctic 20: 255-260)
Macdonald River delta, head of Tanquary Fiord. Three main delta terraces at 11-19 m (1),
28-41 m (2), 77-86 m (3); a kame terrace to about 100 m with kettle topography and a kettle lake.
Marine beaches across the forward slope of the terrace-3 outlier up to 50 m; lower beaches on
terrace 2 at 20-25 m with strandlines and ice-pushed gravel ridges. Highest recognised marine
level about 60 m. Mean tidal range 30 cm, so no tidal correction. Uplift about 3.5 m/100 yr from
6500 to 5000 BP, then about 25 cm/100 yr. 80 per cent of recovery complete by 5000 BP.
Radiocarbon table (field altitude; corrected age after a 500-yr shell correction):
- SI-468 peat, bank of Rollrock River, 72 m, 6480 +/- 200 BP
- GSC-373 Hiatella arctica, Macdonald River delta, 55-58 m, 6820 +/- 140 (corr. 6320)
- I-1125 Hiatella arctica, Flora Island, 24-27 m, 6285 +/- 250 (corr. 5785)
- I-1126 Hiatella arctica, near Macdonald River delta, 22-33 m, 6340 +/- 200 (corr. 5840)
- GSC-145 Hiatella arctica and Mya truncata, near delta, 12-14 m, 5700 +/- 140 (corr. 5200)
- SI-393 Echinoidea, in grey marine silts 55 cm below surface, 9 m, 4220 +/- 100 (corr. 3720)
- SI-407 random shells, grey marine silts 55 cm below surface, 9 m, 4360 +/- 500 (corr. 3860)
Comparisons cited: Axel Heiberg shells at the 60 m level 7000 +/- 100 BP (L647E, Muller 1966);
M'Clintock Inlet raised beach at 38 m, 7200 +/- 250 BP (L248B, Christie 1967).
Archaeological sites at the 23 m level near Tanquary Camp thought about 4000 years old
(Independence I microblades, Knuth).

## Lyons and Mielke 1973, Holocene History of a Portion of Northernmost Ellesmere Island (Arctic 26: 314-323)
Ward Hunt Island, 83 N 74 W. Radiocarbon table:
- SI-718 Hiatella arctica, raised beach 38 m, north side of Ward Hunt Island, 7755 +/- 150 BP
  (cf. Crary 1960 L248A 7200 +/- 200)
- SI-720 Hiatella arctica, raised beach (?) 5 m, east side of Ward Hunt Island, 5950 +/- 155 BP
- SI-723 Hiatella arctica, beneath Camp Creek Ice Rise, till(?) uncovered by ablation, 5 m, 4775 +/- 120 BP
- SI-724 Hiatella arctica, south of Camp Creek Ice Rise, 10 m, 5735 +/- 110 BP
- SI-725 Hiatella arctica, Astarte, Vermetus, half a mile above the mouth of Camp Creek, 30 m, 7045 +/- 190 BP
- SI-721 Astarte, Vermetus, in basement ice 1 mile NNE of the NE tip of Ward Hunt Island, 6815 +/- 190 BP
- SI-638 shell in basement ice, SE edge of Ward Hunt Island, 3623 +/- 120 BP
- SI-719B organic material in sponge debris, SE edge, 3400 +/- 140 BP
- SI-722 Hiatella, Astarte, Vermetus, basement ice SE edge, 3390 +/- 130 BP
- SI-727 Hiatella arctica, Astarte, basement ice at the south edge of the ice shelf, 3680 +/- 100 BP
- GSC-1025 pelecypods collected by Hattersley-Smith, same locality, 4510 +/- 150 BP
Marine level now 40 m above sea level dated 7500 +/- 300 BP. Ward Hunt Ice Shelf bottom at 43 m.
Pleistocene ice estimated at least 600 m over the Ward Hunt area, 1800 m over Tanquary Fiord.

## England 1974, A Note on the Holocene History (Arctic 27: 154-157)
Argues the local Pleistocene/Holocene boundary on northern Ellesmere lies at 7500-8100 BP, not
10,000-13,000 BP. Oldest date on postglacial emergence on the north coast 8150 +/- 140 BP
(GSC-1534, in Blake 1972). Ward Hunt Island emergence 38 m a.s.l.; Tanquary Fiord head 76 m a.s.l.
Driftwood collected by Crary at 3 m a.s.l. in Disraeli Fiord dated 3000 +/- 200 BP.
Uplift curves from Archer Fiord/Lady Franklin Bay, on stratigraphically controlled shell deposits
in delta foreset beds and stranded driftwood in raised beaches, are smoother than Lyons and
Mielke's or Hattersley-Smith and Long's.

## Thorsteinsson 1961, The History and Geology of Meighen Island (GSC Bulletin 75)
- p. 18: isolated segments of raised beaches to about 25 feet a.s.l. in the north-eastern and
  south-western coastal areas of Meighen Island; two molluscan shells collected from one of them;
  25 feet tentatively the approximate limit of marine emergence.
- p. 13: three excavations reached the frost table at 20, 23 and 26 inches. The coarse clastic
  Beaufort formation drains the active layer rapidly.
- p. 15: ice-wedge polygons near Andersen Point, fissures 4 inches to 2 feet wide and 6 inches to
  a foot deep, polygons four to six sided and about 50 feet across; excavation below one fissure
  found a vertical wedge of clear ice about 2 inches thick going to undetermined depth in the
  permafrost.
- p. 15: soil and rock stripes on the terraces east of Andersen Point, visible on high-altitude
  air photographs.
- p. 15: lingering snowdrifts have cut linear, irregular and crescent-shaped scarps as much as
  30 feet high, with solifluction terraces at their feet. Nivation and solifluction are named as
  two of the dominant levelling processes at high latitudes.
- p. 13-14: master streams reach the sea through braided channels, building deltas and offshore
  shoals.

## Savile 1964, General Ecology and Vascular Plants of the Hazen Camp Area (Arctic 17: 237-258)
- p. 245: run-off from retreating permafrost in the talus slopes increased in July until most of
  the springy slopes were flooded.
- p. 245: at the bases of slopes, where snow drifts into the cracks, ice-wedging makes hummocks
  occasionally 60 cm high.
- p. 248: Dryas integrifolia in nearly pure stands forms hummocks 10 cm or more high.
- p. 240: saline clay flats with a superficial crust of salts, baked and cracked into polygons;
  at site N7 Puccinellia angustata grew from the cracks and P. poacea from the polygon centres;
  true desert, like the badlands of North Dakota.
- p. 238: the Silurian rocks of the range shatter by frost action into boulders or slabs.

## Bentham 1941, Structure and Glaciology of South-East Ellesmere Island (Geographical Journal)
- p. 43: raised beaches at about 60 feet in Starnes Fiord (62 ft), opposite the Stewart Islands
  (62 ft) and in Makinson Inlet (58 ft); others at about 180 feet in Starnes Fiord (176 ft) and
  Makinson Inlet (184 ft).
- p. 43: marine-cut benches at 334 and 458 feet, and a third less obvious at 530 feet.
- p. 43: marine-cut benches at about 60 feet on King Edward VII Point and Smith Island.
- p. 43: the most extensive raised beaches on the south coast between Craig Harbour and Grise
  Fiord, the old cliffs now up to 2 miles from the coast.
- p. 39: plateau surfaces of frost-shattered material; a series of levels between 1720 and 2080 ft.
- p. 38: the coast has steep cliffs 800 to 1700 feet, scarred with gullies and extensive screes.

## Moore, Humphreys and Bentham 1936, Ellesmere Land and Grinnell Land (Geographical Journal 87)
- p. 430: a series of seven terraces at 40, 90, 125, 200, 290, 350 and 400 feet above sea level at
  the head of Foulke Fiord, Greenland.
- p. 429: the higher plateau near Foulke Fiord capped by dolerite so shattered by frost action
  that walking over its surface is extremely difficult.

## Hattersley-Smith, Crary and Christie 1955, Northern Ellesmere Island 1953 and 1954 (Arctic 8: 3-36)
- p. 26: raised beaches on Ward Hunt Island to at least 125 feet a.s.l. undisturbed by glacial
  action; radiocarbon 6500 to 7000 years.
- p. 26: marine deposits with bivalve shells intact at about 200 feet a.s.l. near the ice cliff
  (Christie).
- p. 26: highest marine beach so far found at 300 feet.
- p. 26: a glacier 15 miles south of Alert Point advanced across a marine terrace at 200 to 250 ft.
- p. 26: an erratic of microgranite from the mainland found on Ward Hunt Island at 1,100 feet.

## Dunbar and Greenaway 1956, Arctic Canada from the Air (figure captions and physiography)
- soil polygons at Limestone Island, Somerset Island.
- soil polygons and frost smoke over the shore lead, Prince Patrick Island (Fig. 481, 9 April 1951).
- a crater-like nivation hollow at the summit of a hill at the south end of Lougheed Island
  (Fig. 451, 4 August 1950).
- beach lines with lagoon lakes at Ell Bay, Southampton Island; well developed beach lines at
  Igloolik Island; old beaches showing through the snow on Mansel Island.
- the Great Plain of the Koukdjuak: a coastal strip of flat grassy marshland 10 to 30 miles wide
  and a slightly more elevated plain separated from it by a series of old beaches.
- Lake Hazen only 300-400 feet above sea level, the plateau about 3,000 feet.
- glossary: patterned ground, polygonal patterns formed by frost action in surface deposits,
  a few inches to several hundred feet across, edges either raised or depressed.
- a terraced river delta near Troll Fiord; braided channels and fan-shaped deltas on the
  Queen Elizabeth Islands.
