Male-otter; lake, Kaniapiskau river, Ung. ; translation of the Indian name, ‘ euchukamao.’

Manico; point, Southampton I., Kee. ; named by Lyon, 1824, after Lieut. P. Manico, of II.M.S. Griper.

Mansel; island, Hudson bay, Ung.; after Vice-Admiral Sir Robert Mansel (1573-1653); Treasurer of the Navy; named by Button, 1613; Button married Mansel’s niece.

Marjorie; lake, Dubawnt river, Kee. (See Lady Marjorie.)

Meadowbank; mountain, Backs river, Kee.; named by Back, 1834, ‘in honour of the learned Lord of that name ’ — Alexander Maconocliie-Welwood, Lord Meadowbank (1777-1861), Scottish judge. r Mecham; cape, Prince Patrick I., Frank.; after Lieut. Medicine-stone ; lakes, Berens river, southern Kee. ; from a large boulder stand- ing alone, which is an object of wonder to the Indians, and offerings of tobacco, pipes and other valuables have been made at its base for many years. Megik; -lake, Churchill river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘otter.’

Mercy; cape, Baffin I., Frank.; named by Davis on his first voyage, ‘Cape of God’s Mercy, as being the place of our first entrance for the discovery.’ Merry; cape, Churchill river, Kee.; after Capt. John Merry, Deputy Governor, Hudson’s Bay Company, 1712-29.

Merry ; island, Hudson bay, Kee.

Merry; headland, Chesterfield inlet, Hudson bay, Kee.

Michikamau; lake, Ung. (See Mishikamau.)

Miles; lake, Boothia gulf, Kee.; named by Rae, after a friend. In 1832 Simpson wrote : ‘Chief Trader Miles is accountant and winter chief of the [York] Factory. He is a good-hearted Englishman, very able at the desk, but eternally grumbling; and his judgment is no deeper than his ink- stand.’

Miller; island, Nastapoka group, Hudson bay, Ung. ; after an officer of the Hudson’s Bay Company.

Minto; lake, east of Hudson bay, Ung. ; after Sir Gilbert John (Elliot-Murray- Kynynmound) 4th Earl of Minto, Governor General of Canada, 1898-1904; b. 1847 ; styled Lord Melgund till sue. to title, 1891.

Mishikamats ; lake, Northwest river, Ung.; or Little Mishikamau, Indian for ‘ little great lake.’

Mishikamau; lake, Northwest river, Ung.; Indian name, meaning ‘great lake.’

Missi ; fall, Churchill river, Kee. ; from the Indian 1 missipowatik,’ meaning ‘ big fall ’ or ‘ big rapid.’

Mistassin; lake, Winisk river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘big stones,’ refer- ring to large rocks on its shores.

Montagu; cape, Wager inlet. Kee.; after John (Montagu), 2nd Duke of Montagu (1689-1749) ; Master General of the Ordnance, 1742-49. r Montreal ; island, Arctic coast, Kee. ; named by Back, 1834, ‘ in commemora- tion of the attention we had received from the public-spirited and hospitable inhabitants of that city ’ — Montreal, Canada.

Montresor; river, tributary to Backs river, Kee.; named by Back, 1834, ‘after Lieutenant-General Sir Thomas Montresor.’

Mourning; point, James bay, Kee.; from the burying of one of Capt. James’ men here.

Mowatt; island, Nastapoka group, Hudson bay, Ung. ; after an officer of the Hudson’s Bay Company.

Muddy; lake, Saskatchewan river, Kee. ; after the low mud flats along its banks.

Mukalik; river, Ungava bay, Ung. ; Indian name, meaning ‘muddy.’ (Wallace.)

Mukoman; river and rapid, Churchill river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘ knife.’

Munk; cove, mouth of Churchill river, Kee.; after Jens Munk, commander of a Danish expedition in search of the Korth West passage; wintered here, 1619-20.

Murchison; promontory, Boothia pena., Frank.; named by M’Clintock, 1859. '-Murchison; river, Arctic coast, Kee.

Nappartok; bay, Hudson bay, Ung. ; Eskimo name for ‘wood.’

Naskaupi ; river, tributary of the Northwest river, Ung. ; in the Montagnais dialect signifies ‘ the ignorant ones.’

Natuakami; lake, Stillwater river, Ung.; Indian name, meaning ‘still-water.’

Nelson; port and river, Hudson bay, Kee.; named by Button, 1612. ‘North- West Foxe,’ says: ‘Which river he named Port Nelson, after the name of his [Sailing] Master,’ whom he buried there.

Nepishkow; rapid, Churchill river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘leaf.’

Neptune; head, Hudson strait, Ung.; after the whaling steamer, Neptune, chartered by the Dominion Government for Hudson Bay expeditions, 1884 and 1903-04.

Nicholson : island, east of Hudson bay, Ung. ; after A. Nicholson, an officer of the Hudson’s Bay Company.

Nipartolik; bay, Hudson bay, Ung.; Eskimo name for a ‘wood country.’

Nipishish: lake, Ung.; Indian name, meaning ‘little water.’ (Wallace.)

North; foreland, Loks Land, N.W.T.; formerly called ‘Queen Elizabeth Fore- land,’ after Queen Elizabeth of England, and so named by Frobisher on his first voyage, 1596; present name, from position on north side of Hudson strait.

Nueltin ; lake, Thlewiaza river, Kee. ; the Indian name, meaning ‘island.’

Nuskwesi; river, Churchill river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘grass.’

Nutarawit; lake and river, tributary of the Kazan, Kee.; Indian name, mean- ing ‘ dead-child.’

Ogle ; point, Adelaide pena., Kee. (Back, 1834.)

O’Reilly; island, Arctic coast, long. 99°, Kee. ; probably after Lieut. Col. Walter Frederick O’Reilly, C.B., of the 41st Foot, who married Harriet, widow of the 5th Duke of Roxburghe (see Roxborough) ; named by Simp- son, 1839.

Paint ; hills, east coast of Hudson bay, Ung. ; the rocks composing these hills ‘ carry considerable pyrite, which, on the surface, is often rusty, and to this, the name of the locality is due.’

Pasquia ; hills and river, Kee. ; Richardson says that the hills are ‘ named by the Crees Wapus-keow-ivatchi [ wapus , strait ; heoiv, woods ; ivatchi, hill : the signification being “a pass through woods on a hill”], and by the Cana- dians, Basquiau.’ McIntyre says it signifies ‘narrow ridges or bluffs,’ prob- ably same as Richardson’s and referring to the gravel ridges — old beaches of the glacial lake Agassiz.

Passage; island, Southampton I., Kee.; named by Parry, 1821, after a passage for the ships found between it and Southampton island.

Payne; bay, lake and river, TJngava bay, Ung. ; after F. F. Payne, Secretary of the Meteorological Service, Toronto.

Pechell ; point, Arctic coast, Kee. ; named by Back, 1834, ‘after Sir J. B. Pechell, Bart.’— Rear Admiral Sir Samuel John Brooke-Pechell (1785- 1849) ; Lord of the Admiralty, 1830-34.

Peeshew-pukwagan ; lake, Kelson river, Kee. ; Indian name, meaning ‘wild- cat-fishing.’

Pekangikum; lake, Berens river, Kee.; Indian name, meaning ‘dirty water narrows.’

Pelly; bay, Arctic coast, Kee.; named by Rae, after Sir John Henry Pelly (1777-1852) ; Governor of Hudson’s Bay Company, 1822-52.

Pike ; river, Kee. ; after the abundance of these fish found in its waters. In 1740, was called ‘ pointe au Xord’ by a French trader.

Pistol; bay, Hudson bay, Kee. ; this bay was named by Capt. Smith, I747r ‘Douglas harbour,’ ‘after Mr. James Douglas — a merchant of the city of London and one of the adventurers in this undertaking’; derivation of present name unknown.

Princess Victoria; promontory, Arctic ocean, Kee. ; after Alexandrina-Victoria (1819-1901), only child of Edward, Duke of Kent, 4th son of George III.; later, Queen Victoria.

Queen Maud; sea, Frank, and Kee.; named by Amundsen, 1904, after Maud, Queen of Norway and third daughter of King Edward VII. ; m., 1896, Haakon VII., second son of Frederick VIII. of Denmark.

Rae; isthmus, Frank, and Kee.

Rae; strait, King William I. and Franklin isth., Frank, and Kee.

Ramage; island, gulf of Boothia, Kee.; named by Ross, 1829, possibly after Capt. Edward Ramage, R.N., who, in 1796, commanded the Rattlesnake (16) during an expedition against Ceylon.

Richardson; point, Adelaide pena., Kee. (Back. 1834.)

Ripon; island, Arctic ocean, Kee. ; named by Back, 1S34, ‘out of respect to the Earl of Ripon’ (1782-1859), English statesman.

Rocky islands; lake, Berens river, Kee.; translation of the Indian name kasapakamuk, from the isolated knolls situated near the shores.

Roes Welcome; strait, west of Southampton I., Kee. ; Foxe named an island in the strait after Sir Thomas Roe (1580-1644), diplomatist, who greatly assisted Foxe in his voyage of discovery. The name has since been trans- ferred to the strait.

Roggan; river, called ‘Bishop (or Pishop) Roggan,’ Hudson bay, Ung. ; Bishop Roggan is a corruption of the Indian name pichipouian , meaning ‘ fishing weir’; from the immense willow weirs, with basket sluices, built across the stream by the Indians to catch fish descending the river.

Romney; islands, Hudson bay, Ung.; named by Hudson, 1610, after Rebecca, Lady Romney, one of his patrons.

Roosevelt; hill, Kee. ; after Col. Theodore Roosevelt, President of the United States, 1901-08; named by Munro-Ferguson.

Roosevelt; lake, Thebayazie river, Thlewiaza river, Kee.
