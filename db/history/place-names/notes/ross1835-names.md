## Notes on Attribution and Unknown Names

- **Ross family members commemorated**: Andrew Ross (son), possibly others in family-named features
- **Booth family members**: Sir Felix Booth (founder), his sister (Brown Island), amiable sister (multiple islands/bays)
- **Officials and officers**: Beaufort (hydrographer), Parry (Arctic explorer), Franklin (Arctic explorer), Richardson (naturalist), Young (MP), Sabine
- **Expedition personnel**: Fearnall (builder), Tennent (expedition member), Emerson (associated name), Bannerman (MP)
- **Undetermined names**: Several islands and features mentioned "in the charts and tables" without full explanation in the narrative (line 9441)
- **Post-voyage naming**: Victoria Harbour was named after-the-fact by permission of royal personages, not by Ross during expedition

## Key Patterns

1. **Patron commemoration**: Most major features (Boothia, Cape Felix, Adelaide Bay) named after voyage patron Sir Felix Booth or his family
2. **Explorer recognition**: Arctic explorers honored (Parry, Franklin, Richardson)
3. **Personal relationships**: Friends, colleagues, and expedition members at Woolwich and other institutions
4. **Feminine names**: Several islands and features named after women (Elizabeth, Louisa, Grace, Matty, Brown for sister)
5. **MPs and officials**: Political figures honored (Young for Tynemouth, Bannerman for Aberdeen)
6. **Inuit geographic knowledge**: Ross records Inuit names primarily for major waterways, lakes, and the central gathering place Neitchillee
