## Key Findings

**Hogarth's Sound discovery:** In 1840, Captain William Penny sailed into an unknown inlet and confirmed it was neither Cumberland Strait nor any known entrance. He named it "Hogarth's Sound" in compliment to William Hogarth, Esquire, of Aberdeen, in gratitude for Hogarth's kindness to Eenoolooapik (p. 2801-2805).

**Tenudiackbeek etymology:** The Inuktitut place name Tenudiackbeek contains the root "ackbusk/ackbeek" meaning "whale," but McDonald was unable to trace the etymology of the "Tenu-" prefix further despite inquiries (p. 495-496).

**Whaling stations:** Low documents three active stations in Cumberland Gulf (Blacklead, Kekerten, Cape Haven) and one at the mouth of Hudson Bay (Fullerton), all dependent on Indigenous Inuit labour and serving scattered Inuit communities totaling ~500 people by 1903.
