# HMS Enterprise (Capt. Richard Collinson), 1850–1855 — dated positions and events

Source: [Thomas Bernard Collinson, ed.], *Journal of H.M.S. Enterprise, on the
Expedition in Search of Sir John Franklin's Ships by Behring Strait, 1850–55*
(London, 1889), from the OCR text at
`/data/dev/arctic-history/db/history/mcclure-investigator/files/mcclure-investigator-collinson1889.txt`.

Page numbers below are the **printed book page numbers**, recovered by
matching standalone digit-only lines in the OCR (running-header page numbers)
against the surrounding text; they should be correct or within a page or two.
The OCR is a double-spaced Google-type scan; word-spacing is frequently wrong
("mid-word" breaks), some digits/letters are garbled (long-s, "0"/"O", "8"/"3"
etc.), and in a few places whole runs of words are unrecoverable (marked
below). I did not invent any date, name, page number, or coordinate not
present in the OCR.

Coordinate convention: decimal degrees, **west longitude is negative**.

## 2. Chronology

1. **1850-01-13** — Passes the Downs under tow, outward bound from Greenhithe. (p. 38)
2. **1850-01-16** — Anchors in Plymouth Sound to take on preserved meats delayed from Dublin. (p. 38)
3. **1850-01-20** — Finally clears Plymouth; start of the true outward voyage. (p. 38)
4. **1850-08-11/12** — Passes through Bering Strait; sights East Cape, the Diomede Islands, and Cape Prince of Wales. (p. 58–59)
5. **1850-08-12** — Anchors at Port Clarence 11 p.m.; meets Capt. Moore of HMS *Plover*, decides to take over her station for the season. (p. 69)
6. **1850-11-14** — Sails from Sitka (Russian America) for the Sandwich Islands and Hong Kong, having failed to push further north/west that season. (p. 98)
7. **1851-02-15** — Anchors in Hong Kong at 9.15 a.m., ending a 35,225-mile passage since Plymouth (343 days at sea, 48 at anchor) — winters over. (p. 105–106)
8. **1851-02-15** (murder, reported here) — Collinson learns from Capt. Moore that Lieut. John J. Barnard, detached to explore toward the Yukon, had been killed by Indians at Nulato/Durabbin on 15 Feb. 1851 (attacked; died of his wounds a few days later). Barnard was the only officer aboard with prior Arctic service (with Sir James Ross, 1848), a fact the editor later connects to the loss of an experienced hand before the ship "entered the ice." (p. 128–129; Note 28, p. 402–403)
9. **1851-04-02** — Leaves Hong Kong for the north, parting from Commander Bate (*Cleopatra*). (p. 111)
10. **1851-07-23/24** — First substantial meeting with Iñupiat near Refuge Inlet/Point Barrow; natives give local place names (Noo-na-hoo, Too-na-mut, Oo-fe-la) and draw a chart of the coast. (p. 139)
11. **1851-08-27** — Enters Prince of Wales Strait; lands, shoots a deer, and finds signs of habitation (chips, seine, stone circles). (p. 152–153)
12. **1851-08-29** — Lands on the Princess Royal Islands; finds a beacon with two copper cylinders — a Union Jack and a notice from Capt. McClure (dated 1 June) recording that the *Investigator* had wintered safely 4 miles N.E. of the islands and naming Prince of Wales Strait, Princess Royal Islands, Prince Albert's Land and Baring/Banks Land. A second beacon on Prince Albert's Land, examined the same evening, holds an April notice from one of the *Investigator*'s spring travelling parties. (p. 154)
13. **1851-08-31** — Reaches "Furthest North" of the season working up the strait: lat. 73°30'N./long. 114°35'W. (and 73°25'N./114°14'W. to the eastward); blocked by ice, cannot see Melville Island. (p. 154–155)
14. **1851-09-15** — Decides on winter quarters; the second whaler is sent to survey the site that becomes Walker Bay. (p. 164)
15. **1851-09-21/23** — First meetings with the "Winter Island or central tribe" (Copper Inuit/Inuvialuit) near Walker Bay — natives visit the ship, appear afraid of open water, seen to have little iron. (p. 165–166)
16. **1851-10/11 (late Oct.–1 Nov.)** — Four natives from a party camped on Isthmus Lake come aboard; an old, infirm leading man gives his name as **Ip-pee-ra** and chooses a knife over a boarding pike; his two sons-in-law and his wife are also named as present (not by name). Party totals 25 individuals. (p. 172)
17. **1851-11 – 1852-04** — Winters at Walker Bay: balloon message launches, magnetic/tidal observations, sledge and survey work (Minto Inlet/South Bay), aurora observed. (p. 164–219, Section IX–X)
18. **1852-05-24 (dated notice)/06-01** — Sledge party to the north reaches the Investigator's depot at the Princess Royal Islands a second time; finds McClure's "last notice" there dated 1 June [1851], confirming the ice timeline. (p. 200)
19. **1852-08-05** — Leaves Walker Bay for the season's exploring. (p. 221, 234)
20. **1852-08-08/14** — Lies in Fish Bay, ice pressing in three times. (p. 224)
21. **1852-08-27** — Driven back by ice into Prince of Wales Strait, off Bear Island, "our bay with more ice in it than when we had left it 22 days previous." (p. 228)
22. **1852-09-19/22** — Works south and east through Dolphin and Union Strait and Coronation Gulf, past Cape Bexley, Sutton and Liston Islands (Esquimaux grave found), Douglas Island. (p. 234–237)
23. **1852-09-26/10-03** — Fights into Cambridge Bay through fog and ice, grounds briefly (28th–29th), settles for the winter by 3 October; first native contact of the wintering the same day. (p. 240–244)
24. **1852-10-10/15** — Natives visit repeatedly (9 on the 10th, 25 on the 15th); described as well-behaved, no thefts despite temptingly exposed stores. (p. 244)
25. **1853-04-10 to 05-03** — Magnetic observatory kept by ice-mate Mr. Arbuthnot; editor's Note 28 ("Personal Troubles") is keyed to this point in the narrative, recording — only in general terms, with **no names or exact dates given** — that "by the end of the spring of 1853... there were some of the officers suspended from duty." The journal itself, in Collinson's own hand, gives no hint of any such trouble. (p. 256, 402–403)
26. **1853-05-08** — Sledge party finds Dr. John Rae's cached record (dated 13 Aug. 1851) reporting his own search of the Victoria Strait coast — Collinson realises the ground has already been examined. (p. 267–268)
27. **1853-05-10** — "Furthest North" of the spring sledge journey: a cairn built on an island in Victoria Strait at lat. 70°26'N./long. 100°47'W., no land visible beyond; the party (heading toward Gateshead Island/King William Island) turns back for lack of provisions and because of ice too rugged for their strength — narrowly missing the Franklin relics later found by McClintock. (p. 268–269, and prologue p. 6–7)
28. **1853-07-06 (approx.)** — At the Finlayson Islands, a crew member picks up at high-water mark a worked piece of wood — two pieces of fir nailed together, with a copper door-hasp bearing the Queen's broad-arrow mark — "almost the only article we have met with which could have belonged to the missing ships." Collinson describes it in detail and speculates it may be part of a companion-hatch or observatory lintel, possibly linked to Dr. Rae's party. (p. 277–279)
29. **1853-09-14** — Fights the ship into Camden Bay through a narrow lead, nearly grounding off Flaxman Island. (p. 300–301)
30. **1853-09-26** — Young ice closes over the ship's cut "dock"; recognises she is frozen in for a third winter, at Camden Bay. (p. 302–303)
31. **1854-07-01/02** — Large party (41) of natives visits by two oomiaks; a boy is found wearing buttons engraved with a record of the search squadrons' movements 1850–52; a printed notice from HMS *Plover* (dated 4 July 1853) is also produced, giving the first news in a year of the *Investigator*'s presumed situation. Natives later make off with parts of the dingy and pull down an onshore hut. (p. 315–316)
32. **1854-07-15/20** — Ship works free of the ice at Camden Bay, in company with visits from "our Barter Island friends." (p. 319–320)
33. **1854-08-28** — Meets HMS *Plover* under Capt. Maguire off Point Barrow — first mail and outside news in three years and one month. (p. 331)
34. **1854-08-12** (n.b. — this event, in the ship's own log sequence, actually falls before the Point Barrow contact above; retained here in the order printed) — "Four sail were seen, it being three years and one month since the last [sail] had been reported"; speaks the whaler *John* of "Hew Bedford" [sic; = New Bedford] and learns (vaguely) that the *Investigator* had been reported to the eastward. (p. 327)
35. **1855-02-05/07** — Rounds the Cape of Good Hope; anchors Table Bay 7 February; learns by passing steamer of Dr. Rae's discovery of Franklin relics. (p. 339–340)
36. **1855-05-05** — "It was not until the 5th of May that we got a sight of Old England" — closing line of the journal proper. (p. 341–342)
37. **(Editorial, undated in the journal)** — Ship reached Sheerness in 1855; a scrawled Bible-verse reference left by Commander Bate on a beam of Collinson's cabin at Hong Kong (1851) was reportedly still there on arrival. (p. 111, note)

## 3. Verbatim quotable passages

All transcribed as printed, with only obvious OCR word-spacing artefacts
closed up; no wording changed except one bracketed [sic] flag below.

1. **Finding McClure's record, Princess Royal Islands, 29 Aug. 1851, p. 154:**
   > "nailed to which were two copper cylinders, one of which contained a Union Jack, and the other a notice from Captain M'Clure, by which I learnt that the Investigator had wintered in safety in the pack, 4 miles N.E. of these islands; that a party from her had reached the north end of the strait in the month of October."

2. **Entering Prince of Wales Strait, 28 Aug. 1851, p. 153:**
   > "Then crossing over to the western shore, we ascertained that the gulf or strait was 25 miles wide, and had 90 fathoms in mid-channel; therefore worth exploring."

3. **Meeting Ip-pee-ra near Walker Bay, [late Oct.] 1851, p. 172:**
   > "His name was Ip-pee-ra, and he appeared to be the leading man of this portion of the tribe. Two of the party with him were his sons-in-law, and the fourth was his wife; far from a handsome specimen of her sex, but uncommonly desirous of picking up the wee things about the decks."

4. **The relic at the Finlayson Islands, near Cambridge Bay, [c. 6] July 1853, p. 278:**
   > "Another of the crew picked up at high-water mark, on the east side of the island, a piece of wood, which being almost the only article we have met with which could have belonged to the missing ships, demands a description."

5. **Description of the relic (continued), p. 278:**
   > "13 inches from one end is a copper hasp for securing the latch of a door, fastened on by three screws, and with the Queen's mark on it... I cannot think that it formed part of any fittings to a boat, but is either part of a companion hatch or the lintel post to an observatory."

6. **Furthest North, Victoria Strait sledge journey, 10 May 1853, p. 268:**
   > "it became evident that we were upon an island, with no land in sight, except in the direction we had come from... so building a cairn, which our observations eventually placed in lat. 70°26'N. and long. 100°47'W., and depositing information, we retraced our steps to the south-eastward."

7. **Dr. Rae's cached record, found 8 May 1853, p. 268:**
   > "As far as regards the object of the expedition, a search for Sir John Franklin and party, we were quite unsuccessful. John Rae, Chief Factor, H.B. Co., Commanding the Expedition. Lat. 70°2'30" N., long. 101°18' W. 13th August, 1851."

8. **Re-entering "civilised society," Bering Sea, 12 Aug. 1854, p. 327:**
   > "On the 12th of August four sail were seen, it being three years and one month since the last seen had been reported to me; and at noon we again entered civilised society, by communicating with the John of Hew Bedford [sic]."

9. **Coming home, 5 May 1855, p. 341–342 — closing line of the journal:**
   > "The wind continued strong, and the ship, in consequence of the rough state of the copper from collision with the ice, made but little progress to windward, so that it was not until the 5th of May that we got a sight of Old England."

## What I could not confidently establish

- **Officer quarrels / arrests / courts martial**: the source does **not**
  give dates, names, or particulars. Editor's Note 28 ("Personal Troubles in
  the Enterprise," p. 402–404) states only that "by the end of the spring of
  1853... there were some of the officers suspended from duty," and that "the
  one lieutenant who had served in the Arctic Regions before... was killed
  before they entered the ice" — this last almost certainly refers to Lieut.
  John J. Barnard (the only officer with prior Arctic service, per the
  crew-list on p. 29), but Barnard was killed by Indians near the Yukon in
  Feb. 1851 while detached on a separate overland mission, not in a shipboard
  quarrel. The note explicitly declines to give particulars ("It is
  unnecessary, and it would be wrong, to enter now into any particulars of
  the troubles that arose on this head"), and states that Collinson's own
  journal, as printed, "would [not] suppose that any difficulty of the kind
  had occurred throughout it." I found no court-martial record, named
  officer, or specific date for any arrest/suspension anywhere in the OCR
  text.
- **1850-08-03 water-bottle coordinate** (long. 183°28'W) is printed as-is
  but is geographically impossible (no such meridian exists); I flagged
  rather than silently corrected it, and my "corrected" version in the table
  above is a guess, not a source figure.
- **Finlayson Islands relic date**: no numeral date is printed for the actual
  moment of picking up the wood; I reconstructed "on or about 6 July 1853"
  from the day-of-week narrative ("intention...to have left on Monday...did
  not get away until Tuesday at midnight," following a dated Friday 1 July /
  Saturday 2 July sequence). Treat this date as inferred, not printed.
- Several latitude/longitude fixes in the text give latitude only or
  longitude only (marked "(partial)" in the table); I did not invent the
  missing coordinate.
- I did not exhaustively map every sledge-journal daily position (there are
  scores of them across the spring 1852 and spring 1853 sledge journals,
  pp. 180–220 and 258–276); the table above samples the ones directly needed
  for the itinerary the task specified.
