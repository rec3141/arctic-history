This page is a gazetteer rather than a story. It is ordered by place, running
outward from Beechey Island, because that is how a mariner reads a coast: not
by date but by what happened where you are now. Every point in it is on the
map with a position, a date and a source. Where another researcher on this
project has told the story properly, the entry here is a dot and a paragraph
and a link, and you should follow the link.

Three general things are worth saying first.

**Almost nobody drowned.** The characteristic Arctic loss is not a ship going
down with all hands in a gale. It is a ship being squeezed flat while her crew
stand on the ice beside her watching, which is what happened to the
[[artifact:wrecks-disasters-001|Breadalbane]] in fifteen minutes off Beechey
Island, and to nineteen whalers in Melville Bay in June 1830. Ice gives
warning: beams groan, bulkheads creak, and people walk off. What kills in this
region is what comes after the ship is gone — the winter, the distance and the
food.

**The ice takes ships and sometimes gives them back.** Kellett's
[[artifact:wrecks-disasters-023|Resolute]] was abandoned in Barrow Strait in
May 1854 under protest and was found, sixteen months and some twelve hundred
miles later, floating dry and sound off Cape Mercy. That single fact has made
every Arctic seaman since slow to write a beset ship off.

**The modern losses are navigational, not structural.** The last three entries
here are an ice-strengthened cruise ship on a charted shoal, a supply ship on
an uncharted reef, and a military transport that flew into a hill on a routine
approach. None of them was defeated by the ice.

## Beechey Island and Barrow Strait

- The [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-001|Breadalbane]], a chartered transport,
  crushed half a mile south of [[place:Beechey Island]] a little after four in
  the morning on 21 August 1853, gone in a quarter of an hour, all twenty-one
  hands saved by HMS Phoenix. Found by [[person:Joseph Beverley MacInnis]] on
  [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-002|13 August 1980]] in a hundred metres of
  water, upright, two masts standing: the best-preserved wooden wreck in any
  ocean. See [[page:the-breadalbane]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-027|Lieutenant Joseph Rene Bellot]], 18 August
  1853, carried away on a floe in [[place:Wellington Channel]] near
  [[place:Cape Bowden]] and lost through a crack in the ice while carrying
  despatches. He was twenty-seven, and French, and the whole search fleet
  mourned him.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-024|HMS Assistance and HMS Pioneer]], abandoned
  in Wellington Channel on 25 August 1854 at 75 degrees 31 minutes north.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-023|HMS Resolute and HMS Intrepid]], abandoned in
  Barrow Strait on 13 May 1854, twenty-eight miles from [[place:Cape Cockburn]].
  See [[page:the-ships-left-in-the-ice]].

## The western channels

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-003|HMS Investigator]], left in
  [[place:Mercy Bay]] on the north coast of Banks Island on 3 June 1853 after
  three winters, her stores landed in what became McClure's Cache. Inuvialuit
  families worked that cache for generations. The hull was found by Parks
  Canada in [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-004|July 2010]] in eight metres of
  water. The voyage is at [[topic:mcclure-investigator]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-012|Erebus and Terror]], deserted 22 April 1848
  off the northwest coast of King William Island; the wrecks found in 2014 and
  2016. See [[topic:franklin-1845]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-009|The Hanseatic]], aground in
  [[place:Simpson Strait]] on 29 August 1996 with 259 people aboard, on a
  charted shoal, in fine weather, because a buoy had moved over the winter.

## Prince Regent Inlet and the Gulf of Boothia

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-010|HMS Fury]], driven ashore and abandoned at
  [[place:Fury Beach]] on 25 August 1825. Her stores on that beach saved John
  Ross's people seven years later. See [[page:the-loss-of-the-fury]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-011|The Victory]], abandoned at Victoria Harbour
  on 29 May 1832 after four winters. See [[page:abandoning-the-victory]].

## Smith Sound and the Ellesmere shore

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-005|The Proteus]], crushed in the pack off
  [[place:Cape Sabine]] on 23 July 1883 with the relief stores still in her.
  Nineteen men died ashore the following winter as a direct result.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-015|Camp Clay]]: six alive out of twenty-five
  when the Thetis came in on 22 June 1884. See [[topic:nares-greely]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-013|The Polaris]], run ashore at
  [[place:Life Boat Cove]] on 16 October 1872, and
  [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-014|the nineteen people]] carried away on a floe
  the night before who drifted 1,800 miles and all lived. See
  [[page:the-polaris-and-the-floe-drift]].

## Melville Bay and Baffin Bay

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-017|The Baffin Fair]], 19 to 24 June 1830:
  nineteen ships destroyed and twelve damaged in one week, a loss of 142,600
  pounds, and not one man killed. See [[page:the-whalers-bad-years]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-019|1819]]: fourteen ships lost in the same bay;
  eleven in 1821, seven in 1822.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-020|The fleet frozen in]], 11 October 1835:
  eleven ships and six hundred men caught by an early winter in Davis Strait.
  The [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-021|William Torr]] left twenty-two sick men
  ashore in the care of Inuit families and all twenty-two died. HMS Cove was
  sent to find them and [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-022|found nobody]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-041|The Isabella]], wrecked off the
  [[place:Whalefish Islands]] on 18 May 1835, with the William Lee off Dark
  Head on 4 July and the Mary Francis crushed on 26 August: the Greenland side
  of the strait was no safer than the Middle Ice.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-030|HMS Resolute]] herself, sighted twenty miles
  off [[place:Cape Mercy]] on 10 September 1855 and sailed home to New London
  by a whaling master with a quadrant and a chart drawn on foolscap. The
  [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-038|Grenville Bay's drift]] and
  [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-037|HMS Cove's search]] are the two tracks on
  this topic's map.

## Beyond the bay: the two seventeenth-century disasters

Both are outside the area this gazetteer otherwise covers and both are here
because nothing later matched them.

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-043|Henry Hudson set adrift]], 22 June 1611: nine
  people put into a boat by their own crew in James Bay and never seen again.
  The only loss in this list caused by human beings rather than by ice. See
  [[page:hudsons-fate]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-042|Jens Munk's wintering]], 1619-20: sixty-one
  of sixty-four men dead of scurvy at the mouth of the Churchill, and three
  survivors who sailed the smaller ship home to Norway. See
  [[page:munk-at-the-churchill]].

## Foxe Basin and south Baffin

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-008|The Nascopie]], on an uncharted reef at the
  entrance to [[place:Kinngait]] harbour on 21 July 1947. Thirty-five years of
  the eastern Arctic supply line ended on that rock. See [[page:the-nascopie]].

## The high Arctic stations

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-006|The Lancaster crash at Alert]], 31 July 1950:
  nine dead, including the man who chose every site in the weather station
  network. See [[topic:science-stations]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-007|Boxtop 22]], 30 October 1991: a Hercules into
  a hillside sixteen kilometres south of [[place:CFS Alert]], five dead,
  thirteen alive after thirty hours in the dark. See [[page:alerts-two-air-disasters]].
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-031|Resolute, 7 December 1947]]: a polar bear in
  October and an electrocution in December in the station's first winter.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-032|Eureka, Christmas Day 1948]]: fire took the
  mess, the generator and both vehicles, and the relief flight took seven weeks.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-033|Isachsen and Resolute, spring 1949]]: two
  aircraft written off on thawing mud strips, no one hurt, both fuselages
  turned into warehouses.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-034|Mould Bay, April 1948]]: a bulldozer through
  the cockpit wall, a tent burned down, and the executive officer invalided out.

## The DEW Line and the eastern Baffin airfields

The radar line built across the Arctic from 1955 put airstrips where there had
been nothing, and the aircraft that supplied them are the commonest twentieth
century loss in this region after the sea.

- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-047|A USAF C-47 down on the sea ice]] five miles
  off [[place:Resolution Island]] on 18 January 1961; all twelve aboard lifted
  off by an RCMP ski Otter in two overloaded flights while the ice broke up
  under the aircraft.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-045|Nordair's C-46]], into snow-covered ground
  4,500 feet short of the runway at [[place:Cape Dyer]] on 15 May 1967 in
  whiteout. All four aboard killed.
- [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-048|A Twin Otter at Frobisher Bay]], rolled into
  the glide slope antenna from forty feet on 23 August 1978, one crewman killed;
  and [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-046|a second Twin Otter of the same operator]]
  into rising ground a third of a mile east of the same runway on 29 August
  1979, on a Special VFR approach under a 400-foot ceiling, with all nine
  aboard killed.

## Why this matters here

For the people of [[place:Qausuittuq]], [[place:Ausuittuq]],
[[place:Mittimatalik]] and [[place:Kinngait]] this is not a list of curiosities.
McClure's Cache in Mercy Bay was a hardware store for Inuvialuit families for
half a century. The men of the William Torr died in Inuit camps, being nursed.
The [[artifact:wrecks-disasters-008|Nascopie's]] loss changed how every post in
the eastern Arctic was supplied. And for anyone working from a ship today the
gazetteer has a simple use: it says where ships have actually been lost, and
what conditions were doing when it happened.

The parties who survived what should have killed them are gathered at
[[page:the-parties-that-should-not-have-lived]].
