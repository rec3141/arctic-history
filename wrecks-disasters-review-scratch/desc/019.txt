Fourteen ships were lost outright in the ice of Melville Bay in 1819, eleven in 1821 and seven in 1822. Robert Goodsir, who went through the bay himself in 1849, wrote that since 1819, when the trade began pushing this far north, not a year had passed without ships being damaged between latitudes 74 and 76. The bay is the pinch point of the Baffin Bay circuit: to reach the North Water and the west-side grounds a ship had to be warped and tracked through the lane between the land floe of the Greenland shore and the moving pack, and if the pack came in against the fast ice there was nowhere to go. Clements Markham put it that until steam came in, scarcely a season passed without the destruction of many vessels there. No positions are recorded for the individual 1819 losses; the point is pinned at the middle of the bay. See [[topic:whaling]].
