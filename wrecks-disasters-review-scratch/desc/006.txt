Gazetteer entry. An RCAF Lancaster, up from Thule where it had been flying ice reconnaissance, was dropping spark plugs and other supplies to the twelve men building the weather station at Alert when its parachute fouled the aircraft's elevator; the Lancaster dived into the ground about 450 metres west of the station and exploded. Nine died: Charles J. Hubbard of the United States Weather Bureau, who had chosen the sites for the whole Joint Arctic Weather Stations network, the Canadian ice observer Dr D.W. Kirk, and seven RCAF crew. Hubbard's family asked that he be buried where he died, and the graves at the north end of the airstrip, looking over Cape Belknap and the Arctic Ocean, are the northernmost cemetery in the world.
