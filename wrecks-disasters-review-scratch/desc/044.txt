No single source gives a complete list of the ships lost in Melville Bay in June 1830, and the three that name ships do not agree on the total. Named by Robert Goodsir, quoting the Edinburgh Cabinet Library: Baffin, Achilles, Ville de Dieppe and Rattler, all four broken up; Progress of Hull, crushed to atoms by an iceberg; Oenhope of Hull, a total wreck; with the Eliza Swan and the St Andrew damaged but surviving, and the surgeon of the North Britain watching the ice meet in his own cabin before he could get out. Named additionally by Clements Markham: Princess of Wales and Letitia, cut in two lengthwise; Resolution of Peterhead, her counter pierced, sank; Laurel and Hope, squeezed flat and thrown on their broadsides; Commerce, a brig, lifted bodily onto the ice with a twisted stern post and sunk as soon as the pack loosened. Totals: Goodsir nineteen lost and twelve seriously damaged, 142,600 pounds; David Lindsay nineteen lost, 140,000 pounds, and the date 19 June; Markham twenty lost, with no date and no figure. Goodsir places the besetment at 75 degrees 10 minutes north, 60 degrees 30 minutes west, about forty miles south of Cape York, beginning on 19 June, with the crushing from the evening of the 24th. Lloyd's Register and the Hull and Peterhead port records would settle the count; none of the three published accounts does.
