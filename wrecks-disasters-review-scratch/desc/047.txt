A survival rather than a disaster, and one whose details the sources tell differently. A United States Air Force C-47 flying from Saglek to Frobisher Bay with twelve people aboard - Colonel Victor Milner, commander of the Goose Air Defense Sector, at the controls; Air Commodore Doug Bradshaw of the RCAF; seven more American service personnel; and the USO trio Betty Amos, her sister Jean Amos and Judy Lee Schreiber - lost an engine on 18 January 1961 and belly-landed on the sea ice near Resolution Island, at the eastern mouth of Hudson Strait, in the January dark. The landing gear collapsed and the wing and tail were damaged against a wall of ice, and the crew got everybody off because the ice was cracking under the weight of the aircraft. A ski-equipped single Otter of the RCMP came over, landed alongside, and lifted all twelve out in one grossly overloaded flight, taxiing in circles to get airspeed, striking a lump of ice hard enough to tear off a ski, and landing on the station's short strip on the remaining one. Nobody was hurt. Who flew that Otter is not agreed: the RCMP Veterans' Association names its own pilot, Staff Sergeant Robert Lorne Fletcher of the Frobisher Bay air detachment; the United States Air Force historical report for the quarter and Judy Lee Schreiber's own account both name Ken Dempster, with Milner beside him in the right-hand seat. The USAF report puts the landing about five miles from the station, Schreiber about ten. The point is the station's, not the aircraft's, and the source does not say outright that the C-47 was written off.
