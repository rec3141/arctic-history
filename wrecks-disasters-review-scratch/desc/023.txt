Henry Kellett's two ships wintered in 1852-53 at Dealy Island off Melville Island, in latitude 74 degrees 56 minutes 25 seconds north, longitude 108 degrees 48 minutes 33 seconds west. Working east in September 1853 they were caught in young ice five inches thick at eight in the evening of 10 September and spent the second winter beset in Barrow Strait at 74 degrees 41 minutes north, 101 degrees 22 minutes west, with Cape Cockburn bearing north 34 east, twenty-eight miles - the position Kellett gave at the head of his orders. On 28 April 1854 Francis Leopold McClintock came back from Beechey with Sir Edward Belcher's decided orders to abandon; Kellett, who thought both ships would swim out, obeyed under protest. Preparations ran through 12 and 13 May, and on the last day, Monday 15 May 1854, the pilot jack was hoisted at the foretopmast head and the red ensign and pendant displayed, so that if she went down she would go down with her colours flying. At a quarter past six in the evening Kellett went through the lower deck for the last time, drank a glass of wine to the old Resolute, and the main hatchway was secured; the four sledges, forty-two officers and men, marched for Cape Cockburn at seven and reached the North Star at Beechey Island at seven in the evening of 18 June. George McDougall's own preface and his plate both date the abandonment to 15 May; his journal entry for Saturday the 13th, which describes the preparations, has sometimes been read as the day itself.
