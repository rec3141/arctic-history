CC-130 Hercules 130322 of 435 Transport Squadron, Edmonton, flying flight 22 of Operation Boxtop, the twice-yearly resupply of CFS Alert, came up from Thule with eighteen people and 3,400 litres of diesel aboard; many of the passengers were communications researchers going in to the station's signals work. On final approach at about 4.40 in the afternoon of 30 October 1991 the pilot flying lost sight of the runway, radar contact and radio went at the same moment, and the aircraft struck rock about sixteen kilometres from the station. Captain Judy Trepanier, Master Warrant Officer Tom Jardine, Warrant Officer Robert Grimsley and Master Corporal Roland Pitre died in the impact. The other fourteen waited more than thirty hours in the polar night, in high wind and temperatures of minus 20 to minus 30, twelve of them crowded into the broken tail section while Captain Wilma de Groot, a physician travelling as a passenger, called the roll every hour or two to keep them awake; Sue Hillier and Bob Thompson were too badly hurt to be moved and a shelter was built around them where they lay. Two overland parties turned back, and search and rescue technicians from 413 Squadron at Greenwood jumped in after about eight hours and lost most of their gear to the wind. Captain John Couch, the pilot, answered the roll for hours and then died of hypothermia. Thirteen came out alive. The plotted point is a nominal position sixteen kilometres south of the runway; the crash site has no published survey position.
