Edward Belcher's Assistance and her steam tender Pioneer wintered in 1852-53 in Northumberland Sound at about 76 degrees 52 minutes north, 97 west, and in 1853-54 in Wellington Channel at 75 degrees 31 minutes 14 seconds north, 92 degrees 21 minutes 55 seconds west, roughly fifty-two miles north of the North Star at Beechey Island; the ground beside them holds the graves of the men who died there that winter. At six on the morning of 25 August 1854 the crews assembled on the floe in travelling order, the hatchways already caulked down and the colours secured so that they might be deemed nailed to the mast, and walked away. Belcher's own sledge crew carried him to Cape Bowden and his gig took him on, and twenty-five hours after quitting the ship he was back in Northumberland House at Beechey Island; Commander George Henry Richards and the boat parties, having left a great deal of baggage on the floe, came in about twelve hours after him, with eighteen men who could not be carried walking round by land. At half past one on the afternoon of 27 August, after a marble tablet to Joseph Rene Bellot had been set up on the beach and a military salute fired over it, the whole party sailed: the companies of five abandoned ships in three hulls, the North Star, Inglefield's Phoenix and Jenkins's Talbot. Belcher was court-martialled and acquitted, but never employed at sea again. See [[topic:franklin-search]] and [[page:belchers-five-ships]].
