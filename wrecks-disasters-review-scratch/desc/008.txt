The Hudson's Bay Company's supply ship Nascopie, for thirty-five years the one annual link between the eastern Arctic posts and the south, was working into the harbour at Kinngait (Cape Dorset) under her master James Waters on 21 July 1947 when she struck an uncharted and steeply rising reef. She grounded hard and half flooded. Nobody was killed; Waters got the passengers, the crew and most of the cargo off, and she was left on the rock and declared a total loss. A storm broke her in two on 25 September and her stern went down on 15 October. The 1947 post records went with her. Her loss ended the Eastern Arctic Patrol as it had been run since 1933 and pushed the trade onto chartered tonnage and, before long, onto aircraft; the Crown's liability for the passengers' fares and the freight is the subject of a Department of Transport file at Library and Archives Canada.
