The worst week the British Arctic whale fishery ever had. The fleet was beset in Melville Bay in a gale that sprang up on 19 June 1830 at about 75 degrees 10 minutes north, 60 degrees 30 minutes west, some forty miles south of Cape York; the ships lay stem to stern under the lee of a large floe, and from the evening of 24 June the floes began to overlap and close on them. Nineteen ships were destroyed outright and twelve seriously damaged, a loss put at 142,600 pounds. Robert Goodsir, quoting the Edinburgh Cabinet Library, names the Baffin, Achilles, Ville de Dieppe and Rattler as broken up, the Progress of Hull crushed to atoms by an iceberg and the Oenhope a total wreck; Clements Markham, writing later, says twenty were lost and adds the Princess of Wales and Letitia cut in two, the Resolution of Peterhead pierced through the counter, the Laurel and the Hope squeezed flat and the brig Commerce sunk; David Lindsay gives nineteen and 140,000 pounds. Not one man was killed. About a thousand seamen camped on the ice among the wrecks, broached the spirits, lit bonfires of wreck timber and held what the fleet afterwards called the Baffin Fair.
