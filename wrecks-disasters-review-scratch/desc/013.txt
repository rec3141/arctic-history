Gazetteer entry. The night the Polaris was nipped in Smith Sound, 15 October 1872, nineteen people went onto a floe with stores and were carried away from the ship. The fourteen left aboard, under the sailing master Sidney O. Buddington with the expedition's surgeon Emil Bessels, found her leaking and nearly out of coal, warped her in and ran her ashore at Life Boat Cove the next day. They broke her up for a hut, wintered there with the help of the Inughuit of the district, built two boats out of what was left of her, and came out the following summer to be picked up off Cape York by the Dundee whaler Ravenscraig on 23 June 1873. When the Tigress came north in August 1873 to look for them she found the camp empty, the Inughuit living in it, and the Polaris herself sunk offshore. Charles Francis Hall, whose expedition it was, had died aboard her at Thank God Harbour eleven months before she was beached.
