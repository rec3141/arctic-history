The first of the 1835 losses, and a reminder that the Greenland side was no safer than the Middle Ice. The Isabella was wrecked off the Whalefish Islands in Disko Bay on 18 May 1835, before the season had properly begun, under Captain R. Carlill; her people were all saved. This is the same Isabella of Hull that, under Captain R. W. Humphreys, had picked up John Ross and James Clark Ross out of their boats in Lancaster Sound in August 1833 - A. G. E. Jones says so plainly, and it was Humphreys who then wrote to the newspapers pressing for a relief expedition when the fleet was beset that autumn. Two more Hull ships went the same season: the William Lee, lost abreast of Dark Head on 4 July, and the Mary Francis, crushed in the ice on 26 August. Their survivors were distributed among the ships of the fleet, which is why the Abram reached Hull in February 1836 with ninety-eight hands aboard and the Grenville Bay came into Peterhead with sixteen men belonging to three other ships.
